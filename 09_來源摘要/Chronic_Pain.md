# Source Summary: Chronic Pain

## Source Type

- Textbook chapter / rehabilitation and pain medicine chapter.
- Authors listed in source: Quynh Giao Pham, Jennifer Leet, and Davina S. Sassoon.
- Single-source ingest only.

## Reliability Level

- Tier 1 in this wiki because it is a textbook-style clinical chapter.
- Strongest for chronic pain classification, biopsychosocial framing, CBT-CP, pacing, sleep, multidisciplinary pain services, and opioid risk framing.
- Opioid recommendations cite CDC 2022 concepts but still require current policy and patient-specific review before clinical application.

## One-Sentence Summary

Chronic pain is a multidimensional disease state in which nociceptive, neuropathic, and nociplastic mechanisms interact with mood, sleep, cognition, behavior, social context, opioid risk, and function, making multidisciplinary nonopioid-centered care the default framework.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Nociceptive, neuropathic, and nociplastic pain

#### One-Sentence Definition

Chronic pain classification should distinguish nociceptive pain from tissue-nociceptor activation, neuropathic pain from somatosensory lesion / disease, and nociplastic pain from altered nociception without clear tissue damage or somatosensory lesion.

#### Known Facts

- Chronic pain is defined in the chapter as pain lasting at least three months and exceeding normal healing.
- Neuropathic pain is caused by lesion or disease of the somatosensory nervous system.
- Nociceptive pain reflects actual or threatened non-neural tissue damage with normally functioning somatosensory system.
- Nociplastic pain was adopted by IASP in 2017 and refers to altered nociception despite no clear tissue damage / nociceptor activation or somatosensory lesion.
- These mechanisms can overlap.

#### Inferences

- A single patient may require mixed-mechanism formulation rather than one label.
- Nociplastic pain does not mean imaginary pain; it means altered nociceptive processing.

### Concept: Pain physiology and chronification

#### Known Facts

- The chapter describes transduction, transmission, perception, and modulation.
- A-beta fibers carry tactile / proprioceptive input, A-delta fibers carry fast localized pain, and C fibers carry slow poorly localized pain.
- Gate control theory explains why touch, temperature, TENS, and SCS can modulate pain.
- Central sensitization involves repetitive C-fiber input, dorsal horn wind-up, NMDA mechanisms, protein kinases, descending facilitation, glia, prostaglandins, and nitric oxide.
- Peripheral sensitization involves nociceptor threshold lowering and spontaneous activity mediated by inflammatory mediators, TRPV1, sodium channels, and neurogenic inflammation.
- Chronification involves altered brain connectivity, cortical reorganization, maladaptive neuroplasticity, and gray / white matter changes.

#### Inferences

- Chronic pain severity may diverge from tissue imaging because the nervous system itself becomes part of the pathology.

### Concept: CBT-CP and time-based pacing

#### One-Sentence Definition

CBT-CP targets the interaction between thoughts, emotions, behavior, and pain interference through structured skills such as activity scheduling, time-based pacing, relaxation, sleep education, and cognitive coping.

#### Known Facts

- The chapter states CBT-CP improves function, mood, and pain interference.
- CBT-CP sessions can include increased physical activity, time-based pacing, relaxation, pleasant activity scheduling, sleep education, and cognitive coping.
- Time-based pacing aims to maintain enough activity without overpushing into pain flares.
- CBT-CP targets fear avoidance and catastrophizing.
- ACT and EAET are also discussed as behavioral approaches.

#### Inferences

- CBT-CP is not a claim that pain is imaginary; it is a treatment for modifiable pain-maintaining loops.

### Concept: Long-term opioid risk and buprenorphine option

#### One-Sentence Definition

Long-term opioid therapy for chronic noncancer pain is generally discouraged because benefits are uncertain and harms are substantial, while buprenorphine may be considered in selected chronic pain patients, especially when full agonists are risky or suboptimal.

#### Known Facts

- Long-term opioid harms include adrenal insufficiency, respiratory depression, sex hormone disruption, osteoporosis, bowel dysfunction, opioid-induced hyperalgesia, immunosuppression, misuse, dependence, and OUD.
- CDC 2022 concepts summarized in the chapter include preferring nonopioid care for subacute / chronic pain, using immediate-release opioids when initiating, lowest effective dose, follow-up within 1-4 weeks, PDMP, toxicology, naloxone risk mitigation, benzodiazepine caution, and evidence-based OUD medications.
- The chapter states abrupt discontinuation or rapid tapering should not be done unless there is life-threatening overdose concern.
- Buprenorphine is described as a partial agonist with ceiling effect and lower overdose risk.
- Belbuca and Butrans are FDA-approved for chronic pain; OUD formulations can be used off-label for pain in some contexts.

#### Inferences

- Opioid decisions must include function, mental health, substance-use risk, taper harm, and patient safety rather than only pain intensity.
- Buprenorphine is a risk-management option, not a universal analgesic upgrade.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Chronic pain assessment should include location, intensity, quality, temporal pattern, function, emotional functioning, and global improvement.
- QST and ENFD biopsy can help characterize neuropathic / small-fiber contributions in selected contexts.
- Brain imaging is not routine for chronic pain assessment.
- Treat sleep disorders, including OSA; CBT-I is first-line for chronic insomnia.
- Prolonged rest is not beneficial for chronic pain.
- Exercise and movement-based therapy can improve pain severity, function, and quality of life with few adverse events.
- Multidisciplinary pain center care is best suited for complex chronic pain with medical, psychosocial, and vocational needs.

## Research-Useful Points

- Pain bias and undertreatment in marginalized groups should be captured as a health-equity concept in future sources.
- Placebo / nocebo, pain catastrophizing, and cortical reorganization deserve separate concept pages if supported by additional sources.

## Conflicts / Cautions

- Evidence for long-term opioid benefit in chronic low back pain, osteoarthritis, and diabetic polyneuropathic pain is described as very low quality.
- OUD diagnosis in patients prescribed opioids for pain is complex because tolerance and withdrawal should not be counted when opioids are taken under medical supervision.
- Cannabis, CBD, alcohol, benzodiazepines, and other CNS depressants change opioid and buprenorphine risk assessment.

## One-Sentence Summary

Drug therapy can improve or undermine neurological rehabilitation through timing, adverse effects, interactions, pharmacokinetic variability, and functional effects on cognition, balance, tone, cardiovascular response, fatigue, and motor learning.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Medication effects in neurorehabilitation

#### One-Sentence Definition

Medication effects in neurorehabilitation are functional modifiers that can change arousal, movement, tone, orthostasis, cognition, balance, fatigue, pain, seizure risk, and therapy timing.

#### Known Facts

- Pharmacokinetics includes absorption, distribution, metabolism, and elimination.
- Pharmacodynamics concerns drug effects and mechanisms of action.
- Disease, age, diet, sex, genetics, drug interactions, and comorbidities can alter drug response.
- PD medication timing can affect mobility and "on/off" function.
- Antiseizure medications can cause drowsiness, ataxia, vertigo, and cognitive effects.
- Antihypertensives and cardiovascular drugs can alter exercise tolerance, orthostasis, dizziness, weakness, and electrolyte-related muscle function.
- Drugs can affect spasticity, muscle tone, neuroplasticity, balance, cognition, and therapy participation.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Drug regimen + patient physiology + disease state + timing
-> therapeutic effect and adverse effect profile
-> arousal, motor control, balance, BP, fatigue, cognition, tone, pain
-> therapy safety, participation, learning, and measured functional gains
-> medication-aware rehab scheduling and team communication
```

#### Inferences

- Therapy performance should be interpreted relative to medication timing and adverse effect burden.
- Rehab clinicians can contribute objective functional observations to prescribers without independently changing medications.

#### Assumptions

- Medication list, timing, recent changes, renal / hepatic function, and adverse event history are available.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Drug lists and prescribing recommendations may be outdated; use current formularies and drug references.
- Pharmacogenomics is evolving and not a substitute for clinical monitoring.

## Clinically Useful Points

- For PD, therapy may need to align with medication "on" periods, and wearing-off diaries can inform timing.
- For epilepsy, track seizures during therapy and watch for sedation, ataxia, dizziness, cognitive slowing, and abrupt discontinuation risk.
- For anticoagulants, handling, fall risk, and bruising / bleeding precautions matter.
- For antihypertensives, monitor orthostatic symptoms, perceived exertion, heart rate response, hydration, and electrolytes when relevant.

## Research-Useful Points

- Rehabilitation trials should record medication class, dose changes, timing relative to therapy, and adverse effects.
- Functional outcomes may be confounded by medication timing and polypharmacy.

## One-Sentence Summary

WHO 2020 recommends that first-3-years ECD services support responsive caregiving, early learning, integration of caregiving with nutrition interventions, and psychosocial support for maternal mental health, while warning that intervention definitions, long-term outcomes, and implementation evidence remain limited.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: WHO First-3-Years ECD Recommendation Bundle

#### One-Sentence Definition

The WHO first-3-years ECD recommendation bundle treats responsive caregiving, early learning, nutrition-service integration, and maternal mental health support as core health-sector actions for improving early childhood development.

#### Known Facts

- The guideline provides global evidence-informed recommendations on improving early childhood development.
- Intended users include policy-makers, development agencies, implementing partners, district and sub-national health managers, health workers, and NGOs.
- The guideline focuses on the first 3 years of life within the broader ECD age range from conception to 8 years.
- `Nurturing care` includes health, nutrition, security and safety, responsive caregiving, and opportunities for early learning.
- WHO recommendation 1: all infants and children should receive responsive care during the first 3 years; parents and other caregivers should be supported to provide responsive care.
- Recommendation 1 strength: strong; certainty of evidence: moderate for responsive care.
- WHO recommendation 2: all infants and children should have early learning activities with parents and other caregivers during the first 3 years; parents and other caregivers should be supported to engage in early learning.
- Recommendation 2 strength: strong; quality of evidence: moderate for early learning.
- WHO recommendation 3: support for responsive care and early learning should be included as part of interventions for optimal nutrition of infants and young children.
- Recommendation 3 strength: strong; quality of evidence: moderate.
- WHO recommendation 4: psychosocial interventions to support maternal mental health should be integrated into early childhood health and development services.
- Recommendation 4 strength: strong; certainty of evidence: moderate.
- For responsive caregiving, the guideline reports benefits for ECD and caregiver outcomes, with no harmful impacts found; long-term outcome data were not available.
- For early learning interventions, evidence came from 22 RCTs, mostly in HICs; pooled effects were modest for cognitive development, motor development, and attachment.
- Early learning intervention pooled cognitive effect: SMD 0.20, 95% CI 0.01 to 0.39; certainty low.
- Early learning intervention pooled language effect was not significant: SMD 0.07, 95% CI -0.11 to 0.24; certainty low.
- Early learning intervention pooled motor effect: SMD 0.32, 95% CI 0.12 to 0.52; certainty low.
- Early learning intervention pooled behaviour-problem effect was not significant: SMD -0.25, 95% CI -0.54 to 0.04; certainty very low.
- For caregiving interventions targeting socioemotional and behavioural development, WHO declined to make a recommendation and prioritized future research.
- The socioemotional / behavioural evidence base included 10 studies, all from HICs, and child behaviour pooled effect was not significant: SMD -0.02, 95% CI -0.07 to 0.02.
- Integrated caregiving and nutrition interventions included 18 studies, all in LMICs.
- In general LMIC populations, combined caregiving and nutrition interventions improved cognitive, language, and motor development compared with standard care.
- Combined caregiving and nutrition versus standard care: cognitive SMD 0.57, 95% CI 0.32 to 0.82; language SMD 0.40, 95% CI 0.17 to 0.63; motor SMD 0.40, 95% CI 0.26 to 0.53.
- Combined caregiving and nutrition versus nutrition alone improved cognitive, language, and motor development.
- Growth outcomes were not consistently improved; HAZ and WAZ generally showed no significant benefit, while some WHZ comparisons showed benefits.
- Effects on development outcomes were higher in malnourished children than in universally implemented studies.
- Psychosocial maternal mental health interventions improved maternal anxiety symptoms and maternal depressive symptoms in the perinatal period.
- Maternal anxiety symptom effect: SMD -0.51, 95% CI -0.72 to -0.30.
- Maternal depressive symptom effect: SMD -0.70, 95% CI -0.92 to -0.47.
- Child-development benefits from maternal mental health interventions were limited and less consistently reported than maternal symptom outcomes.
- The guideline states that study definitions of responsive caregiving and early learning were broad and inconsistently operationalized.
- The guideline states that studies were not designed to compare specific caregiving interventions; such comparisons should be interpreted cautiously.
- The guideline planned a five-year review or earlier update if significant new evidence or validity concerns emerged.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Health sector has repeated contact with infants, young children, and caregivers
-> services include responsive caregiving, early learning, nutrition integration, and maternal mental health support
-> caregivers receive observation, coaching, psychosocial support, and practical learning opportunities
-> daily caregiver-child interaction becomes more responsive and developmentally stimulating
-> child developmental inputs improve while health, nutrition, and caregiver mental health needs are addressed together
```

#### Inferences

- The guideline supports treating ECD as a health-sector responsibility, not only an education or social-service topic.
- For clinical workflow, the strongest practical implication is to embed brief caregiving and early-learning coaching into existing child-health and nutrition contacts.
- For malnourished or nutritionally vulnerable children, nutrition services should include responsive feeding, play, communication, and caregiver support rather than focusing only on anthropometry or calories.
- Maternal mental health support should be considered upstream ECD infrastructure, but it should not be oversold as a direct child developmental treatment when child outcomes were sparsely measured.

#### Assumptions

- Health systems have enough workforce training, supervision, referral capacity, and time to add ECD content without diluting other essential services.
- Recommendations are adapted to local sociocultural context, language, family structure, disability access, and resource constraints.
- Programmes monitor both implementation quality and child / caregiver outcomes.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Long-term developmental, educational, and functional outcomes were not available for many intervention types.
- Measurement tools for caregiver knowledge, practices, and interactions were heterogeneous and often not well standardized across sociocultural contexts.
- The evidence does not establish a single optimal programme model, dose, delivery platform, or active ingredient.
- Socioemotional / behavioural caregiving interventions did not receive a recommendation because evidence was insufficient and HIC-only.
- Child outcomes in maternal mental health intervention studies were limited; the recommendation relies strongly on maternal symptom benefits and plausible indirect developmental benefit.
- Because the source itself planned a five-year review after 2020, guideline currency should be checked before policy-level use.

## Clinically Useful Points

- In well-child, nutrition, home-visit, or early-development services, document the actual responsive caregiving or early learning practice coached, not just generic education.
- For nutrition programmes, include responsive care and early learning as part of optimal nutrition intervention design.
- Maternal mental health screening and psychosocial intervention pathways should be part of ECD service planning.
- Do not tell families that a single early-learning or parenting programme is proven superior; the guideline does not support that comparison.
- Do not treat first-3-years ECD support as a replacement for formal early intervention, developmental evaluation, child protection, or specialist mental health referral when those are indicated.

## Research-Useful Points

- Research gaps include scalable responsive caregiving interventions in LMICs, population / setting subgroup effects, intervention costing, outcome-tool validity, and optimization of combined nutrition-caregiving strategies.
- The guideline also identifies gaps in father / non-mother caregiver outcomes, multisectoral implementation, and psychosocial maternal mental health implementation.
- Future studies should report training, supervision, delivery-agent compensation, programme fidelity, barriers, facilitators, and longer-term child outcomes.

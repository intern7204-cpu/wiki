## One-Sentence Summary

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Acute medical frailty and early mobilization

#### One-Sentence Definition

Acute medical frailty rehabilitation treats immobility, sarcopenia, deconditioning, cardiopulmonary limitation, renal dysfunction, delirium, and ICU-acquired weakness as modifiable barriers to function.

#### Known Facts

- Frailty decreases mobility and function; clinicians should prioritize mobility across medical settings.
- Cardiopulmonary rehabilitation principles can apply to patients with primary cardiopulmonary disease and those with secondary cardiopulmonary disability.
- Cardiac rehabilitation is described as class Ia evidence-based secondary prevention that reduces morbidity and mortality.
- Aerobic training prescription includes intensity, duration, frequency, and specificity.
- Immobility has multisystem effects, including muscle, metabolic, cardiovascular, pulmonary, thrombotic, cognitive, and functional consequences.
- Renal failure and dialysis create unique debility and exercise safety issues.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Acute illness + hospitalization + organ dysfunction + bedrest
-> immobility, deconditioning, frailty, delirium, ICU-acquired weakness
-> reduced reserve and rehab tolerance
-> early mobilization + cardiopulmonary monitoring + renal / hemodynamic precautions
-> preserved mobility and safer transition of care
```

#### Inferences

- Frailty is not a reason to omit rehab; it is a reason to dose and monitor rehab more carefully.
- Acute rehab plans should document hemodynamic, ventilatory, renal, delirium, and transition constraints.

#### Assumptions

- Patient has been medically screened for mobilization safety and monitoring parameters are available.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Specific mobilization thresholds may vary by ICU protocol, dialysis timing, hemodynamics, and local policy.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Dyspnea history should differentiate pulmonary, cardiac, anemia, peripheral circulation, obesity, psychogenic, and deconditioning causes.
- CPET is framed as useful for diagnostic, prognostic, and exercise prescription guidance when feasible.
- Cardiac rehab includes assessment, aerobic exercise, strength training, activity counseling, psychosocial management, risk-factor management, nutrition, and weight management.
- Dialysis patients may have sarcopenia, uremia, electrolyte issues, bone disease, weakness, and post-dialysis variability affecting therapy.

## Research-Useful Points

- Acute care outcomes should include mobility dose, delirium, ICU-acquired weakness, discharge destination, readmission, functional capacity, and participation.
- Frailty measurement tools should be linked to intervention decisions, not just risk prediction.

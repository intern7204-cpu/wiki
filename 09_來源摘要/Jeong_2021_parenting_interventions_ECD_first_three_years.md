# Source Summary: Parenting Interventions to Promote Early Child Development in the First Three Years of Life

## Source Type

- Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials.
- Full title: *Parenting interventions to promote early child development in the first three years of life: A global systematic review and meta-analysis*.
- Authors: Jeong J, Franchett EE, Ramos de Oliveira CV, Rehmani K, Yousafzai AK.
- Journal: *PLOS Medicine*, 2021.
- DOI: `10.1371/journal.pmed.1003602`.
- This summary uses only `C:\原始資料\journal.pmed.1003602\journal.pmed.1003602.md`.

## Reliability Level

- Tier 1 within this wiki because it is a global systematic review / meta-analysis restricted to RCTs.
- Strengths: PRISMA reporting, broad database search, duplicate screening / extraction, risk-of-bias assessment, robust variance estimation, multiple child and caregiver outcomes, and moderator analyses.
- Main limitations: substantial unexplained heterogeneity, variable psychometric reporting, inadequate intervention reporting, statistical multiplicity, small-sample bias for child language and parent-child interaction outcomes, and limited long-term follow-up evidence.

## One-Sentence Summary

Parenting interventions delivered during the first 3 years of life improved several early child development and caregiving outcomes, especially when responsive caregiving content was included, but effects varied substantially and parental depressive symptoms did not significantly improve.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Parenting interventions as first-3-years ECD and caregiving support

#### One-Sentence Definition

Parenting interventions in the first 3 years are structured caregiver-focused programs intended to improve caregiver knowledge, practices, parent-child interaction, and early developmental outcomes through support for responsive interaction, learning opportunities, and caregiving behavior.

#### Known Facts

- The review searched MEDLINE, Embase, PsycINFO, CINAHL, Web of Science, and Global Health Library through November 15, 2020.
- The final set included 111 articles representing 102 unique RCTs.
- Included trials were implemented across 33 countries: 61 trials in 14 high-income countries and 41 trials in 19 low- and middle-income countries.
- Included interventions targeted children from pregnancy through the first 3 years of life and had to measure at least one ECD outcome.
- Child outcomes included cognitive, language, motor, socioemotional development, behavior problems, and attachment.
- Parenting outcomes included parenting knowledge, parenting practices, parent-child interactions, and parental depressive symptoms.
- Pooled effects were positive for cognitive development: `SMD = 0.32`, 95% CI `0.23 to 0.40`, `P < 0.001`, `I2 = 89%`.
- Pooled effects were positive for language development: `SMD = 0.28`, 95% CI `0.18 to 0.37`, `P < 0.001`, `I2 = 91%`.
- Pooled effects were positive for motor development: `SMD = 0.24`, 95% CI `0.15 to 0.32`, `P < 0.001`, `I2 = 84%`.
- Pooled effects were positive for socioemotional development: `SMD = 0.19`, 95% CI `0.10 to 0.28`, `P < 0.001`, `I2 = 81%`.
- Pooled effects showed reduction in behavior problems: `SMD = -0.13`, 95% CI `-0.18 to -0.08`, `P < 0.001`, `I2 = 53%`.
- Pooled effects were positive for infant-caregiver attachment: `SMD = 0.29`, 95% CI `0.18 to 0.40`, `P < 0.001`, `I2 = 0%`.
- Parenting knowledge improved: `SMD = 0.56`, 95% CI `0.33 to 0.79`, `P < 0.001`.
- Parenting practices improved: `SMD = 0.33`, 95% CI `0.22 to 0.44`, `P < 0.001`.
- Parent-child interactions improved: `SMD = 0.39`, 95% CI `0.24 to 0.53`, `P < 0.001`.
- Parental depressive symptoms did not significantly improve: `SMD = -0.07`, 95% CI `-0.16 to 0.02`, `P = 0.08`.
- Seventy of 102 interventions included some responsive caregiving component.
- Responsive caregiving interventions had greater effects than non-responsive-caregiving interventions on child cognitive development, parenting knowledge, parenting practices, and parent-child interactions.
- LMIC trials showed larger effects than HIC trials for child cognitive, language, motor development, and parenting practices.
- No clear effect modification was found by child age, delivery modality, setting, or study risk-of-bias score for most outcomes.
- Only 7 interventions engaged fathers to some degree, and paternal outcomes were rarely measured.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Structured parenting intervention in the first 3 years
-> caregiver knowledge, responsive caregiving, stimulation, play / learning support, and interaction practice
-> improved parenting practices and parent-child interaction
-> richer early learning input and more adaptive relational context
-> improved early cognitive, language, motor, socioemotional, behavioral, and attachment outcomes
```

#### Inferences

- Responsive caregiving is a plausible active ingredient because it moderated several child and caregiver outcomes, but this source does not isolate it as the only mechanism.
- Parenting interventions should be written as structured programs with content, practice, fidelity, and implementation context, not as generic advice.
- Parent mental health cannot be assumed to improve from parenting content alone; depressive symptoms likely require explicit psychological or mental-health components.
- Larger LMIC effects may reflect greater baseline developmental risk, different program designs, or contextual differences; the review did not directly compare the same intervention across LMIC and HIC settings.

#### Assumptions

- Included RCTs are sufficiently comparable for pooled meta-analysis despite wide differences in content, delivery setting, measurement, and population risk.
- Short-term post-intervention outcomes meaningfully reflect early developmental change.
- Parenting practices and observed parent-child interaction are valid proximal mechanisms for downstream child outcomes.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Considerable heterogeneity remained unexplained across most outcomes.
- Intervention content and implementation details were often inadequately reported.
- Many studies did not report reliability, validity, or adaptation procedures for outcome measures.
- Multiple outcomes and moderator tests increase the risk of false-positive subgroup findings.
- Egger's tests suggested small-sample bias for child language and parent-child interactions.
- Long-term sustainability, fadeout, booster-session needs, cost-effectiveness, paternal inclusion, and implementation at scale remain uncertain.

## Clinically Useful Points

- For infants and toddlers, parenting interventions can be framed as an upstream developmental support, especially for cognition, language, motor development, attachment, and parent-child interaction.
- Program notes should specify the active content: responsive caregiving, play / learning support, parent-child interaction feedback, behavioral guidance, or caregiver practice.
- Do not write that parenting intervention treats caregiver depression unless the program explicitly includes a mental health component and the evidence supports that outcome.
- Delivery format should be selected by local feasibility, access, resources, and cultural context; this review did not show one universally superior setting or modality.

## Research-Useful Points

- Future studies should report intervention content and implementation using structured reporting standards such as TIDieR.
- Mediation analysis is needed to test whether improved parent-child interaction actually carries child developmental effects.
- Trials should preregister outcomes and report allocation concealment, analyzed outcomes, and null findings.
- Father-inclusive intervention design and paternal outcome measurement remain major evidence gaps.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- 「越早、越久、越多次、home visiting 一定比較有效」不被本來源直接支持；child age、duration、delivery modality 與 setting 大多沒有穩定 moderator effect。
- 「parenting intervention 可同時處理 caregiver depression」不被 pooled result 支持。
- 「ECD benefit 代表所有 developmental domains 都等量改善」不成立；effect sizes and measurement certainty vary by outcome.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Pulmonary rehabilitation in chronic lung disease

#### One-Sentence Definition

Pulmonary rehabilitation is multidisciplinary care for chronic lung disease that combines medical optimization, exercise, breathing retraining, airway clearance, oxygen planning, education, nutrition, and psychosocial support.

#### Known Facts

- PR is described as evidence-based, multidisciplinary, and comprehensive.
- Chronic lung diseases include obstructive, restrictive, pulmonary vascular, and hypoventilation patterns.
- COPD is the most common reason for PR referral.
- Oxygen therapy reduces mortality in appropriately selected COPD patients with hypoxemia.
- PR exercise prescription includes aerobic, resistance, flexibility, breathing, posture, and functional training.
- Airway clearance strategies are indicated when cough mechanics, mucus rheology, airway structure, or mucociliary clearance are abnormal.
- Diaphragmatic breathing may increase work of breathing and dyspnea in COPD compared with natural breathing pattern.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Chronic lung disease
-> dyspnea, deconditioning, muscle weakness, secretion burden, anxiety, activity avoidance
-> lower exercise tolerance and ADL limitation
-> PR: exercise + breathing retraining + airway clearance + oxygen + education + nutrition
-> improved symptoms, function, quality of life, and self-management
```

#### Inferences

- PR requires physiologic classification; obstructive, restrictive, pulmonary vascular, and hypoventilation disorders should not share one generic program.
- Oxygen logistics are part of rehabilitation feasibility, not only pulmonary medicine.

#### Assumptions

- Patient is medically stable enough for progressive training and has oxygen / cardiopulmonary risk assessment when indicated.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Long COVID respiratory symptoms may be multifactorial and still evolving.
- Transplant and diabetes recommendations are highly time-sensitive.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Pursed-lip breathing, slow deep breathing, localized expansion, posture, and inspiratory resistance training may be used selectively.
- Cough assist has contraindications such as recent pneumothorax / pneumomediastinum and bullous emphysema.
- Transplant rehabilitation must account for deconditioning, immunosuppression, glucocorticoid effects, neuropathy, myopathy, and infection risk.
- Diabetes exercise prescription must account for neuropathy, retinopathy, autonomic dysfunction, renal disease, foot risk, and hypoglycemia.

## Research-Useful Points

- PR outcomes should track symptoms, 6MWT / CPET when appropriate, dyspnea, strength, ADL, readmission, oxygen use, and quality of life.
- Transplant rehabilitation should study prehabilitation, early ICU rehabilitation, posttransplant complications, and transition to outpatient programs.

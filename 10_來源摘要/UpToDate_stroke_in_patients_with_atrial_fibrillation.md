AF-related stroke 的核心不是看到 AF 就停止診斷，而是先完整確認 competing stroke mechanism，再依 hemorrhagic transformation risk 暫停/重啟 anticoagulation，長期多數使用 DOAC，若發生 anticoagulation failure 則先查 adherence、dose、LAA thrombus 與 competing mechanism，而非 reflex 加 antiplatelet。

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: AF-related stroke 病因歸屬與診斷評估

#### One-Sentence Definition

AF-related stroke attribution 是在 AF patient 發生 ischemic stroke / TIA 時，判斷 LAA / cardiac embolism 是否為最可能病因，同時主動排除 large artery disease、small vessel disease、aortic source 或其他 competing mechanisms。

#### Known Facts

- AF patient 的 ischemic stroke 常由 cardiac embolus caused by LAA thrombus 引起。
- Cardioembolic stroke from AF generally has increased severity compared with embolic stroke from carotid disease.
- AF-related embolic strokes may affect any vascular territory or multiple territories, often with wedge-shaped cortical and subcortical infarcts.
- Silent cerebral infarction is associated with AF; one 2014 systematic review/meta-analysis cited by the source found MRI prevalence 40 percent and CT prevalence 22 percent among AF patients, but most included studies were cross-sectional.
- The presence of AF in a stroke patient does not always mean a causal relationship.
- Complete stroke evaluation is still useful even when AF is present because another mechanism may change treatment.
- Workup includes brain and neurovascular imaging, acute cardiac rhythm monitoring, TTE, and selected TEE.
- TEE is useful for LAA thrombus and other cardiac/aortic embolic sources; TTE rarely sees LAA thrombus.
- TEE is not used to exclude AF as the cause because residual atrial thrombus may or may not be present.

#### Mechanism Chain

AF -> atrial mechanical dysfunction and stasis, especially in LAA -> thrombus formation -> embolization to intracranial artery -> often larger occlusion / cortical or multi-territory infarct -> higher disability and mortality risk.  
However, AF also coexists with age, hypertension, atherosclerosis and small vessel disease -> competing mechanisms remain plausible -> treatment must be mechanism-informed rather than AF-only.

#### Inferences

- A chart diagnosis should distinguish "stroke in a patient with AF" from "AF-caused cardioembolic stroke."
- If vascular imaging shows high-grade ipsilateral stenosis or lacunar pattern is convincing, AF still requires anticoagulation in eligible patients, but the competing mechanism may require separate treatment.

#### Assumptions

- Adult ischemic stroke / TIA setting.
- Brain imaging, neurovascular imaging and basic cardiac evaluation are available.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Silent cerebral infarction association does not prove direct causality in cross-sectional studies.
- AFDAS may include neurogenic and cardiogenic subtypes, but anticoagulation management does not currently distinguish them in this source.

### Concept: AF stroke anticoagulation timing and long-term strategy

#### One-Sentence Definition

AF stroke anticoagulation strategy separates acute hemorrhagic transformation risk from long-term embolic prevention: anticoagulation is usually withheld immediately, restarted within days to two weeks by infarct size and bleeding risk, and continued long term in eligible patients.

#### Known Facts

- Anticoagulation is usually temporarily withheld during acute ischemic stroke due to hemorrhagic transformation risk.
- Acute antiplatelet therapy may be warranted while anticoagulation is withheld; early recurrent stroke risk is 3 to 5 percent in the first two weeks.
- Long-term oral anticoagulation is started or resumed after hemorrhagic transformation risk diminishes, usually within the first days to two weeks, guided mainly by infarct size.
- For medically stable AF patients with small- or moderate-sized infarct and no intracranial bleeding, warfarin can be initiated soon, while the source prefers waiting 48 hours to start a DOAC because DOACs act faster.
- Withholding anticoagulation for one week has generally been recommended for large ischemic stroke, symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation or poorly controlled hypertension.
- Early heparin after acute cardioembolic stroke should generally be avoided.
- For most ischemic stroke / TIA patients with AF, DOAC is preferred over VKA.
- VKA is indicated or preferred in selected settings including mechanical valves, clinically significant rheumatic mitral stenosis, relevant DOAC interactions, and selected severe kidney dysfunction.
- LAA occlusion should be considered when long-term anticoagulation is not an option but short-term antithrombotic therapy can be tolerated.

#### Mechanism Chain

Acute infarcted tissue -> fragile vasculature and blood-brain barrier injury -> anticoagulation increases hemorrhagic transformation risk.  
AF -> ongoing embolic risk -> long-term anticoagulation substantially reduces recurrent embolism.  
Therefore acute timing depends on infarct size / hemorrhage risk, while long-term selection depends on AF indication, renal function, valve disease, interactions and bleeding risk.

#### Inferences

- "AF stroke needs anticoagulation" and "do not anticoagulate immediately" are not contradictory; they refer to different phases.
- In rehab or ward transfer, anticoagulation plan should document target restart date or reason for delay.

#### Assumptions

- No active intracranial hemorrhage.
- Stroke mechanism and infarct size have been evaluated enough to estimate hemorrhagic transformation risk.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Optimal timing for larger nonhemorrhagic infarcts remains evolving; the source notes studies suggesting early DOAC within 4 to 7 days may be safe in some patients.
- Prior ICH requires individualized risk balance and is not resolved by this source alone.

### Concept: Anticoagulation failure in AF stroke

#### One-Sentence Definition

Anticoagulation failure in AF stroke is an ischemic stroke / TIA occurring despite DOAC or VKA therapy, requiring reassessment of exposure, adherence, dose, LAA thrombus and competing stroke mechanisms before changing drugs or adding therapies.

#### Known Facts

- All AF patients with ischemic stroke despite VKA or DOAC should have thorough evaluation for cardioembolic and noncardioembolic mechanisms.
- Missed DOAC doses and subtherapeutic VKA intensity are common causes.
- In an analysis cited by the source, DOAC breakthrough events were attributed to cardioembolism 49 percent, poor adherence or insufficient dose 23 percent, and competing mechanism 28 percent.
- In the same analysis, VKA breakthrough events were attributed to cardioembolism 37 percent, poor adherence or insufficient dose 43 percent, and competing mechanism 20 percent.
- LAA thrombus suggests reassessing dosing and compliance.
- For DOAC events, correct dose and adherence should be verified; if thrombus is present despite appropriate dosing and compliance, changing DOAC may be reasonable, but optimal treatment is uncertain.
- A retrospective cohort cited by the source found higher recurrent ischemic stroke risk among patients switched to another DOAC or warfarin compared with unchanged DOAC after an event; this supports caution rather than a simple switching rule.
- For warfarin breakthrough with therapeutic INR 2 to 3, the source favors switching to DOAC rather than routine antiplatelet addition when DOAC is not contraindicated.
- Routine antiplatelet addition increases major hemorrhage, especially brain hemorrhage, and benefit is less defined than optimizing anticoagulation.

#### Mechanism Chain

Breakthrough stroke -> possible no/low anticoagulant exposure, persistent LAA thrombus, cardioembolism despite adequate therapy, or noncardioembolic mechanism -> evaluation determines target -> optimize exposure or address competing mechanism -> avoid empiric stacking of antithrombotics unless a separate indication exists.

#### Inferences

- "DOAC failure" should not be written until dose, renal function, interactions, adherence and stroke mechanism are reviewed.
- In rehabilitation medication reconciliation, the most important question is not only "which anticoagulant" but "why did stroke occur despite this regimen?"

#### Assumptions

- The event is confirmed ischemic stroke / TIA rather than mimic or hemorrhage.
- Medication history and renal function data are available.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- No consensus exists for optimal DOAC-to-DOAC switching after confirmed adherent therapeutic DOAC breakthrough.
- Observational switching data may be confounded by indication severity.

## Clinically Useful Points

- AF does not eliminate the need for full stroke workup.
- Acute reperfusion eligibility must account for warfarin anticoagulant effect, recent DOAC use, kidney function and relevant coagulation assays.
- Acute anticoagulation is usually withheld; early heparin after cardioembolic stroke is generally avoided.
- Restart anticoagulation by infarct size and hemorrhagic transformation risk; small/moderate infarct can restart earlier than large infarct.
- Long-term anticoagulation is indicated for most AF patients after ischemic stroke / TIA; DOAC is preferred unless VKA-specific conditions exist.
- High fall risk alone should not automatically preclude anticoagulation; absolute intracranial bleeding risk attributable to anticoagulation may be small relative to AF embolic stroke risk.
- Anticoagulant-intolerant patients may be considered for LAA occlusion if they can tolerate short-term antithrombotic therapy.
- Breakthrough stroke requires adherence, dose, interaction, renal function, TEE/LAA thrombus and competing-mechanism evaluation.

## Research-Useful Points

- AFDAS may not be homogeneous; neurogenic versus cardiogenic mechanisms need better stratification, but current anticoagulation management does not distinguish them.
- Optimal early DOAC timing after larger nonhemorrhagic infarcts remains active.
- Anticoagulation failure after DOAC therapy lacks a consensus strategy; observational switching data should be interpreted cautiously.
- AF with high-grade ipsilateral carotid stenosis is a major competing-cause problem requiring adjudication rather than single-label classification.

## One-Sentence Summary

Exerkines are exercise-responsive signalling moieties released by multiple tissues through endocrine, paracrine, or autocrine pathways; they plausibly mediate exercise-related cardiometabolic, immune, neurological, musculoskeletal, and resilience effects, but the field requires stricter exposure control, sampling, omics methods, human validation, and restraint against “exercise pill” overclaiming.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Exerkines 作為運動誘發多器官訊號分子

#### One-Sentence Definition

Exerkines are signalling moieties released in response to acute and/or chronic exercise that act through endocrine, paracrine, and/or autocrine pathways across multiple tissues, not only skeletal muscle.

#### Known Facts

- The review defines exerkines as signalling moieties released in response to acute and/or chronic exercise and acting through endocrine, paracrine, and/or autocrine pathways.
- The term exerkine was coined in 2016, but the concept of humoral factors mediating exercise benefit is older.
- Skeletal muscle-derived exerkines are myokines, but exerkine work now includes cardiokines, hepatokines, adipokines, batokines, neurokines, and other tissue-derived factors.
- Exerkines can include cytokines, hormones, neurotransmitters, proteins, nucleic acids, microRNA, mRNA, mitochondrial DNA, lipids, metabolites, and extracellular-vesicle cargo.
- IL-6 has been the most extensively studied myokine since demonstration of contracting skeletal muscle IL-6 release in 2000.
- The review distinguishes exercise from broader physical activity: exercise is intentional physical activity such as aerobic training, resistance training, or HIIT, whereas physical activity includes occupational and domestic activity.
- Exercise response varies by external factors such as exercise timing, fed-fasting state, post-exercise diet, exercise type, intensity, duration, and sample timing.
- Exercise response varies by internal factors such as genetics and phenotype.
- The HERITAGE Family Study is cited as finding maximal heritability estimate for aerobic capacity response around 47%.
- Chronic exercise training in HERITAGE elicited around 20% non-response for improved aerobic capacity.
- The review cites 7-15% adverse responses in systolic blood pressure, HDL cholesterol, triglycerides, or insulin in some exercise-training contexts.
- MoTrPAC is described as a large NIH-supported effort to characterize molecular transducers of physical activity in humans and animals.
- Acute exerkine response is influenced by exercise type, duration, underlying fitness, fed-fasting status, and post-exercise sample timing.
- Classic acute exercise cytokine responses include IL-6, IL-8, IL-1RA, and IL-10.
- A marathon study is cited in which IL-6, IL-1RA, IL-10, and TNF increased immediately after exercise, peaked 1-2 hours after exercise, and remained elevated for about 4 hours.
- The acute exerkine response does not necessarily parallel the chronic training response.
- Chronic exercise exposure may alter humoral factors at rest and is often associated with long-term metabolic adaptations and lower inflammation.
- Exerkine research is shifting from single-factor measurements to omics profiling: lipidomics, metabolomics, proteomics, transcriptomics, epigenomics, RNA-seq, methyl-seq, and ATAC-seq.
- A 2020 human acute exercise study measured molecular changes across insulin sensitivity spectrum and found exercise altered more than 50% of measured molecules across multiple omics platforms.
- Extracellular vesicles are increasingly studied as carriers of exercise-related inter-organ signals, but plasma-derived vesicle analysis has major preanalytical and isolation challenges.
- Exerkines act not only as distant endocrine signals but also locally through autocrine and paracrine effects.
- Cardiometabolic exerkine examples include nitric oxide, VEGF, IL-6, IL-8, FGF21, angiopoietin 1, fractalkine, musclin, and myonectin.
- Exercise may affect adipose tissue through lipolysis and possibly WAT browning, but the human relevance of irisin-mediated browning remains controversial.
- 12,13-diHOME is discussed as a BAT-derived exerkine that can affect skeletal muscle fatty acid uptake and cardiometabolic phenotypes.
- TGFβ2 is discussed as an exercise-induced adipokine linked to lactate-mediated tissue-to-tissue communication and glucose / fatty-acid metabolism.
- Exerkines can influence skeletal muscle through signals such as apelin, 12,13-diHOME, follistatin, fetuin-A, IL-7, IL-15, LIF, syndecan 4, and myostatin.
- Exercise alters the gut microbiome in humans and animals; effects may depend on diet, intensity, modality, and sustainment.
- Exerkines relevant to glucose homeostasis include BAIBA, fractalkine, fetuin-A, follistatin, irisin, and IL-6, but some findings conflict.
- Acute exercise can be initially pro-inflammatory and then followed by anti-inflammatory cytokine responses; chronic training is associated with lower resting systemic and tissue inflammation.
- IL-6 is context-dependent: transient acute exercise IL-6 differs from chronically elevated resting IL-6.
- Exercise-related immune effects are discussed as possible contributors to cancer resilience or co-adjuvant cancer therapy, but much evidence is preclinical.
- Exercise may benefit brain function; effects on cognition remain variable due to lack of standardized lifelong RCTs and comparable cognitive assessments.
- Exercise-brain exerkine candidates include BDNF, irisin / FNDC5, cathepsin B, adiponectin, kynurenine pathway shifts, GPLD1, and clusterin-related pathways.
- The review states that whether exerkines cross the blood-brain barrier to exert CNS effects remains uncertain.
- Bone-muscle crosstalk involves mechanical loading and secreted factors; candidate signals include TGFβ1, sclerostin, apelin, myostatin, irisin, IL-6, IL-7, and BAIBA.
- The review explicitly lists contentious questions: acute versus chronic inconsistency, animal versus human inconsistency, and variability in outcomes and sampling.
- The review states that “exercise in a pill” remains wishful thinking despite therapeutic interest.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Acute or chronic exercise exposure
-> tissue-specific metabolic, mechanical, inflammatory, neural, and vascular perturbations
-> release or alteration of exerkines from skeletal muscle, heart, liver, adipose tissue, BAT, endothelium, immune cells, nervous system, bone, gut-related pathways, and extracellular vesicles
-> endocrine / paracrine / autocrine signalling across target tissues
-> acute homeostatic responses and chronic adaptation in metabolism, inflammation, vascular function, muscle remodeling, brain function, bone, and resilience
-> heterogeneous outcomes because exposure, timing, sampling, species, phenotype, genetics, and disease context differ
```

#### Inferences

- Exerkines are best treated as a systems-biology language for exercise adaptation, not as a list of clinical biomarkers ready for routine ordering.
- When discussing exercise as medicine, exerkines help explain why exercise effects are pleiotropic and multi-organ, but they do not replace exercise prescription or outcome measurement.
- Clinical translation should start by defining exercise exposure and target outcome before interpreting any molecular signal.

#### Assumptions

- The review's broad synthesis is being used for conceptual organization, not for disease-specific prescribing.
- Exercise exposure, timing, and sampling can be documented with enough rigor to interpret molecular data.
- Human validation is required before a candidate exerkine becomes a clinical target or biomarker.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The molecular mechanisms underlying many exercise benefits remain incompletely defined.
- Acute and chronic exercise responses can differ or conflict.
- Animal, cell, and human findings are not interchangeable.
- Sampling timing, tissue source, exercise mode, intensity, fed-fasting state, circadian context, fitness, phenotype, and comorbid disease can all change interpretation.
- Exerkine therapeutics remain investigational; “exercise in a pill” is not established clinical care.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Use exerkines to teach why exercise benefits cannot be reduced to calorie expenditure, VO2, lactate, or one molecule.
- Do not order or interpret exerkine-like biomarkers clinically unless a validated indication exists.
- When reviewing exercise studies, check whether acute versus chronic exposure, sampling time, exercise mode, intensity, diet, circadian timing, and participant phenotype are specified.
- Interpret IL-6 by context: transient exercise-induced IL-6 and chronically elevated resting IL-6 are not the same biological statement.
- Treat “myokine” as a subset of “exerkine,” not as a synonym.

## Research-Useful Points

- Exerkine research needs standardized exercise exposure, metadata, sampling timing, phenotype description, and outcome definitions.
- Multi-omics and extracellular-vesicle methods are promising but require preanalytical rigor and cross-study comparability.
- Human translational studies should separate biomarker association from causal mediation and therapeutic target validation.
- Wearables may help quantify real-world exposure and phenotype, but device accuracy, standardization, data access, and context capture remain limitations.

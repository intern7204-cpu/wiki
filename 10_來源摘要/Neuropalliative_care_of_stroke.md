# Source Summary: Neuropalliative care of stroke

## Source Type

## Reliability Level

Tier 6：expert topic review。可用於整理 palliative approach、communication、symptom burden 與 hospice eligibility frame；若需 policy-grade hospice criteria 或 guideline-grade stroke palliative care recommendation，需回查 CMS / AHA scientific statement / specialty guideline 原文。

## One-Sentence Summary

Stroke neuropalliative care is an approach that can begin at presentation and run concurrently with acute treatment, combining symptom management, functional optimization, goals-of-care communication, advance care planning, shared decision-making, caregiver support and end-of-life care when appropriate.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Stroke neuropalliative care integration

#### One-Sentence Definition

Stroke neuropalliative care integrates serious illness communication, symptom management, functional goals, caregiver support and advance care planning into acute and postacute stroke care without requiring abandonment of disease-directed treatment.

#### Known Facts

- Source defines neuropalliative care as optimizing symptom management and functional status, addressing goals of care, discussing advance care planning and engaging in shared decision-making in neurologic disease.
- Palliative care can begin at stroke presentation and can be offered concurrently with acute treatment.
- Palliative care and hospice are not interchangeable; hospice is for patients nearing end of life.
- Primary palliative care is provided by the primary stroke team; specialist palliative care is provided by a multidisciplinary palliative team.
- Primary palliative skills include basic symptom management, serious illness communication and care coordination.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Sudden severe stroke
-> patient often lacks decision capacity + family unprepared
-> high morbidity / mortality + uncertain recovery trajectory
-> need concurrent acute treatment + symptom relief + values clarification
-> shared decisions about ventilation, feeding, procedures, rehabilitation and future care
```

#### Inferences

- Stroke teams need baseline palliative communication skills even when specialist palliative care is unavailable.
- Palliative involvement should be framed as added support and communication infrastructure, not as a signal that active treatment is stopping.

#### Assumptions

- Adult stroke population.
- Local care setting can provide at least primary palliative communication and symptom management.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Stroke-specific palliative care trials remain limited; much efficacy evidence comes from cancer or chronic noncancer serious illness.

### Concept: Serious illness conversation triggers after stroke

#### One-Sentence Definition

Serious illness conversation triggers identify stroke patients and families who need structured discussion of current status, prognosis, treatment goals and possible specialist palliative support.

#### Known Facts

- Source lists general triggers: age 80 or older and hospitalized, patient/family/caregiver request, and an affirmative answer to the surprise question.
- Stroke-specific triggers include mechanical ventilation, predicted major functional and/or cognitive deficits requiring long-term assistance, and long-term artificial nutrition.
- A change in health or prognosis can trigger conversation about status quo, treatment goals and palliative care referral.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
High-risk stroke feature or family request
-> recognition of palliative need
-> family meeting / goals-of-care discussion
-> primary support or specialist palliative referral
-> clearer care plan and transition strategy
```

#### Inferences

- Mechanical ventilation, PEG/tracheostomy decisions and predicted major dependency are not only procedural issues; they are triggers for values-based conversation.

#### Assumptions

- Trigger tools prompt conversation; they do not automatically determine treatment limits.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Trigger tools vary by institution and may be based on age, severity, comorbidity or prognosis rather than direct need.

### Concept: Prognostic uncertainty and time-limited trials

#### One-Sentence Definition

Prognostic uncertainty after severe stroke requires transparent communication about what is known and unknown, with time-limited trials used when recovery potential and patient values are uncertain.

#### Known Facts

- Source states stroke presents suddenly, often before patients/families can prepare values or preferences.
- Many severe stroke patients cannot participate in early treatment decisions; surrogates must use substituted judgment.
- Source cites 2024 neurocritical care guidance recommending avoidance of premature prognostication after severe stroke.
- Prognostic uncertainty includes clinical uncertainty about physical/cognitive recovery and affective uncertainty about future adaptation to disability.
- Prognostic models should not be the primary source to guide treatment decisions, including end-of-life decisions.
- Time-limited trials can reduce uncertainty but require a clear follow-up strategy.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Severe stroke + uncertain recovery + uncertain patient values
-> risk of overuse or underuse of life-sustaining treatment
-> transparent best-case / worst-case communication + second opinion / team input
-> time-limited trial with explicit reassessment plan
-> later decision with more information and less premature closure
```

#### Inferences

- Early DNAR or withdrawal decisions can be vulnerable to self-fulfilling prophecy if based on overly pessimistic predictions.
- Goals-of-care conversations should occur early, but conclusions should remain revisable as prognosis clarifies.

#### Assumptions

- Clinicians can reassess neurologic status and communicate updates over time.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- No prognostic model fully captures individual recovery, family values or adaptation to disability.

### Concept: Symptom and caregiver burden as palliative needs

#### One-Sentence Definition

Poststroke palliative needs include pain, dysphagia, depression, anxiety, caregiver distress and team burden, not only end-of-life care.

#### Known Facts

- Poststroke pain occurs in approximately 30 percent in the source.
- Dysphagia affects more than 50 percent of acute stroke patients in the source; about 50 percent recover within two weeks and about 15 percent have persistent dysphagia at one month.
- Dysphagia is associated with impaired rehabilitation, prolonged hospitalization, aspiration pneumonia, malnutrition and increased mortality.
- A large randomized study cited by the source found no significant benefit to early PEG versus NGT and a borderline increase in death or poor outcome with PEG.
- Caregiver burden after stroke can be substantial and may be higher than in cancer or dementia in cited data.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke impairments + immobility + uncertain prognosis
-> pain, dysphagia, mood/anxiety symptoms, dependency
-> rehabilitation limitation + caregiver strain
-> need symptom control, feeding decisions, education, respite / support and care coordination
```

#### Inferences

- Feeding-tube decisions should be goals-of-care decisions, not only discharge logistics.
- Palliative care should be connected to rehabilitation because symptom control can optimize function and participation.

#### Assumptions

- Symptom management decisions consider sedation, confusion, falls, antithrombotic therapy and rehabilitation goals.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Oral supported feeding after stroke has not been studied in the same way as advanced dementia.
- Stroke-specific palliative symptom protocols remain less mature than general palliative care protocols.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Palliative care can be introduced at stroke presentation and can coexist with reperfusion therapy, neurocritical care and rehabilitation.
- Goals of care are not code status; they describe what outcomes and tradeoffs matter to the patient.
- Stroke-specific triggers for serious illness conversation include mechanical ventilation, predicted major functional/cognitive dependency and long-term artificial nutrition.
- Time-limited trials require an explicit reassessment point and follow-up strategy.
- Prognostic models and scores should support, not replace, clinical judgment and shared decision-making.
- Feeding-tube decisions after dysphagic stroke should explicitly address prognosis, recovery window, patient values and transition constraints.
- Caregiver assessment belongs in stroke care, especially when the patient lacks speech, decision capacity or self-care ability.

## Research-Useful Points

- Stroke palliative care studies should specify operational intervention components, not only the label "palliative care".
- Research should separate acute decision support, symptom management, caregiver intervention, hospice referral and postacute advance care planning.
- Outcomes should include patient and caregiver quality of life, symptom burden, decisional conflict, goal-concordant care, transitions and functional recovery.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- Conflicts with equating palliative care with hospice.
- Conflicts with waiting until treatment failure before discussing goals of care.
- Conflicts with using code status as a substitute for values and goals.
- Conflicts with using prognosis scores alone to justify early withdrawal after severe stroke.
- Conflicts with treating PEG placement as a purely administrative discharge requirement.

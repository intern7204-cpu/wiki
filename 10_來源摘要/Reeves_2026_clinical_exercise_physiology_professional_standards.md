## One-Sentence Summary

Reeves et al. 2026 used a modified e-Delphi consensus process to establish International Clinical Exercise Physiology Professional Standards with 20 elements across professional practice, foundational knowledge, assessment/client management, and evidence-based exercise intervention delivery.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Clinical Exercise Physiology Professional Standards

#### One-Sentence Definition

Clinical Exercise Physiology professional standards define the minimum international competency domains for safe, ethical, culturally responsive, risk-aware, evidence-informed exercise-based care for people at risk of or living with disease or injury.

#### Known Facts

- The project objective was to develop consensus on international professional standards for Clinical Exercise Physiologists.
- The professional standards were designed to inform education and practice internationally.
- The study used a modified e-Delphi model with online surveys and focus groups.
- The steering committee had four members with experience in Clinical Exercise Physiology standards, professional associations, and university-level competency teaching.
- Expert working group members were selected for Clinical Exercise Physiology practice, standards, or curriculum expertise.
- Expert criteria included more than 10 years of applied experience, experience developing/reviewing professional standards, or Clinical Exercise Physiology curriculum development.
- Target expert working group size was 15-30.
- A priori consensus threshold was >=80% of expert working group members rating an element 7-9 on a 9-point Likert scale.
- Elements were also rated as core or non-core.
- The consensus process included initial voting, additional element identification, rationale review, additional element voting, consensus meeting, final voting, steering committee deliberation, and external stakeholder review.
- 21 individuals were recruited to the expert working group.
- Round completion: 19 completed round 1a, 13 completed round 1b, 15 completed round 2a/b, and 14 completed round 3.
- The final standards include four domains.
- The four domains are professional practice, foundational knowledge, assessment and client management, and case formulation / design / delivery of evidence-based interventions.
- The final document includes 20 elements.
- 18 of the 20 final elements reached consensus as core elements.
- Reflective practice and self-management strategy elements did not meet the 80% inclusion threshold in round 3, but were retained by the steering committee because they appear in other health-profession standards and were considered important.
- External stakeholder feedback came from 27 individuals across seven countries.
- The authors state the standards can support international education, accreditation, credentialling, quality assurance, and workforce mobility.

#### Mechanism Chain

1. Exercise is increasingly used to prevent and manage chronic disease, injury, cancer, mental illness, neurological disease, and cardiometabolic conditions.
2. Safe exercise delivery in clinical populations requires more than generic fitness programming.
3. A profession therefore needs explicit standards defining expected knowledge, abilities, ethical boundaries, documentation, risk management, referral behavior, assessment, and intervention delivery.
4. Modified e-Delphi consensus turns distributed expert judgment into a structured professional standard.
5. The resulting four-domain framework can guide education programs, credentialing systems, and practice expectations.
6. The framework improves consistency only if local systems translate it into accreditation, training, registration, and governance.

#### Inferences

- A valid clinical exercise prescription should be judged not only by FITT content, but also by scope, risk management, documentation, referral pathways, behavior-change strategy, contraindication screening, outcome measurement, and self-management planning.
- Clinical Exercise Physiology overlaps with rehabilitation but should not be reduced to either sports training or generic wellness coaching.
- For wiki use, this source strengthens professional-practice framing around [[02_方法學/治療性運動處方]] rather than proving a specific exercise protocol.

#### Assumptions

- International expert consensus can define a meaningful minimum professional framework across countries.
- Local professional bodies, education programs, and clinical systems can adapt the standards without erasing local legal and cultural requirements.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Most experts were recruited from the ICSESP network.
- All but one expert were from western countries, limiting cultural, religious, political, and regional transferability.
- The standards require local governance before they can become licensure or reimbursement rules.
- The source does not test patient outcomes after implementing these standards.
- The source does not compare Clinical Exercise Physiology against physiotherapy, occupational therapy, sports coaching, or other exercise-delivery roles.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Clinical exercise work should explicitly include scope of practice, ethical practice, documentation, privacy/confidentiality, culturally safe person-centred care, collaboration, referral, risk management, and reflective practice.
- Foundational knowledge should connect anatomy, physiology, exercise physiology, prescription/delivery, biomechanics, development, assessment, motor learning/control, nutrition, physical activity/health, health psychology, research methods, and data analysis.
- Assessment/client management should include risk-factor surveillance, adverse sign/symptom action, client-centred communication, referral, risk stratification, contraindication checks, standardized and individualized measures, and patient-reported outcome measures.
- Intervention delivery should include meaningful goal-oriented exercise prescription, safe delivery for complex presentations, monitoring, adaptation, client education, engagement, and self-management support.

## Research-Useful Points

- The source is a professional-standard consensus document, not an outcome trial.
- It can be used as a framework to evaluate whether exercise intervention studies report enough professional-practice context.
- It highlights a research gap: whether implementation of Clinical Exercise Physiology professional standards improves safety, adherence, access, or clinical outcomes.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- Conflicts with the idea that exercise prescription is only FITT.
- Conflicts with the idea that clinical exercise delivery can ignore medications, diagnostic procedures, comorbidity, contraindications, cultural context, referral, or documentation.
- Conflicts with treating international consensus as automatically equivalent to local legal scope of practice.
- Conflicts with treating Clinical Exercise Physiology as purely athletic training.

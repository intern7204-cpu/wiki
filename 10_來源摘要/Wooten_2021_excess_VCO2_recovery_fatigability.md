# Source Summary: Wooten et al. 2021 - Measures of Excess VCO2 and Recovery VCO2 as Indices of Performance Fatigability During Exercise

## Source Type

- Original article.
- Cross-sectional pilot / feasibility study.
- Journal context: *Pilot and Feasibility Studies* 2021;7:131.

## Reliability Level

- Tier 5: original feasibility / pilot study.
- Evidence strength: low to medium for association, low for clinical implementation.
- Primary value: protocol feasibility and hypothesis generation.
- Not guideline-level evidence, not a mechanistic validation study, and not a diagnostic accuracy study.

## One-Sentence Summary

Wooten et al. 2021 showed that a small healthy-adult protocol could obtain estimated excess VCO2, recovery VCO2 off-kinetics, VO2 kinetics, and performance fatigability measures, with several strong exploratory associations, but the findings remain hypothesis-generating because excess VCO2 was estimated and acid-base markers were not measured.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Estimated Excess VCO2 and Recovery VCO2 as Performance Fatigability Indices

#### One-Sentence Definition

Estimated excess VCO2 and recovery VCO2 off-kinetics are CPET-derived, feasibility-stage measures explored as possible indices of performance fatigability after peak and submaximal exercise.

#### Known Facts

- Seven apparently healthy subjects participated; 5 were female and 2 were male.
- Mean age was 30.7 +/- 5.1 years.
- Recruitment occurred between November and December 2017.
- All enrolled subjects completed all exercise testing sessions without adverse events.
- Visit 1 used peak treadmill CPET by Bruce protocol, followed by 10-min recovery and 10-min walk test.
- Visit 2 used submaximal constant work rate treadmill walking at 80% of each subject's AT, using three 6-min bouts separated by 8-min active recovery, followed by 10-min passive recovery and 10-min walk test.
- Gas exchange was measured with a MedGraphics CardiO2 CPET system.
- AT was determined by V-slope method and reported as AT-VO2.
- All subjects achieved RER >= 1.10 and peak heart rate >= 90% of age-predicted values during peak CPET.
- VO2 on-kinetics during CWRT and VO2 / VCO2 off-kinetics after CPET and CWRT were fit with mono-exponential models.
- The first 20 seconds of recovery were excluded from recovery kinetic fitting.
- PFSS was calculated from 10-min walk test velocity change and normalized to distance, then multiplied by 1000.
- Total excess VCO2 was estimated as the difference between total VCO2 area and estimated metabolic VCO2 area above AT, then converted to liters.
- Excess VCO2 accounted for 61% of variability in performance fatigability measured by VO2 on-kinetic ORI: R2 = 0.614.
- Excess VCO2 accounted for 62% of variability in PFSS: R2 = 0.619.
- During CPET recovery, VCO2-off ORI accounted for 70% of variability in VO2 on-kinetic ORI: R2 = 0.695.
- During CPET recovery, VCO2-off Kt accounted for 73% of variability in VO2 on-kinetic ORI: R2 = 0.730.
- During submaximal CWRT recovery, VCO2-off ORI accounted for 93% of variability in performance fatigability in this sample: R2 = 0.928.
- During submaximal CWRT recovery, VCO2-off Kt accounted for 96% of variability in performance fatigability in this sample: R2 = 0.955.
- Peak VO2 and AT-VO2 also related to PFSS in this small sample.

#### Mechanism Chain

1. High-intensity exercise increases reliance on anaerobic glycolytic contribution when oxidative supply cannot fully match demand.
2. H+ load and related by-products can interfere with contractile and metabolic processes.
3. Bicarbonate buffering and related acid-base handling can increase expired CO2 beyond purely oxidative metabolic CO2.
4. This nonmetabolic / excess CO2 can be estimated from CPET VCO2 behavior above AT.
5. The recovery rate of VCO2 may reflect how quickly the system returns toward preexercise acid-base / metabolic homeostasis.
6. If performance fatigability partly reflects impaired metabolic stability and recovery, then estimated excess VCO2 and VCO2 off-kinetics may correlate with fatigability measures.

#### Inferences

- Recovery VCO2 off-kinetics may be a useful research signal for performance fatigability, especially after controlled submaximal work.
- Submaximal CWRT may be a more physiologically controlled setting than a post-CPET 10-min walk test for studying fatigability.
- The strong R2 values are probably best interpreted as a feasibility signal requiring confirmation, not as validated effect sizes.

#### Assumptions

- Estimated metabolic VCO2 above AT can be approximated by extending the pre-AT VCO2-time relation.
- Progression from AT-VCO2 to peak VCO2 would remain linear without excess VCO2.
- V-slope AT is a usable anchor for this algebraic separation.
- PFSS from 10-MWT is a reasonable fatigability measure in younger healthy adults, although the authors explicitly question this.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Sample size was very small: n = 7.
- Sample was non-randomized and convenience-based.
- Participants were apparently healthy young-to-middle adults, limiting clinical generalizability.
- 10-MWT PFSS had been validated in older adults but not in this younger population.
- Work rate constancy could not be controlled during the 10-MWT.
- Excess VCO2 cannot be directly differentiated by the study's method; it was estimated by an algorithm.
- The algorithm assumes linear VCO2 behavior between AT and peak exercise in the absence of excess VCO2.
- AT was determined by V-slope method, which is sensitive to ventilatory response and breathing irregularities.
- Plasma lactate, bicarbonate, and pH were not measured.
- The lack of a gold-standard excess-VCO2 measure reduced precision and statistical power.
- The study was not designed to delineate the primary biochemical mechanism of increased VCO2.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Do not treat estimated excess VCO2 as a direct lactate, pH, or buffering-capacity measurement.
- Do not use Wooten 2021 to justify routine clinical fatigability classification from VCO2.
- The most clinically useful idea is narrower: recovery gas-exchange kinetics may deserve attention when evaluating fatigability, especially in rehabilitation research.
- If this line is used clinically, it should be framed as exploratory physiology, not as a decision cutoff.

## Research-Useful Points

- Future studies should use larger samples and prespecified primary outcomes.
- Future protocols should consider a modality that controls work rate better than 10-MWT, such as cycle ergometry.
- Direct lactate, bicarbonate, and pH measures would be needed to validate the assumed acid-base mechanism.
- VCO2 recovery kinetics after submaximal controlled work may be a promising target for confirmatory study.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- This source conflicts with any claim that excess VCO2 is directly measured during routine CPET.
- This source conflicts with treating estimated excess VCO2 as a validated clinical biomarker of performance fatigability.
- This source conflicts with reading AT terminology as proof of discrete muscle dysoxia; AT here is an operational V-slope anchor.

# Source Summary: Scionti et al. 2020 — Preschool EF Cognitive Training

## Source Type

Systematic review / meta-analysis.

- Citation: Scionti N, Cavallero M, Zogmaister C, Marzocchi GM. 2020. *Is Cognitive Training Effective for Improving Executive Functions in Preschoolers? A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis*. Frontiers in Psychology. DOI: `10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02812`.
- Source file: `C:\原始資料\fpsyg-10-02812\fpsyg-10-02812.md`

## Reliability Level

- Source tier: Tier 1, because this is a systematic review / meta-analysis.
- Confidence: medium.
- Main reason for downgrading confidence: heterogeneity, small at-risk subgroups, task impurity in preschool EF measures, and limited evidence for children with neurodevelopmental disorders other than ADHD.

## One-Sentence Summary

Preschool EF cognitive training shows a low-to-medium positive effect on EF task performance, including near and far EF transfer, but this source does not establish reliable generalization to learning or behavior outcomes.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Preschool EF Cognitive Training and Transfer

#### One-Sentence Definition

Preschool EF cognitive training is child-facing practice of working memory, inhibitory control, cognitive flexibility, planning, or problem solving tasks, with outcome interpretation limited to EF task performance unless non-EF transfer is separately demonstrated.

#### Known Facts

- The review focused on children aged 3-6 years.
- The final dataset included 27 papers, 32 studies, and 123 EF effect sizes.
- Included studies used cognitive training programs; the review excluded strictly physical exercise, drama, art activities, curriculum-based EF programs, mindfulness-based interventions, neurofeedback, parent training, and broader multimodal systems.
- Overall EF training effect was significant: `g = 0.342`, 95% CI `0.252 to 0.451`, `p < 0.001`.
- Heterogeneity was significant: `Q(122) = 172.340`, `p < 0.001`.
- Near EF transfer was significant: `g = 0.352`, 95% CI `0.252 to 0.451`, `p < 0.001`.
- Far EF transfer was significant: `g = 0.318`, 95% CI `0.186 to 0.449`, `p < 0.001`.
- Near vs far EF transfer difference was not significant: `p = 0.619`.
- Non-EF outcomes, including learning-related and behavior-related outcomes, were not significant overall: `g = 0.169`, 95% CI `-0.047 to 0.383`, `p = 0.122`.
- Developmental risk status moderated effect size: `p = 0.033`.
- No-risk samples showed significant EF effects: `g = 0.291`, 95% CI `0.192 to 0.390`.
- Low-SES samples showed significant EF effects: `g = 0.430`, 95% CI `0.219 to 0.641`.
- ADHD-symptom samples showed significant EF effects: `g = 0.785`, 95% CI `0.451 to 1.120`.
- The at-risk evidence was based on few studies: four studies with ADHD-symptom children and four studies with low-SES samples.
- Child age within the preschool range was not a significant moderator.
- Active vs passive control did not significantly moderate effects.
- Group training was associated with larger EF effects than individual training.
- Number of sessions was not a significant moderator, but total training length in minutes was significant.
- Computerized vs non-computerized training did not reach a significant moderator effect in the table, despite larger point estimates for non-computerized programs.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Preschool EF structure still relatively overlapping
-> child practices cognitive tasks loading WM / IC / flexibility / planning / problem solving
-> repeated task engagement improves trained EF process
-> because preschool EF components are less modularized, some transfer to untrained EF tasks may appear
-> this does not automatically transfer to learning, behavior, social adaptation, or everyday participation
```

#### Inferences

- This source supports a cautious child-facing EF training concept for preschoolers, especially when the goal is EF task performance.
- The positive far EF transfer signal should be interpreted as transfer among EF-related task domains, not as broad real-world generalization.
- For ADHD-symptom preschoolers, EF cognitive training is promising but should not be framed as replacement for diagnostic evaluation, behavioral intervention, parent training, school support, or medication decision-making when indicated.

#### Assumptions

- Training is delivered with age-appropriate engagement, fidelity, and adequate total practice time.
- Outcome measures validly capture EF constructs despite preschool task impurity.
- The child does not have unaddressed sleep, hearing, vision, language, anxiety, trauma, or broader developmental problems that explain the EF complaint.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Far EF transfer may be partly inflated by task impurity, because preschool EF tests often activate multiple processes.
- Some included studies had small samples, which may overestimate treatment effects.
- Unpublished studies had unusually large effects and were excluded from main analyses.
- Evidence for ADHD-symptom children and low-SES children is promising but small.
- This review did not find evidence for preschoolers with ASD, language impairment, or developmental coordination disorder.
- Long-term maintenance, ecological validity, and impact on school readiness, behavior, and participation remain uncertain.

## Clinically Useful Points

- EF cognitive training may be reasonable when the target is EF task practice in a 3-6-year-old child.
- It should not be sold as a broad treatment for learning, behavior, ADHD, ASD, or language disorder.
- If the complaint is everyday dysregulation or school participation, combine EF training evidence with differential diagnosis, caregiver coaching, sleep assessment, classroom scaffolding, and behavior plan evidence.

## Research-Useful Points

- Future studies need better separation of near EF transfer, far EF transfer, and non-EF ecological outcomes.
- At-risk preschooler subgroups need larger trials, especially ASD, language impairment, developmental coordination disorder, and complex adversity populations.
- Outcome sets should include follow-up, real-world function, and implementation fidelity.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- This source conflicts with a blanket claim that cognitive training has no far transfer in preschoolers; it reports significant far transfer among EF task domains.
- It also conflicts with a broad commercial claim that EF training improves school learning or behavior; non-EF transfer was not significant.
- It reinforces the existing wiki caution that [[Executive_Function_總論]] is not equivalent to ADHD and cannot be treated with one generic app.

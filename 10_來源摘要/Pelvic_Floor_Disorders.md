## One-Sentence Summary

Pelvic floor disorders are multisystem pelvic conditions in which pelvic floor muscle tone, pain, strength, coordination, visceral function, peripheral nerves, pregnancy / postpartum biomechanics, and chronic pain mechanisms must be evaluated together.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Pelvic floor disorders rehabilitation

#### One-Sentence Definition

Pelvic floor disorder rehabilitation uses pelvic floor, lumbopelvic, hip, neurologic, urologic, gastrointestinal, gynecologic, psychologic, and pain assessment to target pelvic pain, dyspareunia, urinary / fecal incontinence, urgency, constipation, and function.

#### Known Facts

- Pelvic floor disorders can occur in both sexes.
- Pelvic floor dysfunction categories include increased tone, pain, decreased tone, and coordination disorders.
- The chapter distinguishes pelvic floor disorders from pelvic floor muscle dysfunction.
- Musculoskeletal causes should be included in differential diagnosis for pelvic pain, urinary / fecal symptoms, urgency / frequency, and defecatory dysfunction.
- Chronic pelvic pain differs from acute pelvic pain because centralized pain mechanisms may be present.
- PFPT is described as first-line rehabilitation treatment for pelvic floor myofascial pain.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Pelvic floor muscle tone / pain / weakness / dyssynergia
+ visceral, neurologic, lumbopelvic, hip, pregnancy, trauma, and psychosocial contributors
-> pelvic pain, dyspareunia, urinary / fecal symptoms, constipation, avoidance
-> reduced sexual function, ADL, activity, mood, participation
-> consent-based exam + PFPT + interdisciplinary care
-> improved function and symptom control
```

#### Inferences

- A normal urologic or gynecologic workup does not exclude pelvic floor myofascial pain.
- PFPT should match high-tone versus low-tone versus coordination pattern rather than defaulting to generic Kegel strengthening.

#### Assumptions

- Patient consents to appropriate exam components and has access to pelvic floor physical therapy when indicated.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Some interventions such as vaginal diazepam and botulinum toxin have limited, mixed, or off-label evidence as presented in the source.
- Chronic pelvic pain requires broader evidence integration beyond this single chapter.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Internal pelvic floor exam requires clear explanation, consent, privacy, chaperone, and patient right to stop.
- Increased pelvic floor tone can coexist with weakness and impaired relaxation.
- Pelvic floor dyssynergia is paradoxical contraction or failure to relax when relaxation is functionally required.
- Pudendal and border nerve pain can have minimal positive exam findings; history is crucial.

## Research-Useful Points

- Pelvic floor disorders are better studied by mechanism categories than by symptom labels alone.
- Chronic pelvic pain research needs to distinguish local myofascial pain, neuropathic pain, visceral disease, and nociplastic / centralized mechanisms.

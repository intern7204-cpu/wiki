## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Osteoporosis rehabilitation and fracture prevention

#### One-Sentence Definition

Osteoporosis rehabilitation is a bone-health and fall-risk strategy that matches loading, posture, strengthening, orthotic support, and activity advice to BMD, fracture history, and spinal / hip fracture risk.

#### Known Facts

- WHO defines osteoporosis by BMD T-score of 2.5 SD or more below young adult mean.
- Osteopenia is described as T-score between -1 and -2.5; osteoporosis as T-score -2.5 or less; severe osteoporosis as osteoporosis plus fragility fracture.
- Bone remodeling normally replaces about 10% of bone tissue annually.
- Peak adult bone mass is achieved around ages 30 to 35.
- Osteoporotic fractures commonly involve thoracic / lumbar vertebrae, hip, and distal forearm.
- Moderate-intensity resistance training and impact exercise can decrease vertebral fracture risk in osteoporosis and osteopenia, but recommendations must be individualized.
- The source emphasizes back extensor strength and posture in vertebral fracture / kyphosis risk.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Low bone mass + microarchitectural compromise + fall or excessive spinal strain
-> fragility fracture, pain, kyphosis, fear, inactivity
-> further weakness, fall risk, bone loss
-> individualized loading + extensor / posture training + fall prevention + medical bone therapy
-> lower fracture risk and better function
```

#### Inferences

- Exercise prescription must specify safe direction, magnitude, frequency, and progression of load.
- Flexion-heavy spine exercise may be inappropriate in high vertebral fracture risk contexts.

#### Assumptions

- BMD, fracture history, pain status, fall risk, medication exposure, and secondary osteoporosis evaluation are available.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Pharmacologic recommendations are time-sensitive.
- Individual fracture risk needs current tools and clinical context beyond T-score alone.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Osteoporosis is "silent" until fracture, but rehabilitation should act before fracture when risk is high.
- Vertebral compression fracture can be subclinical; chronic kyphotic pain and posture change matter.
- Prolonged immobilization worsens bone loss.
- Secondary causes include endocrine, nutritional, renal, hepatic, inflammatory, malignancy, medication, immobility, and neurologic conditions.

## Research-Useful Points

- Back extensor training, posture intervention, fall prevention, and bone-specific loading are distinct outcomes from generic exercise tolerance.
- Osteoporosis trials should separate vertebral, hip, and nonvertebral fracture outcomes.

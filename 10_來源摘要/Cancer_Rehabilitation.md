## One-Sentence Summary

Cancer rehabilitation should be integrated across the oncology continuum to maintain or restore function, reduce symptom burden, manage treatment-related impairments, and protect safety in medically complex survivors.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Cancer rehabilitation continuum

#### One-Sentence Definition

Cancer rehabilitation is function-focused PM&R care across preventive, restorative, supportive, and palliative phases of cancer survivorship.

#### Known Facts

- Cancer survivors commonly develop functional impairments from cancer itself, surgery, chemotherapy, radiation therapy, endocrine therapy, targeted therapy, immunotherapy, and comorbid disease.
- Dietz's four-stage framework includes preventive, restorative, supportive, and palliative rehabilitation.
- Stage, prognosis, metastatic pattern, life expectancy, goals of care, symptom burden, and treatment toxicity influence rehabilitation goals.
- Common cancer-related impairments in the chapter include fatigue, cognitive impairment, lymphedema, chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy, pain, radiation fibrosis, and bone metastasis.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Cancer and cancer treatment
-> tissue injury, systemic toxicity, neurologic / musculoskeletal / cognitive impairment
-> reduced activity, participation, independence, and quality of life
-> phase-matched rehabilitation + oncology-informed precautions
-> safer functional recovery or support
```

#### Inferences

- A cancer rehabilitation plan should state the oncologic context, functional target, expected disease trajectory, and safety constraints together.
- Rehabilitation intensity should be matched to prognosis and medical stability, not only to diagnosis name.

#### Assumptions

- Oncology disease status, current treatment plan, blood counts, bone involvement, and goals of care are available or can be clarified with the oncology team.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- New systemic therapies and supportive care standards evolve quickly.
- This chapter supports rehabilitation framing but does not replace current oncology-specific treatment guidelines.

### Concept: Rehabilitation precautions in cancer

#### One-Sentence Definition

Cancer rehabilitation precautions are risk controls for bone instability, cytopenia, lymphedema, radiation fibrosis, cardiotoxicity, neuropathy, and systemic treatment toxicity.

#### Known Facts

- Bone metastasis can require medication, radiation, surgery, and rehabilitation with precautions.
- The chapter includes SINS for spinal instability and Mirels criteria for long-bone metastatic fracture risk.
- Platelet count affects exercise and modality safety.
- Lymphedema, CIPN, fatigue, and cognitive impairment can directly limit function and participation.

#### Inferences

- "Exercise helps cancer survivors" is incomplete unless framed by marrow reserve, bony integrity, cardiopulmonary reserve, neurologic toxicity, and infection / bleeding risk.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Cancer-related fatigue is common, underrecognized, and may persist months to years.
- Cancer-related cognitive impairment often affects executive function, concentration, memory, processing speed, and short-term memory.
- Lymphedema evaluation should include alternative causes of limb swelling.
- CIPN symptoms require differential diagnosis, not automatic attribution to chemotherapy.
- Bone metastasis needs fracture-risk and spinal-instability assessment before loading or manual intervention.

## Research-Useful Points

- Rehabilitation disparities across age, sex, geography, socioeconomic status, and insurance are highlighted as emerging cancer rehabilitation concerns.
- Long-term survivorship creates a need for phase-specific outcome measures beyond mortality.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- This source reinforces that palliative rehabilitation is active function-oriented care, not absence of rehabilitation.

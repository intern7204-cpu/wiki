## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Burn rehabilitation continuum

#### One-Sentence Definition

Burn rehabilitation is early-to-long-term interdisciplinary care that prevents contracture and complications while restoring mobility, ADL, exercise capacity, psychosocial function, and community participation.

#### Known Facts

- Burn severity is defined by burn size, depth, etiology, and associated injury.
- TBSA estimation guides fluid resuscitation, transfer decisions, prognosis, and complication risk.
- Burn depth includes superficial, superficial partial thickness, deep partial thickness, and full thickness.
- Certified burn-center transfer criteria include high-risk location, inhalation injury, TBSA thresholds, full-thickness burns, electrical burns, and chemical burns.
- Early physiatric involvement supports acute care, inpatient rehab candidacy, positioning, splinting, ROM, pain, and functional planning.
- Burn complications include hypermetabolism, dysphagia, cognitive concerns, pruritus, neuropathy, heterotopic ossification, amputation, hypertrophic scarring, and contracture.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Burn injury + wound inflammation + surgery / grafting + immobilization + hypermetabolism
-> pain, edema, scar, contracture, weakness, dysphagia, cognitive / psychological burden
-> ADL, mobility, work, community, body image, sexuality limitation
-> early positioning + ROM + splinting + pain control + nutrition + exercise + scar care
-> long-term function and participation
```

#### Inferences

- Burn rehab is a prevention workflow; waiting for mature scar misses the window to prevent contracture and functional loss.
- Pain, itch, anxiety, and cognition should be treated as barriers to rehabilitation participation, not side issues.

#### Assumptions

- Burn team provides surgical plan, graft status, weight-bearing / ROM restrictions, wound precautions, and medical stability parameters.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Some pharmacologic interventions for hypermetabolism, neuropathic pain, pruritus, and scar management have evolving evidence and local practice variation.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Electrical burns can produce deep tissue, nerve, muscle, vascular, ocular, rhabdomyolysis, and renal complications beyond small skin entry / exit wounds.
- Inhalation injury predicts mortality and affects pulmonary function, exercise tolerance, and dysphagia risk.
- Multimodal analgesia should combine pharmacologic and nonpharmacologic strategies.
- Early ambulation after selected lower-extremity grafting may be feasible with proper compression / splinting precautions.

## Research-Useful Points

- Burn outcomes should include ROM, scar, contracture, pain, itch, cognition, exercise capacity, body image, sexuality, return to work, and community reintegration.

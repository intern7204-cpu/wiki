## One-Sentence Summary

WHO CST Introduction defines CST as a family-centred, caregiver-mediated, stepped-care package for caregivers of children aged 2-9 years with developmental delays or disabilities, delivered through 9 group sessions, 3 home visits, trained non-specialist facilitators, and ongoing supervision.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: CST package architecture

#### One-Sentence Definition

CST package architecture is the overall service design that links a defined target population, 9 group sessions, 3 home visits, home practice, adult-learning methods, facilitator training, supervision, and referral pathways.

#### Known Facts

- CST is intended for caregivers of children aged 2-9 years with developmental delays or disabilities, especially social and communication delays or impairments.
- A formal diagnosis is not required before a child can be referred to CST.
- The standard format is 9 core group sessions and 3 home visits.
- Group sessions are usually 2.5-3 hours, delivered weekly or every two weeks.
- Home visits occur before group session 1, between sessions 5 and 6, and after the final group session.
- Home visits are usually 1.5-2 hours and usually occur in the caregiver's home, but can occur in a community setting when home delivery is infeasible.
- The package is designed to integrate into community-based health and social services.
- CST is positioned within stepped care: families should receive the most effective and least resource-intensive treatment before stepping up to specialist services when needed.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Developmental delay or disability is identified
-> caregiver can enter CST without waiting for formal diagnosis
-> group sessions teach and rehearse strategies
-> home visits individualize goals and coaching
-> caregivers practise strategies in daily routines
-> supervision and referral pathways handle fidelity, risk, and higher-need cases
```

#### Inferences

- CST is best understood as a structured service package, not a manual-only educational product.
- The 9+3 format matters because group teaching and individualized home coaching solve different implementation problems.
- The no-diagnosis entry criterion can reduce access delay, but it does not replace diagnostic evaluation when clinically indicated.

#### Assumptions

- Local systems can identify developmental delay or disability even when formal diagnosis is delayed.
- Community-based services have at least some pathway for referral, safeguarding, caregiver mental health, and specialist escalation.
- Caregivers can practise at least some routines at home if attendance barriers are addressed.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The Introduction does not quantify the independent contribution of each group session, home visit, or supervision component to child outcomes.
- Field-testing signals should not be overread as definitive effectiveness evidence.
- Local feasibility depends on workforce capacity, supervision quality, travel burden, safety, and referral resources.

### Concept: Trained non-specialist delivery with supervision

#### One-Sentence Definition

CST is designed for trained and supervised non-specialist facilitators, but non-specialist delivery requires staged training, field practice, debriefing, ongoing supervision, and access to a master trainer or supervisor.

#### Known Facts

- Facilitators may include community health workers, social service workers, nurses, early childhood education facilitators, teachers, experienced caregivers, or comparable community workers.
- Facilitators should be approachable, supportive with adults, able to facilitate groups, and able to provide supportive feedback and coaching.
- Initial facilitator training is staged: a 3-day classroom course for sessions 1-5, supervised field experience, a second 3-day classroom course for sessions 6-9, further field experience, advanced skills training, and additional supervised delivery.
- Debriefing after group sessions and home visits is part of training and field experience.
- Supervision is described as essential, and at minimum should occur after each home visit.
- Supervision should cover child developmental competencies, target skills, caregiver goals, additional family needs, home-visit coaching, group dynamics, facilitator workload, and facilitator self-care.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Non-specialist workforce is selected
-> classroom training introduces CST content and delivery skills
-> supervised co-delivery turns content knowledge into practice
-> debriefing and supervision correct delivery drift
-> facilitators can coach caregivers while recognizing risk and referral needs
```

#### Inferences

- Non-specialist delivery is a scalability strategy, not permission for unsupervised delivery.
- Supervision functions as both fidelity control and safety infrastructure.
- Facilitator workload and self-care are part of implementation quality, because overextended facilitators may lose coaching quality and risk detection.

#### Assumptions

- Supervisors have enough CST delivery experience to identify problems in group facilitation, home visits, goal-setting, and risk handling.
- Local programs allocate time for training, travel, documentation, debriefing, and supervision.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The Introduction does not define a universal minimum dose of supervision sufficient for every context.
- The optimal facilitator profile may vary by local service system, language, culture, and available workforce.

## Clinically Useful Points

- CST can be considered before formal diagnosis is completed when developmental delay or disability has been identified, but it should not be used to bypass needed developmental, medical, speech-language, behavioral, or safeguarding evaluation.
- A CST-like program should be checked for package fidelity: 9 core group sessions, 3 home visits, daily home practice, facilitator training, supervision, and referral pathways.
- Non-specialist delivery is clinically meaningful only when paired with training and supervision.
- Home visits are not optional social follow-up; they are part of the standard course architecture.
- Safeguarding, caregiver mental health, material deprivation, and child health needs require supervisor consultation and referral pathways.

## Research-Useful Points

- Implementation reports should separate package components: group sessions, home visits, facilitator training, supervision, adult-learning methods, home practice, and referral/safeguarding.
- Outcome interpretation should not attribute effects to CST if major structural components were removed without documentation and process evaluation.
- Field-testing evidence in the Introduction supports feasibility and acceptability more directly than definitive clinical efficacy.
- Research should specify whether CST was delivered in person, adapted for virtual delivery, shortened, or supplemented with additional modules.

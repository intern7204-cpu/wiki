## One-Sentence Summary

Exercise energy metabolism is best understood as simultaneous activation of PCr, glycolysis, and oxidative phosphorylation, with relative contribution determined mainly by exercise intensity and duration; carbohydrate is the dominant fuel for high-intensity performance, lactate is substrate/signaling molecule rather than waste, and metabolic interventions only help when matched to the limiting mechanism.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept 1: 三條 ATP resynthesis 路徑的動態協調

#### One-Sentence Definition

運動時 skeletal muscle 不是依序切換單一路徑，而是從 exercise onset 同時動員 PCr、glycolysis、oxidative phosphorylation，以不同 power–capacity profile 共同維持 ATP demand。

#### Known Facts

- Intramuscular ATP stores are small（約 5 mmol/kg wet muscle），無法長時間支撐 contraction。
- 900 W all-out exercise（約 300% VO2max）若只靠 stored ATP，估計只能維持 <2 秒。
- 約 200 W submaximal exercise（約 75% VO2max）若只靠 stored ATP，估計只能維持約 15 秒。
- ATP is required for Na+/K+ ATPase, SR Ca2+ ATPase, and myosin ATPase。
- ATP resynthesis includes substrate-level phosphorylation（PCr breakdown、myokinase、glycogen to lactate）and oxidative phosphorylation（carbohydrate and fat oxidation）。
- Anaerobic pathways have higher power but smaller capacity；aerobic pathways have lower power but much larger capacity。
- During intense exercise, PCr and anaerobic glycolysis activate within milliseconds to seconds；oxidative phosphorylation also activates but takes longer to fully contribute。
- In a 30-s all-out sprint, VO2 can reach 70–100% VO2max, and about 50% of the energy contribution in the last 5 s is aerobic。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Exercise onset → ATP hydrolysis rises abruptly → free ADP / AMP / Pi and Ca2+ signals increase → PCr and glycolysis provide rapid ATP bridge → oxidative phosphorylation ramps up → ATP content is defended despite >100-fold metabolic demand increase
```

#### Inferences

- “Aerobic vs anaerobic” should not be taught as mutually exclusive systems; they are simultaneous systems with different response times and capacities.
- Oxygen-independent ATP resynthesis is needed even when oxygen delivery is adequate because oxidative phosphorylation has onset kinetics.

#### Assumptions

- The source’s athlete-oriented examples are used as general exercise physiology principles, not as direct clinical exercise prescription thresholds.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Direct measurement of compartment-specific metabolites and proteins during human exercise remains limited.
- Whole-body measures cannot fully resolve intracellular compartment behavior.

### Concept 2: Exercise intensity and duration determine substrate selection

#### One-Sentence Definition

Exercise intensity and duration are the primary determinants of whether skeletal muscle relies more on PCr/glycolysis, carbohydrate oxidation, or fat oxidation, with high-intensity exercise becoming increasingly carbohydrate-dependent.

#### Known Facts

- Very intense seconds-long efforts rely heavily on PCr breakdown and glycogen to lactate。
- After exercise duration extends beyond approximately 1 minute（e.g., 800 m event），oxidative phosphorylation becomes the major ATP-generating pathway。
- Several-minute to several-hour events rely on oxidative metabolism of carbohydrate and fat。
- Marathon and triathlon events lasting 2–2.5 h still rely primarily on carbohydrate oxidation。
- Carbohydrate oxidation dominates at higher intensity；fat oxidation is more important at lower intensity。
- Maximal fat oxidation occurs around 60–65% VO2max。
- At ~80–100% VO2max, fuel use shifts toward carbohydrate and fat reliance decreases。
- Carbohydrate can support both aerobic and anaerobic ATP production and is activated rapidly during transitions.
- Fat has larger ATP-generating capacity but slower delivery / oxidation kinetics and functions more as a “helper fuel” during high-intensity work.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Higher power output → faster ATP turnover + faster transition demand → carbohydrate pathways can deliver ATP faster than fat oxidation → glycogen / glucose contribution rises → fat oxidation contribution falls despite large fat energy stores
```

#### Inferences

- “Fat adaptation” may preserve low- to moderate-intensity exercise capacity but is unlikely to replace carbohydrate for high-intensity endurance or stop-and-go sports.
- Training or nutrition claims should specify target intensity domain before claiming metabolic benefit.

#### Assumptions

- VO2max-relative intensity is an adequate organizing axis for this source summary, even though individual thresholds and critical power may classify domains more precisely.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- IMTG contribution varies with training status, sex, fiber-type distribution, and resting IMTG stores.
- The review notes that many studies historically involved male participants; sex-specific metabolism evidence remains incomplete.

### Concept 3: Dual-stage metabolic control

#### One-Sentence Definition

Exercise metabolism is regulated by a dual-stage model: feed-forward “gross control” from Ca2+ and epinephrine rapidly activates key enzymes, while feedback “fine-tuning” from ADP, AMP, Pi, substrate availability, and local factors matches ATP resynthesis to actual ATP demand.

#### Known Facts

- Contraction releases Ca2+ from SR; Ca2+ also activates metabolic enzymes.
- PCr reaction is near-equilibrium and responds rapidly to substrate/product changes。
- Glycogen phosphorylase is controlled by covalent and allosteric mechanisms and can increase flux within milliseconds。
- PDH can be activated by exercise regulators such as Ca2+ and pyruvate despite resting inhibitory signals such as acetyl-CoA / NADH。
- AMPK is a muscle energy charge sensor, but the source states that its major role in glucose uptake and fat oxidation during exercise appears overstated。
- AMPK may be more important for postexercise metabolic changes, insulin sensitivity, mitochondrial biogenesis, and GLUT4 expression。
- Phosphoproteomic studies have identified >1000 exercise-regulated phosphosites on >550 proteins。
- Co-administration of Ca2+ and β-adrenergic agonists can recapitulate much of the exercise / contraction phosphoproteomic signature in experimental systems。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Contraction signal → Ca2+ + epinephrine feed-forward activation → phosphorylase / PDH / lipolysis / TCA enzymes turn on → ADP / AMP / Pi / substrate levels feedback to enzyme flux → ATP resynthesis tracks ATP demand
```

#### Inferences

- A single “master regulator” explanation is too simple; exercise metabolism is redundant, distributed, and time-dependent.
- Acute phosphoproteomic or signaling changes should not be interpreted as direct proof of chronic training adaptation without longitudinal phenotype data.

#### Assumptions

- Molecular findings in cell / animal / acute human exercise studies are useful for mechanism hypotheses but require human exercise translation.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Many exercise-regulated phosphosites have unknown function.
- Integrated compartment-specific regulation remains difficult to measure noninvasively in humans.

### Concept 4: Lactate as substrate and signal, not waste

#### One-Sentence Definition

Lactate is produced during accelerated glycolysis but is not merely a waste product; it functions as oxidative substrate, gluconeogenic precursor, muscle glycogenesis substrate, and signaling molecule.

#### Known Facts

- Lactate accumulates in muscle and blood particularly at higher exercise intensities。
- The source states lactate is now recognized as substrate for oxidative metabolism, gluconeogenesis, muscle glycogenesis, and signaling in exercise adaptations / interorgan communication。
- Lactate can interact with broader exercise adaptation and interorgan communication pathways.
- Acidosis, not lactate itself, is the more relevant fatigue-related chemical stress in high-intensity exercise, although even H+ effects vary by experimental model.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
High glycolytic flux → pyruvate-to-lactate conversion increases → lactate exported / shuttled / oxidized or used for gluconeogenesis → lactate also participates in signaling → lactate reflects metabolic flux, not simply oxygen failure or waste accumulation
```

#### Inferences

- Lactate threshold / blood lactate interpretation should avoid the old “lactate = waste / dysoxia” framing.
- This source supports alignment with [[Poole_2020_anaerobic_threshold]] and the lactate shuttle framework.

#### Assumptions

- This summary treats lactate shuttle as a compatible framework, but detailed lactate shuttle mechanisms require dedicated Brooks / Poole sources.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The source is not a full lactate shuttle review; lactate signaling mechanisms require additional dedicated sources.

### Concept 5: Metabolic ergogenic interventions must match the limiting mechanism

#### One-Sentence Definition

Ergogenic interventions only make physiological sense when their target mechanism matches the event’s limiting factor, such as PCr availability, carbohydrate availability, buffering capacity, oxygen cost, central / peripheral fatigue, or thermal strain.

#### Known Facts

- Creatine monohydrate increases total muscle creatine by 20–25% and PCr by 10–15%, supporting repeated high-intensity exercise; long-term safety data are limited。
- Carbohydrate loading improves endurance capacity / performance in events longer than about 60–90 min。
- Carbohydrate ingestion during prolonged exercise maintains blood glucose and carbohydrate oxidation, delays fatigue, but does not prevent fatigue。
- High-fat / ketogenic approaches may maintain low-intensity capacity but do not support high-intensity performance well; elite race-walker data showed impaired economy / performance with high-fat adaptation。
- Ketone ester studies in trained male athletes from multiple laboratories do not support consistent performance increase, and one professional cyclist time trial study reported decreased performance。
- Caffeine ergogenic effects at lower doses may be mediated more through adenosine receptors in central and peripheral nervous systems than through fat oxidation.
- Nitrate / beetroot may reduce oxygen cost and improve performance, with less apparent effect in well-trained athletes.
- β-alanine increases muscle carnosine and can enhance high-intensity exercise performance.
- Bicarbonate / alkalosis can help high-intensity performance partly through buffering and ionic regulation.
- ROS are signaling molecules for adaptation, but high ROS can contribute to fatigue; antioxidant supplementation effects are equivocal and may attenuate adaptation in some reports.
- Hyperthermia impairs performance via central neural drive, cardiovascular function, muscle metabolism, and dehydration-related effects.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Event demand → limiting metabolic / neural / thermal factor → targeted intervention → benefit only if target matches limitation and dose/timing/population fit → otherwise neutral, harmful, or misleading result
```

#### Inferences

- Supplement claims should be mapped to event duration, intensity, training status, and limiting mechanism before clinical or performance recommendation.
- “More fat oxidation” is not automatically beneficial when the target event requires rapid carbohydrate-supported ATP production.

#### Assumptions

- Athletic performance evidence is not automatically clinical rehabilitation evidence.
- Ergogenic interventions require safety screening, contraindication review, and sport / medication context before use.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Ketone, nitrate, antioxidant, carnitine, and high-fat approaches have context-dependent or mixed evidence.
- Long-term safety data for some supplementation strategies remain incomplete.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Explain exercise intolerance by asking which energy system and time scale is failing, not by saying simply “weak” or “deconditioned.”
- For high-intensity or stop-and-go tasks, PCr recovery and carbohydrate availability matter more than fat oxidation rhetoric.
- Lactate should be framed as a metabolic intermediate and signal; do not call it a waste product.
- Exercise prescription and supplement discussion should specify intensity domain, duration, training status, and safety constraints.
- In rehabilitation, these mechanisms are explanatory, not standalone treatment rules; clinical prescription still needs disease-specific safety screening.

## Research-Useful Points

- Important measurement approaches include indirect calorimetry, muscle biopsy, tracer methods, arteriovenous sampling, MRS, omics, and systems biology.
- Major research gaps include compartment-specific regulation, human translation from in vitro / animal models, and repeated sampling of time courses in exercising humans.
- Sex differences and menstrual cycle effects require more high-quality work, especially in trained female participants at high aerobic and supramaximal intensities.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- Conflicts with simplistic “anaerobic first, aerobic later” teaching: all systems activate from exercise onset, but with different kinetics and relative contributions.
- Conflicts with “lactate is waste / lactate causes fatigue” framing: lactate is a substrate and signaling molecule; acidosis and ion changes are more relevant fatigue candidates.
- Conflicts with “fat is better for endurance because fat stores are larger” framing: high-intensity endurance depends heavily on carbohydrate because of ATP provision rate and efficiency.
- Conflicts with “AMPK controls exercise glucose uptake and fat oxidation” simplification: the source states this role appears overstated during exercise.

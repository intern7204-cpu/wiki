## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Geriatric rehabilitation and frailty

#### One-Sentence Definition

Geriatric rehabilitation is function-preserving care for older adults that explicitly accounts for sarcopenia, frailty, falls, cognition, polypharmacy, goals, and ethical decision making.

#### Known Facts

- The chapter states that by 2030, one in six people globally will be age 60 years or older.
- Sarcopenia is associated with falls, fractures, disability, and mortality.
- EWGSOP2 emphasizes muscle strength over muscle mass for sarcopenia case finding.
- SARC-F is described as a screening tool for sarcopenia-associated adverse outcomes.
- Falls in older adults are often multifactorial, including environmental, physical, mental, and pharmacologic factors.
- The Age-Friendly Health Systems framework uses 4Ms: Medication, Mentation, Mobility, and What Matters.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Aging physiology + comorbidity + inactivity + medication burden
-> sarcopenia, frailty, gait impairment, falls, delirium risk
-> ADL loss, hospitalization, institutionalization, mortality
-> screening + exercise/nutrition + medication review + mentation care + mobility planning
-> preserved independence and safer participation
```

#### Inferences

- Geriatric rehab assessment should not rely only on the primary diagnosis; it must screen reversible contributors to functional decline.
- Medication reconciliation is a functional intervention, not an administrative afterthought.

#### Assumptions

- The older adult's preinjury function, goals, cognition, caregiver support, and medication list are available for review.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Operational sarcopenia definitions continue to evolve.
- This chapter does not replace current falls prevention, osteoporosis, delirium, or medication guideline updates.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Hand grip strength, chair rise, gait speed, SPPB, and SARC-F are functionally meaningful in sarcopenia / frailty framing.
- Polypharmacy is associated with mortality, disability, hospitalization, falls, cognitive and functional impairment, adverse drug events, and poorer adherence.
- Delirium screening and prevention should be routine in acute and inpatient rehabilitation.
- Older adult gait disorders require evaluation of neurologic, musculoskeletal, cardiopulmonary, psychiatric, environmental, and medication contributors.

## Research-Useful Points

- Geriatric outcomes should track mobility, falls, cognition, medication burden, ADL, participation, and patient-defined goals.
- Age-friendly rehabilitation can be studied as an implementation framework rather than only as disease-specific therapy.

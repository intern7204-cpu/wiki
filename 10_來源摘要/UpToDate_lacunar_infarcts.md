## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Lacunar infarcts 診斷與病因邊界

#### One-Sentence Definition

Lacunar infarcts are small noncortical infarcts caused by occlusion of a single penetrating branch, but in vivo diagnosis requires compatible clinical syndrome, small noncortical imaging lesion, and exclusion of other ischemic stroke etiologies.

#### Known Facts

- Lacunar infarcts are small noncortical infarcts, traditionally 2-15 mm in diameter, caused by occlusion of a single penetrating branch of a large cerebral artery.
- Acute neuroimaging studies may use 20 mm or 25 mm as the upper size limit because acute lesions shrink over time.
- Not all small deep infarcts are lacunar infarcts.
- Most lacunes occur in basal ganglia, thalamus, subcortical white matter including internal capsule / corona radiata, and pons.
- Described mechanisms include hypertensive microangiopathy, branch atheromatous disease, embolism, and endothelial dysfunction / blood-brain barrier disruption.
- Hypertensive microangiopathy and branch atheromatous disease are pathologically proven; endothelial dysfunction theory remains unconfirmed in this source.
- Lacunar infarcts account for approximately 15-26 percent of ischemic strokes.
- Hypertension is the main risk factor/mechanism; diabetes, smoking, age, LDL cholesterol, hyperhomocysteinemia and selected genetic factors are possible associations.
- Heritability of small vessel ischemic stroke is estimated at 16-25 percent in this source.

#### Mechanism Chain

Penetrating arteries arise acutely from large cerebral arteries and have limited collateral supply  
-> chronic hypertension / arteriolosclerosis or branch atheroma narrows or occludes the perforator  
-> small deep noncortical infarct in internal capsule, basal ganglia, thalamus, pons or corona radiata  
-> lacunar syndrome without cortical signs  
-> mechanism still requires evaluation because embolism, parent artery disease or other causes may mimic the presentation.

#### Inferences

- "Lacunar-looking" should be documented as a working mechanism until imaging and competing-mechanism evaluation support it.
- Parent artery atheroma is especially important because it can make a small deep infarct look lacunar while the operating mechanism is large artery disease at a perforator origin.

#### Assumptions

- Adult ischemic stroke / TIA context.
- Brain CT/MRI, vascular imaging, cardiac monitoring and echocardiography are clinically available.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Imaging cannot directly visualize occlusion of most small penetrating arteries.
- The exact causal meaning of carotid stenosis or cardiac sources in some radiologic lacunes is uncertain.
- Whether embolic or parent-artery lacunar infarcts have different prognosis or treatment response remains unknown.

### Concept: Lacunar stroke syndromes and imaging confirmation

#### One-Sentence Definition

Lacunar stroke syndromes are acute subcortical neurologic patterns without cortical signs; the five classic syndromes raise suspicion but need imaging correlation and standard stroke workup.

#### Known Facts

- Five classic lacunar syndromes are pure motor hemiparesis, pure sensory stroke, ataxic hemiparesis, sensorimotor stroke, and dysarthria-clumsy hand syndrome.
- Lacunar syndromes generally lack aphasia, hemianopia, agnosia, neglect and apraxia.
- Monoplegia, stupor, coma, loss of consciousness and seizures are typically absent.
- Symptoms usually develop over minutes to hours; stuttering course and evolution over days can occur.
- Lacunar infarction is the main ischemic stroke subtype associated with worsening motor deficits after hospital admission in this source.
- Hyperacute clinical lacunar syndrome recognition may have low positive predictive value; one cited study within six hours reported 30 percent positive predictive value by CT.
- CT has low sensitivity for small acute lacunar infarcts, about 30-44 percent in prospective studies, and likely lower within six hours.
- DWI MRI is more sensitive for acute lesions, differentiates acute from chronic lacunes, and can reveal multiple acute lesions suggesting embolic mechanism.
- One study cited by the source found 21 percent of clinically identified lacunar syndromes had acute nonlacunar infarcts.
- Another DWI study found radiologic patterns suggestive of nonlacunar/embolic mechanism in 41 percent of patients with lacunar syndromes.

#### Mechanism Chain

Small deep lesion location  
-> interruption of compact motor, sensory, corticopontocerebellar or corticobulbar pathways  
-> focal motor/sensory/ataxic/dysarthric syndrome without cortical signs  
-> clinical syndrome suggests but does not prove lacunar infarct  
-> DWI MRI and vascular/cardiac evaluation determine whether the final mechanism is small vessel, parent artery, embolic or other.

#### Inferences

- DWI MRI is especially useful when CT is negative, lesions are multiple, or old lacunes make clinical attribution difficult.
- Presence of cortical signs should push the differential away from classic lacunar syndrome and toward cortical or larger-territory mechanisms.

#### Assumptions

- The bedside exam can reliably assess cortical signs, motor/sensory deficits and ataxia.
- The symptom onset and course can be reconstructed.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Atypical lacunar syndromes exist but have not been studied in large clinical series in this source.
- Conventional MRI and CT may confuse acute and chronic lacunar lesions without DWI.

### Concept: Lacunar infarct treatment and prognosis caveats

#### One-Sentence Definition

Lacunar infarcts follow general acute ischemic stroke evaluation and treatment principles, but long-term prevention should avoid indefinite aspirin plus clopidogrel unless another indication exists.

#### Known Facts

- All acute ischemic stroke patients, including suspected lacunar stroke, should be screened for intravenous thrombolysis and mechanical thrombectomy eligibility.
- Confirmed lacunar infarction is not a mechanical thrombectomy target because it lacks an amenable large artery occlusion; however, acute vascular imaging is warranted because small deep infarcts are not always lacunar.
- Standard evaluation includes history and physical, brain CT/MRI, neurovascular imaging with CTA/MRA, cardiac monitoring and echocardiography.
- Intravenous thrombolysis is selected the same way as other ischemic stroke subtypes; a WAKE-UP trial post hoc analysis found similar functional benefit in lacunar and nonlacunar stroke subtypes.
- Antiplatelet therapy is usually started after TIA/ischemic stroke confirmation unless contraindicated; antithrombotics are withheld for 24 hours after IV thrombolysis.
- Secondary prevention includes BP control, antiplatelet therapy, statin therapy and lifestyle modification.
- Beyond the acute phase (>21 days), long-term single-agent antiplatelet therapy is used when there is no indication for anticoagulation.
- A 2015 pooled analysis cited by the source found single antiplatelet therapy reduced ischemic stroke recurrence in lacunar stroke subgroup.
- SPS3 suggested long-term aspirin plus clopidogrel is harmful in lacunar stroke because hemorrhage and death increased without reducing recurrent stroke.
- Lacunar infarcts generally have better short-term prognosis up to one year, but long-term prognosis may not differ greatly from nonlacunar stroke.

#### Mechanism Chain

Acute lacunar-appearing stroke  
-> still enters standard acute ischemic stroke pathway  
-> reperfusion screen and vascular imaging exclude treatable large artery occlusion / alternative mechanism  
-> after acute phase, prevention targets BP, antiplatelet, statin and lifestyle  
-> long-term DAPT exposes small-vessel patients to bleeding without proven recurrence reduction  
-> single antiplatelet plus risk-factor control becomes default if no anticoagulation indication exists.

#### Inferences

- "Lacunar" should not be used to deny IV thrombolysis evaluation when the patient otherwise meets ischemic stroke criteria.
- Long-term DAPT in lacunar stroke should require an indication outside lacunar stroke itself.

#### Assumptions

- The patient has ischemic stroke / TIA rather than hemorrhage or mimic.
- Contraindications to antithrombotic therapy have been assessed.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Silent lacunar infarcts may warrant intensive risk-factor management, but antiplatelet risk/benefit is not adequately studied.
- Prognosis may differ by true underlying mechanism, but this remains unresolved in the source.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Do not diagnose lacunar infarct from syndrome alone.
- Do not diagnose lacunar infarct from "small deep infarct" alone.
- Acute vascular imaging remains appropriate because parent artery occlusive disease, large artery occlusion and embolic patterns can mimic lacunar infarction.
- DWI MRI is preferred when CT is nondiagnostic or acute/chronic lesion attribution is unclear.
- Long-term aspirin plus clopidogrel is not a lacunar stroke prevention strategy.

## Research-Useful Points

- Lacunar infarct definitions vary by chronic pathology size (2-15 mm) versus acute DWI size (20-25 mm).
- Clinical syndrome, imaging pattern and final etiologic mechanism should be separately coded.
- Parent artery atheroma and embolism are important competing mechanisms in small deep infarcts.

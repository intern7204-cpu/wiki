## One-Sentence Summary

此 UpToDate topic 將 ischemic stroke / TIA secondary prevention 建構為多軸風險控制：antithrombotic therapy、BP lowering、LDL-C lowering、diabetes control、lifestyle modification 與 selected mechanism-specific interventions。

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Ischemic Stroke Secondary Prevention Bundle

#### One-Sentence Definition

Ischemic stroke secondary prevention bundle 是在 ischemic stroke 或 TIA 後，同步處理 recurrent ischemia 的 mechanism 與可修正 vascular risk factors，而不是只選一種 antithrombotic drug。

#### Known Facts

- Most patients with ischemic stroke or TIA should receive all available risk reduction strategies.
- Major treatable risk factors include hypertension、dyslipidemia、diabetes、smoking、physical inactivity。
- Secondary prevention strategies include antithrombotic therapy、blood pressure reduction、LDL-C lowering therapy、lifestyle modification。
- UpToDate states that treating all major stroke risk factors, compared with no treatment, has been estimated to reduce recurrent stroke risk by 80 percent.
- Mechanism-specific interventions include symptomatic internal carotid artery stenosis revascularization and PFO closure in selected patients with embolic-appearing stroke, PFO, and no other apparent cause.
- A multidisciplinary, team-based approach should tailor treatment to the individual patient and address social determinants of health.

#### Mechanism Chain

1. Prior ischemic stroke / TIA identifies a patient with high baseline vascular risk.
2. Recurrent stroke risk is driven by both stroke mechanism and systemic vascular risk factors.
3. Antithrombotic therapy reduces thromboembolic recurrence, but the correct class depends on mechanism.
4. BP lowering, LDL-C lowering, diabetes management, smoking cessation, exercise, diet, alcohol reduction, and weight management reduce the background vascular substrate.
5. Selected anatomic or embolic mechanisms require targeted intervention beyond general vascular risk reduction.

#### Inferences

- A secondary prevention checklist should be mechanism-based plus risk-factor-based; either alone is incomplete.
- PM&R / rehabilitation settings should not defer all secondary prevention to neurology follow-up if BP, statin intensity, antithrombotic class, smoking, diabetes, activity, or obesity plans are unresolved.

#### Assumptions

- The topic review assumes a patient has survived ischemic stroke / TIA and is being managed beyond immediate reperfusion triage.
- It assumes patient-specific contraindications, bleeding risk, frailty, goals of care, renal function, medication tolerance, and stroke mechanism are checked before applying drug targets.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- This is not a primary guideline; recommendation grading is mostly inherited from cited guidelines and studies.
- Some mechanism-specific details are deliberately referred to other UpToDate topics.
- BP goals may require individualization in severe frailty, postural hypotension, institutionalized patients, or medication intolerance.
- Lifestyle interventions are recommended, but direct evidence for recurrent stroke reduction is variable by component.

### Concept: Antithrombotic Selection by Stroke Mechanism

#### One-Sentence Definition

Antithrombotic selection after ischemic stroke / TIA depends on whether the likely recurrence mechanism is noncardioembolic, cardioembolic, dissection-related, or otherwise mechanism-specific.

#### Known Facts

- Antithrombotic therapy is important for nearly all patients with ischemic stroke or TIA.
- For noncardioembolic ischemic stroke or TIA, acceptable long-term options include aspirin 50-100 mg daily, clopidogrel 75 mg daily, or aspirin-extended-release dipyridamole 25/200 mg twice daily.
- Early, short-term DAPT, typically aspirin plus clopidogrel, is beneficial for select patients with high-risk TIA or minor ischemic stroke.
- DAPT may also be beneficial for recently symptomatic intracranial large artery atherosclerosis.
- For chronic nonvalvular atrial fibrillation after ischemic stroke or TIA, long-term anticoagulation with warfarin or a direct oral anticoagulant is recommended; benefit outweighs major bleeding risk in most patients.
- For cervical artery dissection with ischemic neurologic symptoms, antithrombotic therapy is indicated, but antiplatelet versus anticoagulant choice remains controversial.

#### Inferences

- Antiplatelet defaulting without stroke mechanism review risks undertreating cardioembolic stroke.
- Long-term DAPT should not be treated as the default simply because short-term DAPT benefits selected early minor stroke / high-risk TIA patients.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- This overview does not fully specify all DAPT duration / eligibility details; it links to separate early antithrombotic treatment topics.
- It does not resolve antiplatelet versus anticoagulation for cervical artery dissection.

### Concept: Risk Factor Targets After Ischemic Stroke / TIA

#### One-Sentence Definition

Risk factor targets after ischemic stroke / TIA are the modifiable vascular targets that reduce recurrent stroke and vascular event risk, including BP, LDL-C, glucose, tobacco, activity, diet, alcohol, and weight.

#### Known Facts

- Hypertension is described as the single most important treatable risk factor for stroke.
- For most patients with ischemic stroke or TIA, the 2021 AHA/ASA guideline target cited by the source is office BP <130/80 mmHg to reduce recurrent stroke and vascular events.
- Acute ischemic stroke is different: during the first hours and days, BP should not be lowered too quickly because elevated BP may help maintain perfusion through collateral circulation.
- Statins are the best studied lipid-lowering therapy for reducing recurrent ischemic stroke.
- For patients with ASCVD including TIA or ischemic stroke, UpToDate treats with high-intensity statin therapy such as atorvastatin 80 mg/day independent of baseline LDL-C, aiming for LDL-C <70 mg/dL.
- If LDL-C remains >=70 mg/dL despite maximally tolerated statin therapy, adding ezetimibe or PCSK9 inhibitor is reasonable.
- For most patients with diabetes, a reasonable A1C goal is <=7 percent.
- Patients capable of physical activity should perform moderate-intensity exercise at least 10 minutes four times weekly or vigorous-intensity exercise at least 20 minutes twice weekly.
- Lifestyle modification includes smoking cessation, regular aerobic physical activity, salt restriction, Mediterranean diet, limited alcohol consumption, and weight control.
- Weight reduction after stroke has indirect benefit through BP, glucose, and lipid control, while direct recurrent stroke reduction evidence is not established.
- Homocysteine-lowering vitamins are not beneficial for secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease or stroke; routine homocysteine / vitamin screening is not recommended.

#### Mechanism Chain

1. Hypertension, dyslipidemia, diabetes, tobacco, inactivity, unhealthy diet, heavy alcohol use, and obesity maintain atherosclerotic and thrombotic risk.
2. Lowering BP and LDL-C reduces vascular event recurrence risk through vascular substrate modification.
3. Diabetes, tobacco, activity, diet, alcohol, and weight interventions reduce overlapping cardiometabolic and vascular stressors.
4. The bundle effect matters more than any single risk-factor intervention alone.

#### Inferences

- Acute stroke BP management and long-term secondary prevention BP targets should be explicitly separated in orders and teaching.
- A statin prescription without LDL-C follow-up and escalation plan is an incomplete lipid-lowering strategy.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Direct recurrent-stroke benefit of weight reduction is not established in this source.
- Sleep apnea is associated with stroke risk, but this overview refers detailed management to another topic.
- Lp(a) is a modest independent risk factor, but evidence that lowering Lp(a) reduces ASCVD risk is limited.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Every ischemic stroke / TIA patient should leave acute care or rehab with a documented secondary prevention bundle, not only a discharge antithrombotic.
- Minimum checklist from this source: stroke mechanism, antithrombotic class, BP phase and target, LDL-C plan, diabetes target if applicable, smoking, activity, diet/salt, alcohol/substance use, obesity, selected mechanism-specific procedures.
- For rehab clinicians, the high-yield distinction is acute permissive hypertension versus long-term BP <130/80 mmHg for most patients.

## Research-Useful Points

- Comparative effectiveness work should evaluate bundle completion and adherence rather than single risk-factor prescriptions alone.
- Implementation research should include social determinants of health: literacy, language proficiency, medication affordability, food insecurity, housing needs, and transportation barriers.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- 與「stroke secondary prevention = antiplatelet」衝突。
- 與「所有 ischemic stroke 長期都用 DAPT」衝突。
- 與「acute stroke BP 高就立刻壓到長期目標」衝突。
- 與「statin 開了就不用追 LDL-C」衝突。
- 與「obesity treatment 已直接證明降低 recurrent stroke」衝突；本來源只支持其間接 cardiometabolic benefit。

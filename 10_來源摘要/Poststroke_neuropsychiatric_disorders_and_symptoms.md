# Source Summary: Poststroke neuropsychiatric disorders and symptoms

## Source Type

UpToDate topic review. Literature review current through 2026-04；topic last updated 2025-11-24。

## Reliability Level

Tier 6：expert topic review。可作臨床辨識、轉介與初步 management frame，但若要建立 guideline-grade treatment recommendation，仍需回到 AHA/ASA statement、psychiatry guideline 或 disorder-specific guideline。

## One-Sentence Summary

Poststroke psychiatric and neurobehavioral symptoms are common, under-recognized, and clinically important because they reduce rehabilitation engagement, self-care, adherence, participation and caregiver safety; evaluation should focus on functional impairment, safety risk and disorder-specific differential rather than labeling all symptoms as poor motivation or normal grief.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Poststroke neuropsychiatric surveillance and referral

#### One-Sentence Definition

Poststroke neuropsychiatric surveillance is the structured evaluation of mood, motivation, anxiety, trauma symptoms, emotional expression and unsafe behavior after stroke, with referral triggered by functional impairment, diagnostic uncertainty or safety risk.

#### Known Facts

- Source states psychiatric disorders after ischemic and hemorrhagic stroke are common but under-recognized.
- Referral is indicated when poststroke psychiatric symptoms cause functional impairment.
- Other referral indications include lack of response to initial treatment, suicidal ideation or behavior, psychotic features, impulsive/dangerous/aggressive behavior, need for adjunctive psychotherapy, poor judgment with imminent risk, and patient interest in referral.
- Cognitive screening may be warranted because cognitive disorder can mimic or coexist with depressive symptoms.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke
-> neural injury + neuroinflammation + disrupted networks + disability + identity / role loss + caregiver / financial stress
-> mood, motivation, anxiety, trauma, emotional-expression or behavioral syndrome
-> reduced rehabilitation engagement + lower self-care + poorer adherence + caregiver burden / safety risk
-> worse recovery, participation and quality of life
```

#### Inferences

- Rehabilitation readiness should include neuropsychiatric surveillance, not only motor, swallowing and cardiopulmonary stability.
- Therapy refusal, low initiation or irritability should trigger differential diagnosis rather than moral labeling.

#### Assumptions

- Adult poststroke population.
- Clinicians have access to mental health referral pathways and basic screening tools.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- This source is a topic review, not a stroke rehabilitation guideline.
- Disorder-specific treatment evidence varies; apathy, PTSD, PBA and PSAA have weaker evidence than poststroke depression.

### Concept: Poststroke depression

#### One-Sentence Definition

Poststroke depression is a depressive disorder after stroke in which persistent depressed mood or anhedonia plus functional impairment must be separated from overlapping stroke sequelae.

#### Known Facts

- Depression is described as the most common poststroke neuropsychiatric disorder.
- Reported prevalence ranges approximately 25 to 50 percent across studies, with methodologic variation.
- PHQ-2 and PHQ-9 are commonly used in non-aphasic patients; PHQ-2 score of 3 or higher should lead to PHQ-9 evaluation.
- ADRS has been validated for aphasic patients through behavioral observation.
- Positive depression screen should prompt assessment for suicidal ideation and behavior.
- Prophylactic antidepressant or psychotherapy in the first week may reduce depression incidence in selected patients but is not standard care.
- SSRIs require caution in hemorrhagic stroke or concurrent antiplatelet context because of bleeding concerns; fluoxetine has been associated with fracture risk in some analyses.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke-related brain injury + disability / social stressors
-> depressive syndrome
-> lower rehabilitation engagement and health-care adherence
-> poorer ADL and functional outcome
```

#### Inferences

- Depression screening scores should be interpreted item-by-item because fatigue, sleep change, appetite change and slowed behavior may be stroke sequelae.

#### Assumptions

- DSM-5-TR / ICD-11 frameworks are applied in clinical context, not as mechanical checklist-only diagnosis.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Lesion location association with depression is mixed.
- Comparative efficacy among antidepressant choices is not well established by head-to-head poststroke trials.

### Concept: Apathy versus depression

#### One-Sentence Definition

Poststroke apathy is reduced motivation, initiation or goal-directed activity without the negative self-evaluative symptoms that define depression.

#### Known Facts

- Apathy may appear as early as four days after stroke and has been reported in up to 22 percent in observational studies.
- Apathy can affect behavior, cognition, emotion and social engagement.
- Apathy may coexist with depression but is not equivalent to depression.
- There is no proven therapy for poststroke apathy; high-quality treatment evidence is lacking.
- Apathy is associated with functional impairment, slower recovery, caregiver burden and cognitive impairment in the source.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke-related network injury
-> reduced initiation / goal-directed activity
-> needs prompting for ADL and therapy participation
-> slower recovery + caregiver burden
```

#### Inferences

- Low initiation after stroke should be treated as a clinical signal, not automatically as nonadherence.

#### Assumptions

- Motor, sensory, hearing, vision, consciousness, medication and environmental causes of low activity are checked before diagnosing apathy.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Actual prevalence and long-term course remain uncertain because data are limited.

### Concept: Poststroke anxiety, ASD and PTSD

#### One-Sentence Definition

Poststroke anxiety and trauma-related disorders are persistent fear, worry, avoidance, hypervigilance or trauma symptoms after stroke that can reduce participation, adherence and return to previous roles.

#### Known Facts

- Poststroke anxiety can affect approximately 25 to 33 percent of stroke and TIA patients.
- Source lists GAD-7, Geriatric Anxiety Inventory and HADS as screening tools, with caveats about cognitive load and stroke symptom overlap.
- Acute stress disorder occurs within days to weeks after the event; PTSD is diagnosed when symptoms persist beyond one month.
- A 2024 systematic review cited by the source reported PTSD prevalence after stroke of 17.5 percent, higher in SAH survivors than ischemic stroke survivors.
- PTSD is linked to poorer quality of life, lower return to prior employment and medication nonadherence in the source.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke as life-threatening event + fear of recurrence / falling + somatic hypervigilance
-> anxiety, avoidance, PTSD symptoms
-> reduced community exposure + lower adherence + poorer participation
```

#### Inferences

- Recurrent reassurance-seeking for benign somatic symptoms may represent trauma-related hypervigilance, but recurrent stroke red flags still require medical triage.

#### Assumptions

- Assessment separates new neurologic symptoms from anxiety-driven somatic vigilance.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Poststroke anxiety treatment evidence is limited; CBT and SSRI data exist but studies may be small or heterogeneous.

### Concept: Pseudobulbar affect

#### One-Sentence Definition

Pseudobulbar affect is involuntary or inappropriate crying or laughing after neurologic injury, where outward affect is exaggerated or incongruent with internal mood.

#### Known Facts

- A 2016 systematic review and meta-analysis cited by the source estimated PBA prevalence after stroke at 17 percent.
- PBA is more common in acute and post-acute phases and may decline over longer follow-up.
- PBA is hypothesized to involve disruption of cortico-pontine-cerebellar modulation of emotional motor expression.
- PBA can be misdiagnosed as depression because both may involve crying.
- Dextromethorphan-quinidine is identified as the only FDA-approved PBA treatment; SSRI and TCA evidence exists but certainty is limited.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke lesion affecting emotion-expression control circuits
-> impaired modulation of crying / laughing motor output
-> sudden or disproportionate episodes despite different internal mood
-> embarrassment + social withdrawal
```

#### Inferences

- PBA screening questions should ask episode onset, proportionality, suppressibility, trigger and internal mood, not only "are you sad?"

#### Assumptions

- Seizure, dystonia, tics, dyskinesia, mania, depression, panic, psychosis and medication effects are considered when presentation is atypical.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Long-term outcomes and treatment effect size in stroke-specific PBA remain incompletely defined.

### Concept: Poststroke anger or aggression

#### One-Sentence Definition

Poststroke anger or aggression is persistent irritability, disinhibition, reduced frustration tolerance or verbal/physical aggression after stroke, distinct from transient delirium-related behavior.

#### Known Facts

- PSAA has been reported in 11 to 35 percent of acute/subacute stroke patients.
- Acute/subacute aggressive behavior may occur due to delirium and may resolve with delirium treatment.
- Persistent PSAA can inhibit rehabilitation and strain relationships, employment and caregiver safety.
- Evidence for PSAA treatment is limited; source mentions nortriptyline, fluoxetine and escitalopram, while atypical neuroleptics have cardiovascular side-effect concerns.
- Source recommends attention to caregiver safety, including weapons at home when safety concern exists.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke + disability + cognitive / frontal-behavioral change
-> irritability, disinhibition or aggression
-> therapy disruption + caregiver risk
-> need for safety assessment and mental health / behavioral management
```

#### Inferences

- Acute aggression after stroke should first trigger delirium and medical-trigger assessment before diagnosing persistent PSAA.

#### Assumptions

- Local practice has procedures for imminent harm, domestic violence or weapon safety concerns.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Robust prospective treatment trials for PSAA are lacking.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Functional impairment is the key threshold: symptoms matter clinically when they disrupt self-care, therapy engagement, social participation, responsibilities or safety.
- Positive depression screen should include suicidality review, preferably not only in front of family members.
- PHQ-2 / PHQ-9 / GAD-7 / HADS can support screening, but total scores can be inflated by stroke sequelae.
- Aphasia changes depression assessment; ADRS may be more appropriate than self-report alone.
- PBA crying is not the same as depression; internal mood and episode pattern matter.
- Apathy is not laziness; it is a differential diagnosis for reduced initiation.
- Severe aggression, dangerous behavior, psychosis, suicidal ideation, poor judgment with imminent risk and lack of treatment response should trigger mental health referral.

## Research-Useful Points

- Outcome studies should separate depression, apathy, anxiety, PTSD, PBA and PSAA rather than pooling them as generic mood symptoms.
- Studies should report timing after stroke, stroke subtype, aphasia/cognitive impairment, screening instrument and whether diagnosis required functional impairment.
- Treatment evidence is disorder-specific; depression has more mature evidence than apathy, PTSD, PBA and PSAA.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- Conflicts with the simplistic assumption that low therapy participation means poor motivation.
- Conflicts with treating poststroke crying as depression without considering PBA.
- Conflicts with using depression/anxiety questionnaire total scores mechanically despite overlap with fatigue, sleep change, concentration problems and neurologic disability.
- Conflicts with routine prophylactic SSRI for all acute stroke patients; the source describes selected use only and warns about bleeding / fracture concerns.

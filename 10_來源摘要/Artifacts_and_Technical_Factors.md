# Source Summary: Artifacts and Technical Factors

## Source Type

- Textbook chapter on EDX artifacts and technical factors in NCS / needle EMG.
- Single-source ingest only; this page does not synthesize with separate chapters on anomalous innervation, routine nerve-specific protocols, or disease-specific EDX criteria.

## Reliability Level

- Tier 1 within this wiki's existing convention for textbook clinical chapters.
- Strongest for technical validity, artifact recognition, temperature correction, stimulation / recording setup, and false-positive / false-negative prevention.
- Not a disease-specific diagnostic guideline; abnormal patterns still require clinical-electrophysiologic correlation.

## One-Sentence Summary

EDX interpretation is only as valid as its data acquisition: temperature, age, height, impedance, filters, stimulation adequacy, co-stimulation, electrode placement, distance measurement, and display settings can all create false neuropathy, false entrapment, false axonal loss, false demyelination, or false conduction block.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: EDX 技術假象與品質控制

#### One-Sentence Definition

EDX technical quality control is the systematic recognition and correction of physiologic and nonphysiologic factors that distort NCS / EMG waveforms before assigning a physiologic diagnosis.

#### Known Facts

- EDX data value depends on collecting data correctly and interpreting data correctly.
- If collected data are technically inaccurate, correct interpretation cannot occur later.
- EDX signals are small bioelectric signals in the microvolt and millivolt range.
- Failure to recognize technical factors can cause type I errors and type II errors; the chapter stresses type I errors as especially serious because they can mislabel patients and lead to inappropriate testing or treatment.
- Physiologic factors listed in the source include temperature, age, height, proximal versus distal nerve segments, and anomalous innervations.
- Nonphysiologic factors include electrode impedance mismatch / 60-Hz interference, filters, averaging, stimulus artifact, cathode position, supramaximal stimulation, co-stimulation, electrode placement, antidromic versus orthodromic recording, recording-electrode distance from nerve, active-reference distance, limb position / distance measurement, and sweep speed / sensitivity.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Small EDX signal
→ physiologic or technical distortion
→ altered latency / velocity / amplitude / duration / morphology
→ false axonal loss, demyelination, entrapment, conduction block, or normal study
→ misdiagnosis unless technical factor is recognized and corrected
```

#### Inferences

- Technical quality is not a clerical detail; it is part of the diagnostic reasoning chain.
- A waveform pattern that does not fit clinical localization should trigger technical review before diagnostic escalation.

#### Assumptions

- The EDX lab can measure limb temperature, adjust stimulation / recording setup, and repeat or reposition studies when needed.
- Operator understands both electrophysiology and volume conduction artifacts.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Some technical distortion cannot be fully corrected, especially in portable ICU studies, edema, severe disease, or extreme anatomic variation.

### Concept: Temperature and physiologic modifiers

#### One-Sentence Definition

Temperature, age, height, and limb segment differences change NCS / EMG parameters and must be incorporated before assigning disease physiology.

#### Known Facts

- Temperature is described as the most important physiologic factor.
- Cooler temperature slows conduction velocity, prolongs distal latency, and increases amplitude / duration of CMAP / SNAP; SNAP is affected more than CMAP.
- Within approximately 21-34°C, conduction velocity slows about 1.5-2.5 m/s for every 1°C drop, and distal latency prolongs about 0.2 ms per degree.
- Cool limb can mimic polyneuropathy, distal entrapment neuropathy, or demyelinating-range slowing in axonal neuropathy.
- Distal limb temperature should be recorded and ideally maintained between 32 and 34°C.
- With profound cooling, underlying nerve temperature may need 20-40 minutes to equilibrate despite skin temperature changes.
- If warming is not possible, correction factors can help, but the source prefers warming / rewarming because correction factors are derived mainly from normal nerves.
- Full-term infant conduction velocity around 25-30 m/s can be normal at birth but would be demyelinating range in adults.
- Conduction velocity reaches about 75% of adult values by age 1 year and adult range by age 3-5 years.
- In adults older than 60, conduction velocity decreases approximately 0.5-4.0 m/s per decade.
- SNAP amplitude decreases substantially with advanced age; absent or very low distal lower-extremity sensory responses in older adults need caution.
- Taller individuals tend to have slower conduction velocities, partly due to longer / tapered nerves and distal cooling.
- Lower extremity velocities are normally slower than upper extremity velocities.
- Late responses need height or limb-length based normal values.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Cooler / older / taller / distal limb context
→ altered channel kinetics, myelin / axon population, nerve length, or limb temperature
→ slower velocity, longer latency, altered amplitude / duration
→ disease mimic if interpreted with inappropriate norms
```

#### Inferences

- A high-amplitude, long-duration sensory response with slow conduction velocity should raise temperature artifact suspicion before demyelination is diagnosed.
- Pediatric and geriatric interpretation cannot use a single adult normal table without context.

#### Assumptions

- Temperature and age / height norms are available or can be accounted for explicitly in the report.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Temperature correction values may not hold for all diseased nerves.

### Concept: Stimulation / recording artifacts

#### One-Sentence Definition

Stimulation and recording setup can directly create false latency, amplitude, conduction velocity, and conduction block patterns.

#### Known Facts

- Electrode impedance mismatch can allow 60-Hz interference to obscure small SNAPs or fibrillation potentials.
- Matching active and reference electrode impedance enables common mode rejection.
- Filters reduce noise but also alter the signal of interest; normal values should be based on the same filter settings.
- Electronic averaging can improve small sensory / mixed responses by reducing random noise.
- Stimulus artifact can obscure onset and alter amplitude / latency measurement, especially in small potentials or short-distance studies.
- Ground placement between stimulator and recording electrodes, reduced impedance mismatch, coaxial cables, optimized stimulator position, lower stimulus intensity, anode rotation, increased distance, and cable separation can reduce stimulus artifact.
- Depolarization starts under the cathode; the cathode should face the active recording electrode.
- Reversing cathode and anode can prolong distal latency by about 0.3-0.4 ms and slow sensory conduction velocity by about 10 m/s, mimicking polyneuropathy or distal entrapment.
- Supramaximal stimulation requires increasing current until amplitude plateaus and then increasing about another 25%.
- Stopping once the potential is in the normal range is a common error.
- Submaximal distal stimulation can mimic axonal loss; submaximal proximal stimulation can mimic conduction block.
- Co-stimulation may make low amplitudes look normal, create false conduction block, mimic anomalous innervation, or hide true conduction block.
- Recording-electrode distance from the nerve strongly affects sensory / mixed amplitude; edema or off-nerve electrode placement can reduce or abolish sensory responses.
- Active-reference electrode distance that is too short can reduce sensory amplitude by cancellation; 3-4 cm is preferred for sensory / mixed studies.
- Ulnar across-elbow distance measurement with the elbow extended can underestimate true nerve length and artifactually slow conduction velocity.
- Limb position should remain the same across stimulation sites when possible.
- Sweep speed and sensitivity influence onset latency measurement and should remain consistent within a nerve conduction study.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Recording / stimulation setup problem
→ waveform or measurement distortion
→ false amplitude drop, false latency shift, false slowing, false absent response, or false normalization
→ erroneous axonal loss, demyelination, entrapment, conduction block, or normal interpretation
```

#### Inferences

- Before calling conduction block, the examiner must consider submaximal stimulation, distal or proximal co-stimulation, anomalous innervation, temporal dispersion, and nerve-specific normal variants.
- In edema, an absent lower-extremity sensory response may be less informative than a preserved normal response.

#### Assumptions

- Operator can reposition stimulator and electrodes, monitor twitch / waveform morphology, and repeat measurements under standardized settings.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Some proximal stimulation sites inevitably co-stimulate adjacent nerves; specialized methods may be required.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Always record and monitor distal limb temperature; target 32-34°C when possible.
- Prefer warming / rewarming over blind temperature correction.
- Interpret pediatric, older adult, tall-patient, and lower-extremity values with appropriate norms.
- Treat noise, stimulus artifact, submaximal stimulation, co-stimulation, and electrode misplacement as possible explanations before diagnosing new disease.
- In edema, low or absent sensory responses should be explicitly qualified as possibly technical.
- Use consistent sensitivity and sweep speed for latency measurement within the same nerve.
- In suspected conduction block, verify supramaximal stimulation, waveform morphology, twitch pattern, co-stimulation, distal response validity, and anatomic variants.

## Research-Useful Points

- Artifact recognition can be studied as a diagnostic quality-control intervention with outcomes such as false-positive reduction, repeat-study rate, and clinical management change.
- Temperature and edema are practical modifiers that should be recorded in datasets used for automated NCS interpretation.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- Conflicts with "NCS abnormal value equals disease."
- Conflicts with "low amplitude equals axonal loss."
- Conflicts with "proximal amplitude drop equals conduction block."
- Conflicts with "normal-range amplitude proves stimulation adequacy."
- Conflicts with "absent sural response in edema or advanced age automatically means neuropathy."
- Conflicts with "temperature correction fully replaces warming."

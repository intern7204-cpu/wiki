## One-Sentence Summary

Neurological rehabilitation is a goal-directed, team-based, measurement-driven learning process that uses progressive practice, feedback, self-management, complication prevention, and environment planning to reduce disability and improve participation after neurologic disease.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Neurorehabilitation goals and team-based care

#### One-Sentence Definition

Neurorehabilitation links neurologic pathology to impairment, disability, activity, participation, caregiver burden, and quality of life through interdisciplinary goals and coordinated practice.

#### Known Facts

- Rehabilitation training reduces physical and cognitive impairments and related disabilities / activity limitations to increase functional independence and HRQoL.
- Movement and skill training are active learning and self-management processes using motivation, guidance, goal setting, progressive practice, feedback, and social support.
- The interdisciplinary team should solve functional problems rather than work as isolated discipline silos.
- Training strategies should be reinforced by all team members using agreed approaches.
- Short-term goals should be relevant, motivating, explicit, attainable, measurable, and agreeable to the patient.
- Smooth transition between inpatient and community care is emphasized.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Neurologic disease
-> impairment and activity limitation
-> interdisciplinary assessment and goal setting
-> coordinated progressive practice and complication prevention
-> home / community transfer and self-management
-> improved independence, participation, and HRQoL
```

#### Inferences

- A neurorehabilitation plan should state both medical and functional problems, not just therapy disciplines.
- Nursing carryover, caregiver training, and home practice are not peripheral; they are part of the learning environment.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Optimal service setting depends on severity, medical complexity, family / caregiver support, community resources, and payer system.

### Concept: Outcome measurement in neurorehabilitation

#### One-Sentence Definition

Outcome measurement in neurorehabilitation should separately capture impairment, activity / disability, participation, psychosocial needs, and HRQoL rather than treating one ADL scale as the whole outcome.

#### Known Facts

- Outcome measurement is essential for service effectiveness, individual intervention monitoring, and clinical trials.
- Measurement levels include pathophysiology / impairment, disability / functional activity, handicap / participation, and HRQoL.
- ICF emphasizes personal and environmental factors.
- BI and FIM measure assistance needs for ADLs; FIM has floor / ceiling and sensitivity limitations.
- Walking speed and 2- or 6-minute walk tests are useful but offer limited insight into daily real-world behavior.
- Self-report and actual activity may differ, supporting real-world activity monitoring.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Intervention target
-> choose measure at matching level
-> repeat measurement over time
-> revise goals and training dose
-> document service effectiveness and patient-centered progress
```

#### Inferences

- A trial or service report using only impairment outcomes may miss participation and HRQoL changes.
- Wearable monitoring may complement clinic-based tests when daily activity is the real target.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Measures differ in responsiveness, ceiling / floor effects, burden, and applicability across diagnoses.

### Concept: Motor learning and plasticity in neurorehabilitation

#### One-Sentence Definition

Neurorehabilitation uses practice-driven motor learning and biologic plasticity to exploit spared networks, improve task performance, and teach compensatory or restorative strategies.

#### Known Facts

- Physical and cognitive training, stimulation, pharmacologic interventions, and natural recovery processes may enhance restoration.
- Network plasticity includes resolution of edema / conduction block / diaschisis, increased activity in adjacent or spared pathways, map expansion, recruitment of parallel networks, neurotransmitter modulation, and alternative behaviors.
- Neuronal plasticity includes synaptic efficacy changes, LTP / LTD, denervation hypersensitivity, receptor changes, and neurotransmitter release / uptake changes.
- The source cautions against over-extrapolating animal studies.
- Robotic upper limb therapy and treadmill robotics show equal or modest improvement over conventional therapy of similar intensity, not clear superiority in the source.
- Behavioral self-management includes education, goal setting, barrier identification, problem solving, feedback, tailored instruction, decision making, and ongoing support.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Repeated goal-directed task practice
-> feedback, error correction, motivation, and progressive challenge
-> activity-dependent network and synaptic change
-> improved or adapted task performance
-> home/community practice maintains gains
```

#### Inferences

- Technology should be judged by whether it increases meaningful dose, feedback, progression, access, or measurement, not by novelty.
- Practice intensity must be paired with task relevance and patient engagement.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Pharmacologic adjuncts, stimulation, BCI, robotics, and exoskeletons remain context-dependent and require current evidence review.

## Clinical Implications

- Neurorehabilitation goals should be written as patient-relevant, explicit, attainable, measurable tasks.
- The physician’s role includes integrating neurologic, musculoskeletal, cardiopulmonary, and other impairments onto a functional map and facilitating team goals.
- Rehab nursing, PT, OT, SLP, neuropsychology, social work, orthotics, recreation, patient, and caregiver roles should reinforce shared strategies.
- AFO prescription should be tied to heel contact, toe clearance, hip hiking, mediolateral subtalar stability, tibial stability, sensory-driven foot placement, knee control, weight bearing, stance, sway, safety, and speed.
- Self-management and caregiver / family support are central for chronic neurologic disease and community participation.

## Fact

- The source states stroke units reduce death, institutionalization, and dependency, with benefits persisting up to 5 years.
- RCTs cited in the source show aphasia therapy can moderately benefit expression and comprehension, with practice amount important.
- Upper limb static orthoses after stroke are not shown in the source to improve arm / hand function, ROM, or pain incidence in small trials.
- Robotic gait training is not described as superior to overground training for stroke, SCI, or MS in ambulatory patients in the source.
- FNS can support grasp, standing, ambulation, peroneal stimulation, and cycling in selected contexts in the source.

## Inference

- High-quality neurorehabilitation is a system of practice and feedback, not a list of modalities.
- If a technology does not increase relevant practice, measurement, access, or self-management, its clinical value is uncertain.

## Assumption

- Disease-specific contraindications, medical stability, cardiopulmonary capacity, cognition, mood, and caregiver context are assessed before exercise or technology use.

## Uncertainty

- Evidence for stimulation, pharmacologic augmentation, robotics, BCI, exoskeletons, and mobile health is mixed or evolving and should be rechecked for current decisions.

# Source Summary: HCP-ECD Interventions and Cognitive Outcomes

## Source Type

- Systematic review and meta-analysis.
- Full title: *Effect of early childhood development interventions delivered by healthcare providers to improve cognitive outcomes in children at 0-36 months: a systematic review and meta-analysis*.
- Authors: Hirve et al.
- Journal / year: *Archives of Disease in Childhood*, 2023.
- PROSPERO registration: `CRD42019122021`.
- This summary uses only `C:\原始資料\247.full\247.full.md`.

## Reliability Level

- Tier 1 within this wiki because it is a systematic review / meta-analysis of randomized, cluster-randomized, and quasi-randomized trials.
- Strengths: preregistered protocol, broad database search without language or geographic limits, duplicate review / extraction, Cochrane risk-of-bias assessment, GRADE assessment, random-effects meta-analysis, and prespecified subgroup analyses.
- Main limitations: primary cognitive outcome had low-certainty evidence because of heterogeneity and risk of bias; several secondary outcomes had very low certainty and wide confidence intervals; included infants were from families facing adversity, so universal-population extrapolation is limited.

## One-Sentence Summary

Healthcare provider-delivered ECD interventions may improve cognitive and motor outcomes and home environment quality in infants aged 0-36 months, but the evidence is limited by risk of bias, heterogeneity, small effects, and no clear benefit for maternal mental health, language, socioemotional, behaviour, executive, or adaptive outcomes.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: HCP-ECD interventions as health-system early development support

#### One-Sentence Definition

`HCP-ECD interventions` are face-to-face early childhood development interventions delivered by primary-level healthcare providers, usually through home visits, that support responsive caregiving, early learning, and/or motor stimulation.

#### Known Facts

- The review included 97 papers reporting 42 trials in the narrative synthesis.
- Twenty-seven trials were included in meta-analyses.
- The abstract reports 42 randomized controlled trials with `15,557` infants; the results section later reports `15,661` infants in 41 trials. This internal count discrepancy is not resolved in the local source file.
- Thirty-eight trials were individual RCTs and four were cluster RCTs.
- The source reports 33 trials from high-income countries and eight from low- and middle-income countries.
- All included infants were from families experiencing some adversity, such as low socioeconomic status, maternal drug abuse, adolescent motherhood, or prematurity.
- Eligible interventions had to be delivered by primary-level healthcare providers such as generalist nurses, health visitors, midwives, child health nurses, general practitioners, primary care doctors, or community health workers.
- Interventions could start in hospital but had to include community-based postdischarge follow-up.
- Interventions had to be face-to-face through home visiting, mobile health team visits, clinic visits, child health checks, or group programmes.
- Forty trials used home visits and two used community clinics.
- Contacts ranged from 6 to 312, with median 25 and IQR 9-52.
- Interventions lasted from 6 weeks to 36 months.
- Intervention categories were responsive caregiving, early learning support, and motor stimulation.
- `ECD predominance` was defined as ECD content taking more than 50% of contact time between healthcare provider and family.
- Pooled data from 13 trials suggested improved cognitive outcomes at 0-36 months: BSID-III mean difference `2.65`, 95% CI `0.61 to 4.70`, `n = 2482`, low certainty.
- Pooled data from nine trials suggested improved motor outcomes: BSID-III mean difference `4.01`, 95% CI `1.54 to 6.48`, `n = 1437`. The results section calls this moderate certainty, while the abstract calls it low certainty.
- Pooled data from eight trials suggested improved home environment: HOME inventory mean difference `1.37`, 95% CI `0.29 to 2.45`, `n = 1534`, low certainty.
- There was little to no effect on maternal mental health: SMD `-0.13`, 95% CI `-0.29 to 0.03`, `n = 2806`, 11 trials, low certainty.
- There was little to no effect on speech/language, socioemotional, or infant behaviour outcomes.
- No studies reported executive functioning or adaptive functioning outcomes.
- Subgroup analyses did not show clear differences by number of contacts, timing, type of intervention, type of healthcare provider, country income level, or risk of bias.
- The only subgroup signal was ECD predominance: ECD-predominant interventions had larger cognitive effects than non-ECD-predominant interventions.
- There was no evidence of a visit-number dose-response for cognitive outcome: beta coefficient `0.018`, 95% CI `-0.07 to 0.11`, `n = 1811`, 12 trials.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Repeated healthcare-provider contact
-> face-to-face support for responsive caregiving, early learning, and/or motor stimulation
-> caregiver-child interaction and home learning environment may improve
-> infants receive more developmentally relevant input and practice
-> cognitive and motor scores may improve at 0-36 months
```

#### Inferences

- The most defensible health-system interpretation is not "more visits are better," but "visits need enough ECD-specific content and provider skill to change caregiving and learning input."
- Improved HOME inventory scores are compatible with home environment as a mechanism, but this source does not prove mediation.
- HCP-ECD is adjacent to parenting intervention and Early Intervention, but it is a narrower delivery model: primary-level healthcare providers delivering face-to-face ECD content during early-life contacts.
- Because maternal mental health did not clearly improve, HCP-ECD should not be used as a substitute for caregiver mental health screening, referral, or treatment.

#### Assumptions

- Healthcare providers are trained and supervised enough to deliver ECD content with fidelity.
- Families can actually attend, receive, or complete repeated face-to-face contacts.
- The local system can support home visits, clinic-based coaching, or postdischarge community follow-up.
- The target child population resembles the adversity-exposed populations represented in the included trials.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The primary cognitive effect has low certainty because of heterogeneity and risk of bias.
- Timing-specific analyses at 12, 24, and 36 months were underpowered and imprecise.
- Subgroup analyses were limited by small sample size and limited power.
- It is unclear which active ingredient matters most: responsive caregiving, early learning support, motor stimulation, provider relationship, home visiting, or ECD predominance.
- Language, socioemotional, behaviour, executive functioning, and adaptive functioning outcomes remain unproven or unmeasured in this review.
- The evidence applies best to infants facing adversity; it should not be automatically generalized to all infants or to older preschool children.

## Clinically Useful Points

- In pediatric primary care or home-visiting design, HCP-ECD should be treated as structured developmental coaching, not as a leaflet or generic anticipatory guidance.
- If the goal is cognitive or motor developmental support for infants facing adversity, HCP-ECD is a plausible health-system entry point.
- If the goal is maternal depression, speech/language disorder, executive functioning, adaptive functioning, or serious developmental disability management, this source does not support HCP-ECD as a stand-alone solution.
- Program notes should document the ECD content actually delivered: responsive caregiving practice, early learning support, motor stimulation, caregiver practice, and follow-up plan.
- Counting visit frequency alone is insufficient; the review did not find a clear visit-number dose-response.

## Research-Useful Points

- Future studies need better risk-of-bias control, clearer intervention component reporting, mediation testing, longer and more precise follow-up, and direct measurement of language, EF, adaptive function, and caregiver mental health.
- Implementation research should test whether ECD-predominant contact, provider training, supervision, and home-visit fidelity drive outcome differences.
- Any later synthesis with Jeong 2021, WHO parenting guidance, Nurturing Care, and Early Intervention should keep delivery model, target population, and outcome domain separate.

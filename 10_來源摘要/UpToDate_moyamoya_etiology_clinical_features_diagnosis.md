Moyamoya 是 distal ICA / circle of Willis progressive stenosis with basal collateral vessels 的 angiographic vasculopathy pattern；診斷需確認 characteristic vascular pattern、區分 MMD vs MMS、排除 mimics，並用 clinical features 與 imaging clues 觸發 CTA/MRA/DSA evaluation。

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: MMD versus MMS etiologic stratification

#### One-Sentence Definition

Moyamoya disease (MMD) 是 moyamoya angiographic findings 加上 genetic susceptibility 且沒有 underlying contributing medical condition；Moyamoya syndrome (MMS) 是相同 angiographic pattern 出現在可疑 associated medical condition 脈絡中。

#### Known Facts

- Moyamoya refers to unilateral or bilateral stenosis / occlusion around the circle of Willis with prominent arterial collateral circulation。
- MMD is also called primary or idiopathic moyamoya or spontaneous occlusion of the circle of Willis。
- MMS is moyamoya angiographic findings with an associated medical condition implicated in vascular change。
- Pathophysiologic processes involve vessel wall thickening and angiogenesis。
- The terminal portions of the ICAs and proximal MCA / ACA are most involved。
- Some unilateral cases progress to bilateral involvement in approximately one-third of patients over two to eight years。
- Posterior circulation involvement is less common; posterior cerebral artery can be involved。
- Collateral vessels include basal moyamoya vessels, leptomeningeal collaterals and transdural anastomoses。
- Associated aneurysms can occur at circle of Willis branching points or along collateral vessels。
- Small-vessel aneurysms are a major cause of intracerebral and intraventricular hemorrhage in moyamoya。
- Extracranial / systemic arterial stenosis can occur, including cervical carotid, renal, pulmonary, aortic and coronary arteries。
- MMD incidence and prevalence are highest in East Asian populations, especially Japan, China and Korea。
- Familial occurrence is approximately 10 to 15 percent of cases。
- RNF213 variants, especially p.R4810K, are susceptibility factors in East Asian populations; the variant is associated with earlier onset and more severe progression in this source。
- Associated conditions for MMS include cranial irradiation, cranial trauma, brain tumors, meningitis, sickle cell disease, beta thalassemia, autoimmune / vasculitic disease, Graves disease / thyroiditis, congenital / developmental syndromes such as Down syndrome and NF1, and several vascular, metabolic and kidney disorders。
- Annual incidence rates cited by the source range from 0.35 to 0.94 per 100,000 individuals; prevalence ranges from 3.2 to 10.5 per 100,000。
- Age of onset is bimodal, with one peak at 5 to 10 years and another broader peak at 30 to 50 years。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Genetic susceptibility or associated systemic/intracranial condition
-> abnormal vessel wall response, intimal thickening and angiogenesis
-> distal ICA / proximal circle of Willis stenosis or occlusion
-> basal, leptomeningeal and transdural collateral formation
-> chronic hypoperfusion plus fragile collateral vessels
-> ischemic TIA/stroke, hemorrhage, seizure, headache or cognitive symptoms
```

#### Inferences

- The practical diagnostic question is not only "is this moyamoya?" but also "is this MMD or MMS?" because MMS requires looking for a modifiable or syndromic underlying condition。
- RNF213 is useful as susceptibility context, especially in East Asian families, but the source does not make genetic testing alone diagnostic。
- Deep ICH / IVH without typical hypertensive explanation should prompt vascular-pattern thinking, not just small-vessel hypertension labeling。

#### Assumptions

- The patient has cerebrovascular symptoms or imaging findings that raise suspicion for moyamoya。
- Adequate vascular imaging is available or can be obtained。
- Alternative intracranial stenosis causes are actively considered。

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The exact etiology of MMD remains unknown despite genetic associations。
- The mode of inheritance is not established。
- Many associated MMS conditions are based on heterogeneous reports and likely represent diverse mechanisms。
- This source does not determine treatment threshold; treatment and prognosis require separate source processing。

### Concept: Clinical recognition and diagnostic imaging

#### One-Sentence Definition

Moyamoya clinical recognition is triggered by recurrent or provoked ischemic symptoms, young stroke, unexplained deep ICH / IVH, suggestive MRI collateral signs or characteristic CTA/MRA findings; diagnosis requires cerebrovascular imaging showing stenosis plus collateral vessels after excluding mimics。

#### Known Facts

- Moyamoya may be symptomatic or incidentally identified on neuroimaging。
- Ischemic stroke or TIA is the most common presentation in both children and adults。
- Common ischemic symptoms include hemiparesis, hemisensory loss and speech impairment, reflecting anterior circulation involvement。
- TIA / stroke symptoms can be recurrent because stenosis is fixed。
- Ischemic episodes can be triggered by exercise, crying, coughing, straining, fever or hyperventilation。
- Triggered symptoms are postulated to reflect blood pressure reduction and/or hypocapnia-related cerebral perfusion reduction。
- Hemorrhagic complications account for approximately 25 to 30 percent of presentations, mostly in adults。
- Hemorrhagic manifestations include ICH, SAH and IVH。
- ICH typically occurs in deep structures such as basal ganglia, thalamus and/or ventricular system。
- Seizures are uncommon as an initial presentation, accounting for less than 10 percent of clinical presentations, but epilepsy can be a frequent complication。
- Hyperventilation-induced EEG rebuild-up phenomenon is considered pathognomonic in children but hyperventilation should be minimized in known moyamoya because it may induce cerebral vasoconstriction。
- Adults can have neurocognitive impairment even without clinically apparent prior stroke。
- Cerebral infarction often involves cortical/subcortical regions and superficial or deep border-zone regions distal to stenotic / occluded vessels。
- MRI "ivy sign" and "brush sign" can suggest impaired collateral flow / reserve but are not specific for moyamoya。
- Multiple punctate or serpentine T2 flow voids in the basal ganglia or thalamus are considered virtually diagnostic by this source。
- CTA / MRA may show distal ICA or proximal circle of Willis stenosis plus basal collateral vessels。
- MRA is preferred as an initial study in children to avoid radiation and contrast exposure。
- DSA is the gold standard and is required for nondiagnostic or unilateral noninvasive findings。
- DSA is also typically required for surgical treatment planning, but neurologic complications occur in approximately 3 per 1000 procedures。
- Advanced vessel wall imaging may help distinguish MMD from intracranial atherosclerosis: moyamoya can show negative remodeling and concentric enhancement; atherosclerosis more often shows focal eccentric enhancement without reduced outer vessel diameter。
- 2021 Japanese diagnostic criteria allow DSA diagnosis with unilateral or bilateral terminal ICA stenosis / occlusion plus arterial-phase moyamoya vessels。
- Noninvasive MRA diagnosis requires bilateral terminal ICA stenosis / occlusion, narrowed outer diameters of terminal ICAs and proximal MCAs, and collateral vessels in basal ganglia and/or periventricular white matter。
- CTA can identify moyamoya findings but was not specified in those diagnostic criteria。
- Differential diagnoses include intracranial atherosclerosis, arterial dissection, RCVS, focal cerebral arteriopathy and vasculitis。
- General asymptomatic screening is not recommended, but noninvasive screening may be reasonable with strong family history, especially East Asian families, or predisposition to MMS。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Fixed distal ICA / circle of Willis stenosis
-> impaired reserve and reliance on collateral flow
-> symptoms emerge when perfusion demand rises or CO2/BP physiology reduces flow
-> recurrent same-territory TIA / stroke or deep collateral-vessel hemorrhage
-> MRI/CTA/MRA suggest vasculopathy
-> DSA or high-quality noninvasive imaging confirms stenosis plus collaterals
-> alternative intracranial vasculopathies must be excluded
```

#### Inferences

- Recurrent, provoked, same-territory TIA in a child or young adult should be treated as a perfusion / vasculopathy clue, not as benign episodic weakness。
- Deep ICH / IVH in patients without typical hypertensive risk factors should trigger moyamoya consideration。
- Ivy sign or brush sign alone should not be overcalled as moyamoya; the diagnostic weight rests on stenosis plus collateral anatomy。

#### Assumptions

- Stroke workup includes vascular imaging rather than only parenchymal CT / MRI。
- The interpreting team distinguishes luminal stenosis from outer-vessel remodeling and collateral pattern。
- Patient age, ethnicity, family history and associated syndromic conditions are available for interpretation。

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Noninvasive imaging may miss early-stage moyamoya and small collateral circulation。
- The benefit of angiographic screening in asymptomatic high-risk individuals remains unclear。
- A normal single screening study, especially in younger individuals, does not exclude later development。
- Prognosis and treatment implications are intentionally deferred to a separate source。

## Clinically Useful Points

- Do not equate "moyamoya" with idiopathic MMD; first separate angiographic pattern from MMD vs MMS etiology。
- Suspect moyamoya in children or young adults with recurrent same-territory TIA, especially if triggered by hyperventilation, crying, fever, exercise or straining。
- Suspect moyamoya in unexplained caudate / thalamic / intraventricular hemorrhage without typical ICH risk factors。
- In children, MRA is the preferred initial vascular study; in unilateral or nondiagnostic noninvasive studies, DSA is required。
- In adults with possible atherosclerosis, vessel wall imaging and collateral pattern help distinguish moyamoya from intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis。
- Do not use ivy sign or brush sign alone as diagnostic proof。

## Research-Useful Points

- MMD / MMS heterogeneity should be modeled separately in studies。
- RNF213-associated disease, East Asian ancestry and systemic-associated MMS likely represent different risk structures。
- Imaging studies should distinguish luminal stenosis, outer-vessel remodeling, collateral burden, perfusion reserve and hemorrhage markers。
- Screening research must account for uncertain benefit in asymptomatic high-risk relatives or syndrome populations。

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- 與「moyamoya 一定是 bilateral disease」衝突；DSA diagnostic criteria 可接受 unilateral findings。
- 與「有 basal collaterals 就都是 idiopathic MMD」衝突；MMS 需找 associated condition。
- 與「ivy sign / brush sign 就能診斷 moyamoya」衝突；兩者不具 specificity。
- 與「無症狀親屬都應 routine screening」衝突；來源指出一般不篩檢，除非 strong family history 或 predisposition。
- 與「成人 deep ICH 都是 hypertension」衝突；在缺乏典型 risk factors 時，moyamoya 是 differential。

# Source Summary: Maroto-Izquierdo et al. 2024 — RIR for Special Populations

## One-Sentence Summary

RIR is a low-cost, adaptable way to prescribe resistance-training intensity by stopping a set before expected failure, but in special populations it remains a promising method requiring familiarization, monitoring, and formal validation.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: RIR-Based Resistance Training Intensity Prescription

#### One-Sentence Definition

Repetitions in reserve (RIR) estimates resistance-training intensity by asking how many repetitions could still be performed before task failure, allowing load to be adjusted without repeated 1RM testing.

#### Known Facts

- The review compares `%1RM`, velocity-based training, RPE, effort character, and RIR for prescribing resistance training intensity.
- `%1RM` prescription requires direct or estimated maximal strength and may be risky, time-consuming, or inaccurate in special populations.
- X-RM methods often imply testing or training near muscle failure, which can increase injury risk, fatigue, recovery time, and discomfort in special populations.
- Velocity-based training can individualize load and estimate 1RM, but requires valid equipment, time, and exercise-specific velocity profiles; it is harder to apply to non-linear or functional exercises.
- RPE is flexible and usable across settings but requires familiarization and is affected by question wording, timing, fatigue, exercise type, and prior experience.
- RIR quantifies the discrepancy between possible repetitions and actual repetitions completed.
- The review identifies three RIR-related scale approaches:
  - ERF scale: 0 to 10+ estimated repetitions to failure.
  - RPE-RIR scale: 10-point RPE anchored to remaining repetitions.
  - Effort character: low, medium, high, maximal effort categories based on proportion of possible repetitions completed.
- The review reports a PubMed search of RIR / ERF / effort-character / proximity-to-failure terms and summarizes 70 studies screened for validity and reliability relevance.
- RIR validation studies are mostly in healthy or resistance-trained participants, novices, or athletic contexts; direct clinical special-population evidence is limited.
- In special populations, the review proposes four effort levels with approximate RPE and velocity-loss logic.
- A high-effort strength example stops a set two repetitions before mechanical failure and corresponds to RPE about `7-8/10`.
- A muscular-endurance example may prescribe about half of possible repetitions and corresponds to moderate effort with RPE about `4-6/10`.
- The authors explicitly state that RIR needs further validity, reliability, feasibility, familiarization, and standardization research in clinical / rehabilitation settings.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Special population has variable capacity and higher risk from maximal testing
-> repeated 1RM / failure testing is undesirable
-> estimate possible repetitions for a given load or exercise setup
-> prescribe completed reps while leaving defined repetitions in reserve
-> adjust set-by-set based on fatigue, symptoms, motivation, and daily condition
-> maintain a safer resistance-training stimulus without forcing maximal failure
```

#### Inferences

- RIR is best treated as an intensity-control language inside a broader therapeutic exercise prescription.
- In rehabilitation settings, RIR is most useful when `%1RM` is impractical and velocity-based technology is unavailable.
- RIR may improve alignment between prescribed and executed intensity, especially when the clinician explicitly documents target RIR, RPE, symptoms, recovery, and progression rules.

#### Assumptions

- The patient can understand and report proximity to failure after familiarization.
- The exercise movement is safe enough to approach the planned proximity-to-failure range.
- The clinician continues to monitor symptoms, technique, adverse response, and recovery.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Direct validation in older adults, pregnancy, chronic disease, neurological disease, cancer rehabilitation, frailty, pain, or complex multimorbidity remains limited.
- The review does not provide disease-specific contraindications, stop criteria, or monitoring thresholds.
- RIR accuracy may be weaker in inexperienced patients, early familiarization, early sets, and when the patient is far from failure.
- RIR should not be used as the only safety metric in high-risk clinical exercise.

## Clinically Useful Points

- When documenting resistance training, write the intensity metric explicitly: `%1RM`, RPE, RIR, velocity loss, or another disease-specific metric.
- RIR can be documented as `target RIR 2`, `stop with 2 reps in reserve`, or `moderate effort, RPE 4-6/10, about half of possible repetitions`.
- For special populations, avoid treating training to failure as the default.
- RIR requires patient education and calibration; first-session estimates should be conservative.

## Research-Useful Points

- Future studies should compare RIR with `%1RM` and velocity-based training in clinical populations.
- Feasibility outcomes should include comprehension, adherence, adverse events, progression accuracy, symptom flare, and inter-rater / patient-clinician agreement.
- Disease-specific RIR protocols are needed before RIR can be treated as a standard clinical prescription method.

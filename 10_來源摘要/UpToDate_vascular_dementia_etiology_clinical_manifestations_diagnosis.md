# Source Summary: Etiology, clinical manifestations, and diagnosis of vascular dementia

## Source Type

UpToDate topic review。Literature review current through 2026-04；topic last updated 2025-02-25。

## Reliability Level

Tier 6。此來源適合整理 adult vascular cognitive impairment and dementia (VCID) / vascular dementia (VaD) 的 definition、etiology、clinical syndromes、evaluation 與 diagnostic boundary。它不是 treatment / prevention topic，也不是 stroke secondary prevention guideline。

## One-Sentence Summary

VCID / VaD 是由 cerebrovascular disease 或 impaired cerebral blood flow 造成或參與造成的 cognitive impairment syndrome；診斷必須同時確認客觀 cognitive impairment、stroke history 或 neuroimaging evidence of cerebrovascular disease，以及 vascular injury 足以解釋 cognitive impairment，不能只靠 MRI 上有 vascular lesion。

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: VaD diagnosis and etiologic stratification

#### One-Sentence Definition

VaD 診斷是「cognitive impairment + vascular causality judgment」：要先分類 vascular MCI 或 VaD，再用 clinical stroke history / neuroimaging evidence 與 lesion burden、location、severity、mixed pathology 來判斷 cerebrovascular disease 是否足以造成症狀。

#### Known Facts

- VCID includes vascular dementia and milder impairment such as vascular MCI。
- VaD is usually recognized when clinical stroke is followed by dementia, or when imaging identifies vascular brain injury in a person with dementia but no clinical stroke history。
- The mere presence of cerebrovascular brain injury on neuroimaging is not sufficient to diagnose VaD。
- VaD is a syndrome rather than a single disease。
- VaD diagnosis is not complete until the underlying cerebrovascular or cardiovascular damage is characterized。
- Vascular disease is a cause or contributor in 25 to 50 percent of dementia cases in this source。
- Clinicopathologic dementia specialty clinic studies suggest pure VaD accounts for approximately 10 percent of dementia, whereas multiple-etiology dementia with vascular component accounts for approximately 30 to 40 percent。
- Any cause of ischemic stroke, intracerebral hemorrhage, or subarachnoid hemorrhage can cause VaD if brain injury is severe enough。
- Cerebral small vessel disease has an outsized role in VaD burden。
- Main cerebral small vessel disease categories include arteriolosclerosis, cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA), and less common causes such as CADASIL, vasculitides, inflammatory disorders, and venous collagenosis。
- Modern criteria include classification as vascular MCI or VaD, identification of cerebrovascular disease by history or neuroimaging, and judgment that vascular disease is sufficient to cause cognitive impairment。
- Newer criteria do not require memory impairment; significant impairment in only one cognitive domain can be sufficient when functional criteria are met。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Cerebrovascular / cardiovascular disease
-> ischemia, hemorrhage, hypoperfusion, embolism, or small vessel injury
-> strategic lesion, cumulative lesion burden, white matter network disruption, or mixed pathology
-> cognitive impairment
-> classify as vascular MCI or VaD by functional impact and causal sufficiency
```

#### Inferences

- The key diagnostic separation is not "vascular lesion present vs absent" but "vascular lesion sufficient vs incidental / contributory"。
- Etiologic characterization is clinically necessary because prevention strategy depends on stroke mechanism and vascular cause。
- Mixed dementia should be expected rather than treated as an exception in older adults。

#### Assumptions

- The patient is an adult with measurable cognitive impairment。
- Structural neuroimaging and clinical stroke history are available or can be obtained。
- Alternative and coexisting neurodegenerative diseases are actively considered。

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Vascular pathology is common in asymptomatic older adults, making causal attribution difficult。
- Cognitive reserve and lesion location prevent a single infarct-volume threshold from reliably predicting VaD。
- The source notes neurovascular unit dysfunction beyond ischemia / hemorrhage as possible but unproven in causing VaD or AD。

### Concept: VaD clinical presentation and evaluation pathway

#### One-Sentence Definition

VaD evaluation should integrate trajectory, function, cognitive profile, stroke history, neurologic signs, neuroimaging, vascular risk workup, and differential diagnosis rather than relying on one screening test or one MRI finding。

#### Known Facts

- The two main VaD clinical syndromes are poststroke dementia and vascular dementia without recent stroke。
- Poststroke dementia often shows stepwise decline after clinically diagnosed stroke and may show executive dysfunction with relative episodic memory sparing, but profiles vary by stroke location and size。
- Strategic infarcts can produce clinically significant cognitive impairment from a single lesion; examples include medial frontal lobes, language cortices, thalamus, and medial temporal lobes。
- Approximately 10 to 30 percent of patients develop new dementia one to five years after stroke in systematic reviews cited by the source。
- VaD without recent stroke may show progressive or stepwise decline with imaging evidence of clinically unrecognized, better termed covert, cerebrovascular disease。
- Arteriolosclerotic cerebral small vessel disease tends to impair executive function and processing speed, but memory and other domains may also be affected。
- VaD can be accompanied by depression, abulia, apathy, psychosis, pseudobulbar affect, gait slowing / lower-body parkinsonism, and increased urinary frequency。
- Evaluation should assess severity and profile of cognitive decline, stroke history, risk factors and causes of stroke / covert cerebrovascular disease, brain imaging evidence, and signs or biomarkers of other neurodegenerative disease。
- Hachinski Ischemic Score 7 or greater indicates that vascular contribution is likely in this source。
- Cognitive screening is required to show objective cognitive impairment; MoCA appears more sensitive than MMSE for detecting VCID, but scores do not substitute for integrated diagnosis。
- Neuropsychological testing is suggested when severity or pattern remains unclear after screening and neurologic examination。
- Structural brain imaging should be performed; MRI is preferred over CT for cerebral small vessel disease and microbleeds unless cost or contraindication prevents MRI。
- STRIVE terminology includes nonlacunar infarcts, lacunes, white matter hyperintensities / hypodensities, microbleeds, superficial siderosis, perivascular spaces, and atrophy。
- Differential diagnosis includes AD, Lewy body diseases / Parkinson disease dementia, normal pressure hydrocephalus, and depression。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke, covert cerebrovascular disease, or cerebral small vessel disease
-> lesion-location and network effects
-> executive / processing-speed impairment, cortical signs, gait / urinary / affective features, or mixed dementia pattern
-> integrated evaluation with history, MoCA, neuropsychology, exam, labs, MRI and differential diagnosis
```

#### Inferences

- Executive dysfunction and processing-speed impairment support a vascular contribution but cannot prove VaD without imaging / stroke-history context。
- MRI is especially useful when there is no clear clinical stroke history, because covert cerebrovascular disease can still be clinically relevant。
- Screening and neuropsychological profiles should be interpreted as probabilistic pattern recognition, not disease-specific biomarkers。

#### Assumptions

- The cognitive screen is performed in a language, sensory and educational context where interpretation is reasonable。
- Functional loss from cognition can be separated, at least clinically, from motor, sensory or aphasic impairment after stroke。

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Neuropsychological comparisons between VaD and AD are limited by lack of biomarker confirmation and small autopsy-confirmed samples。
- Smooth progressive small vessel disease can mimic neurodegenerative dementia。
- Late-life depression can be both a mimic and a possible early manifestation or risk marker of VCID / neurodegeneration。

## Clinically Useful Points

- Do not diagnose VaD from white matter hyperintensities alone。
- In suspected VaD, first establish objective cognitive impairment and functional impact, then determine whether cerebrovascular disease is sufficient to explain it。
- Absence of clinical stroke history does not exclude VaD; covert cerebrovascular disease can be relevant when lesion burden and clinical syndrome fit。
- Poststroke cognitive decline requires retrospective baseline history to decide whether cognitive impairment preceded stroke。
- MRI is preferred for small vessel disease, microbleeds and CAA clues。
- VaD differential diagnosis should actively check AD, Lewy body disease / Parkinson disease dementia, NPH and depression。

## Research-Useful Points

- VaD is heterogeneous; lumping all VaD mechanisms may obscure treatment and prognosis signals。
- Lesion location, burden, cognitive reserve and mixed pathology should be modeled rather than using infarct volume alone。
- Small vessel disease and CAA need mechanism-specific cognitive outcome studies。
- Better biomarker integration is needed to distinguish pure VaD, mixed AD + vascular pathology, and progressive small vessel disease mimicking AD。

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- 與「MRI 有 white matter lesion 就等於 VaD」衝突。
- 與「沒有 clinical stroke history 就不能診斷 vascular dementia」衝突。
- 與「VaD 是單一 disease entity」衝突；來源將它視為 syndrome。
- 與「VaD 通常是 pure vascular pathology」衝突；older adults 常見 multiple-etiology dementia。
- 與「memory 必須受損才可診斷 dementia / VaD」衝突；新 criteria 可由單一 domain significant impairment 加上 functional impact 支持診斷。

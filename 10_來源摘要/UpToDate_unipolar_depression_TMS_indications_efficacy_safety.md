# Source Summary: Unipolar depression in adults: Indications, efficacy, and safety of transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS)

## Source Type

UpToDate topic review. Literature review current through 2026-04；topic last updated 2025-07-21。

## Reliability Level

Tier 6：expert topic review。可作 clinical selection、safety screening 與 evidence overview，但不是單一 society guideline，也不能替代 formal TMS protocol training、device-specific manual 或 local emergency procedure。

## One-Sentence Summary

For adults with unipolar major depression, repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) is a noninvasive neuromodulation option after at least one failed antidepressant trial; surface cortical TMS、theta burst TMS 與 deep TMS 均有 randomized trial / meta-analysis evidence against sham, but benefit is smaller than ECT in severe depression, maintenance benefit is unresolved, and use requires explicit contraindication and seizure-risk screening.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: rTMS for treatment-resistant unipolar major depression

#### One-Sentence Definition

rTMS for treatment-resistant unipolar major depression 是以 scalp coil 產生快速變化 magnetic field，modulate cortical and connected mood-regulation circuits，用於 adult unipolar major depression 在 antidepressant response 不足後的 noninvasive neuromodulation treatment。

#### Known Facts

- Source scope is adult unipolar major depression；administration protocol details are reviewed elsewhere.
- US FDA approved TMS for treatment-resistant depression in 2008.
- Repetitive TMS is indicated for patients with unipolar major depression who have failed at least one antidepressant medication.
- TMS can be considered again when a patient previously responded to TMS.
- The source describes TMS as potentially useful for patients with general medical comorbidities because it does not cause systemic adverse effects.
- Standard device categories include surface cortical stimulation, theta burst stimulation, and deep stimulation.
- Surface cortical TMS is the most widely studied protocol.
- High frequency left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex stimulation and low frequency right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex stimulation have evidence of efficacy against sham treatment.
- Theta burst TMS and deep TMS also show efficacy in randomized trials and meta-analytic comparisons, with generally comparable response and all-cause discontinuation across standard protocols.
- rTMS is usually administered with ongoing antidepressant pharmacotherapy in clinical trials and practice.
- Evidence indicates rTMS is less efficacious than ECT for major depression.
- Among acute responders, benefit can persist short-term and may persist up to one year in some patients, but the proportion maintaining response declines over time.
- Maintenance TMS after acute response remains uncertain.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Alternating current through scalp coil
-> rapidly changing magnetic field
-> induced focal cortical electric current
-> depolarization / excitability modulation in prefrontal cortex
-> downstream change in connected meso-cortico-limbic / anterior cingulate / default-mode related networks
-> mood symptom improvement in a subset of patients
```

High-frequency surface cortical stimulation is generally conceptualized as excitatory, commonly over left prefrontal cortex；low-frequency stimulation is generally conceptualized as inhibitory, commonly over right prefrontal cortex. The source also describes possible neuroplasticity mechanisms, including brain-derived neurotrophic factor and structural changes, but these are mechanistic hypotheses rather than bedside selection rules.

#### Inferences

- rTMS is best documented as a treatment option for adult unipolar major depression after inadequate response to antidepressant treatment, not as a default first-line replacement for pharmacotherapy or psychotherapy.
- A referral note for rTMS should document failed treatment history, current antidepressant regimen, seizure risk, implanted hardware/device status, and why ECT is or is not preferred.
- Protocol choice should not be inferred from the abstract idea that one TMS type is universally superior; the source emphasizes limited head-to-head evidence.

#### Assumptions

- Adult patient has confirmed unipolar major depressive episode, not bipolar depression, delirium, primary substance-induced syndrome, or psychotic disorder requiring a different treatment priority.
- TMS is delivered by clinicians trained in motor threshold determination, protocol selection, adverse-event monitoring, and seizure response.
- Local device availability and payer criteria may constrain real-world use beyond the medical evidence summarized here.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Many trials did not fully blind clinicians administering TMS.
- Absolute remission rates can be modest even when odds ratios versus sham are favorable.
- Maintenance TMS benefit is not established; existing trials have small samples or potentially insufficient maintenance schedules.
- Predictors of response are inconsistent; functional connectivity and other biomarkers remain research tools rather than routine selection criteria.
- Poststroke, pregnancy/postpartum, older adult and adolescent data are promising but have additional limitations and population-specific safety concerns.

### Concept: TMS safety screen and adverse-effect boundary

#### One-Sentence Definition

TMS safety screening is the pre-treatment exclusion or risk stratification process for seizure disorder, implanted metal/electrical devices, cochlear implants, ferromagnetic head/neck tattoo ink, medications and physiologic conditions that increase seizure or device-related risk.

#### Known Facts

- TMS is contraindicated in seizure disorder.
- TMS is contraindicated with implanted metallic hardware in the magnetic field, metal fragments, cochlear implants, MRI-unsafe implanted electrical devices, and ferromagnetic-containing head/neck tattoos.
- Nonferromagnetic orthodontic hardware and metal implants below the neck are described as safe.
- Vagus nerve stimulator is not always an absolute contraindication if the TMS coil is not near device components, but expert consultation is advised.
- Psychotic features are not an absolute contraindication, but most randomized trials excluded psychotic depression and one guideline favored treatments such as ECT.
- The most serious adverse effect is generalized tonic-clonic seizure, but seizure is rare when safety guidelines are followed.
- Seizure risk increases with epilepsy history, preexisting neurologic disorder, proconvulsant medications, recent withdrawal from alcohol / benzodiazepines / anticonvulsants, stimulant use disorder, sleep deprivation, higher stimulation frequency, higher intensity, and shorter intertrain interval.
- Common adverse effects include headache, scalp pain, transient auditory threshold increase, and vasovagal syncope.
- Randomized evidence summarized in this source does not show cognitive impairment from rTMS in major depression.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Magnetic stimulation parameters + patient seizure threshold / implanted device status
-> cortical excitability or device interaction risk
-> adverse event risk if screening or protocol calibration is inadequate
-> need for contraindication checklist, motor threshold accuracy, hearing protection, and seizure response plan
```

#### Inferences

- TMS should be documented as a procedure requiring safety infrastructure, not as a benign wellness device.
- If headache or scalp pain is managed by lowering stimulation intensity, clinicians must consider that efficacy may also decrease.

#### Assumptions

- Screening includes medication review and recent substance / sedative withdrawal history.
- The treatment setting can terminate stimulation immediately and manage a seizure or syncope event.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Long-term safety for clinicians administering TMS for years is not known from this source.
- Clinically meaningful safety differences among surface cortical TMS, theta burst TMS and deep TMS are not clearly established because direct comparisons are limited.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Indication anchor：adult unipolar major depression with inadequate response to at least one antidepressant medication.
- rTMS may be reasonable when systemic medication adverse effects are a major concern, but the source does not make it superior to pharmacotherapy or psychotherapy as routine first-line treatment.
- ECT remains more efficacious than rTMS for major depression, especially when severity, psychosis, catatonia, imminent suicidality, or need for rapid response dominates decision-making.
- Contraindication checklist is mandatory: seizure disorder, intracranial metal / shrapnel, cochlear implant, MRI-unsafe electrical device, medication pump, ferromagnetic head/neck tattoo ink.
- Risk stratify seizure threshold: neurologic history, proconvulsant medications, withdrawal states, stimulant use disorder, and sleep deprivation.
- Counsel common adverse effects: headache, scalp pain, transient hearing threshold change, syncope; use hearing protection.
- Maintenance TMS should be described as uncertain rather than established relapse prevention.

## Research-Useful Points

- Head-to-head comparisons among surface cortical TMS, theta burst TMS and deep TMS remain limited.
- Predictors of response are inconsistent; benzodiazepine association may be confounded by anxiety.
- Neuroimaging connectivity, cerebral blood flow, EEG and genetic markers are research candidates, not routine clinical selection tools.
- Poststroke depression trials show possible benefit but substantial heterogeneity and limited sham-controlled evidence in many studies.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- Conflicts with any simplification that TMS is equivalent or superior to ECT for severe major depression.
- Conflicts with treating maintenance TMS as a proven standard after acute response.
- Conflicts with framing TMS as risk-free because it is noninvasive.
- Conflicts with applying aphasia / stroke motor recovery TMS evidence directly to adult unipolar depression without separate source tracking.

## One-Sentence Summary

Moyamoya treatment 的核心不是單靠藥物預防，而是依症狀與 hemodynamic compromise 分流到 surgical revascularization 或 surveillance；antiplatelet therapy 可用於 ischemic-type moyamoya，但 long-term anticoagulation 通常禁忌，急性期管理重點是避免 hypoperfusion、hyperventilation 與 perioperative hyperperfusion injury。

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Moyamoya treatment decision and revascularization

#### One-Sentence Definition

Moyamoya treatment decision 是以 symptoms、resting cerebral blood flow、hemodynamic perfusion reserve、progression risk、perioperative risk and patient values 判斷是否 referral for surgical revascularization，並用 serial surveillance 監測未手術者。

#### Known Facts

- Moyamoya is progressive intracranial vasculopathy and has no curative treatment。
- Surgical revascularization is suggested for symptomatic children and adults with cerebral ischemia, TIA, ischemic stroke, cognitive decline or hemorrhage when there is no surgical contraindication。
- Surgical revascularization is also suggested for asymptomatic children and adults with decreased regional cerebral blood flow or inadequate hemodynamic perfusion reserve。
- Asymptomatic patients with preserved cerebral blood flow may continue medical management with follow-up surveillance imaging。
- Surveillance includes periodic clinical examination for TIA / stroke symptoms, neurologic findings and provoked transient weakness。
- Surveillance imaging is performed because stenosis progression or perfusion impairment may precede symptoms。
- Yearly imaging may be used for asymptomatic patients, but severe baseline stenosis may justify shorter follow-up such as six months, while stable adult patients for at least three years may move to every other year or longer intervals。
- MRA is generally preferred for surveillance vascular imaging; CTA is an alternative; DSA is usually reserved for nondiagnostic noninvasive testing or presurgical planning。
- Cerebral blood flow imaging includes perfusion CT, perfusion MRI, arterial spin labeling, PET and SPECT depending on institutional availability。
- Revascularization aims to improve cerebral blood flow and perfusion reserve and reduce ischemic and hemorrhagic complications。
- Direct revascularization connects an external carotid branch such as superficial temporal artery to a cortical artery and can provide more immediate flow。
- Indirect revascularization places vascularized tissue on the ischemic hemisphere to encourage delayed collateralization and is frequently used in children。
- Combined revascularization uses direct and indirect techniques together。
- There are no convincing data that one surgical method is universally more effective than another; procedure selection depends on vessel anatomy, patient age, local protocol and surgical experience。
- In meta-analyses largely based on observational data, surgical revascularization was associated with lower subsequent stroke risk than conservative management。
- Perioperative stroke, delayed transient neurologic deficits and cerebral hyperperfusion syndrome are important complications。
- Hyperperfusion syndrome may cause headache, seizures, focal deficits, edema and rarely intracerebral hemorrhage after abrupt restoration of flow。
- Natural history tends to be progressive, but rate and extent vary substantially。
- Long-term untreated cohorts reported progressive neurologic deficits and poor outcomes in 50 to 66 percent。
- One pediatric cohort reported radiographic progression within five years in 36 percent。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Progressive distal intracranial stenosis
-> reduced cerebral blood flow and impaired perfusion reserve
-> ischemic symptoms or fragile collateral-related hemorrhage
-> hemodynamic imaging identifies tissue at risk
-> surgical revascularization augments flow to ischemic hemisphere
-> collateral pressure and recurrent event risk may decrease
-> perioperative period creates competing risks: hypoperfusion, thrombosis, hyperperfusion, hemorrhage
```

#### Inferences

- Conservative management is appropriate only when hemodynamics are favorable and surveillance is active。
- Surgical referral should be framed as risk stratification, not automatic surgery for every angiographic moyamoya pattern。
- Rehab and stroke teams should document provoked transient symptoms because they can shift a patient from surveillance into surgical evaluation。

#### Assumptions

- Diagnosis of moyamoya vasculopathy has already been established or is being managed by a cerebrovascular team。
- Perfusion imaging and vascular imaging are interpreted by teams familiar with moyamoya。
- Surgical candidacy includes neurologic recovery potential, comorbidities and patient preferences。

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Evidence for surgery is limited by observational design, baseline differences, selection bias and heterogeneous outcome definitions。
- Applicability of pediatric timing data to MMS, hemorrhagic presentations or adults is uncertain。
- Optimal surveillance interval is individualized rather than fixed。
- This source does not replace dedicated neurosurgical or institutional protocols。

### Concept: Moyamoya antithrombotic and acute supportive management

#### One-Sentence Definition

Moyamoya antithrombotic and acute supportive management separates ischemic-type antiplatelet use from hemorrhagic-type recovery, generally avoids long-term anticoagulation, and during acute stroke or perioperative care prioritizes normocapnia, euvolemia, pain control and avoidance of hypotension / hypovolemia。

#### Known Facts

- Antithrombotic decisions are complex because moyamoya carries both ischemic and hemorrhagic cerebrovascular risks。
- Antiplatelet therapy is frequently used for primary and secondary stroke prevention in moyamoya。
- For most adults with asymptomatic or symptomatic ischemic-type moyamoya, the source suggests cilostazol 100 mg twice daily over other antiplatelet agents。
- Alternatives for adults with cilostazol intolerance or contraindication include low-dose aspirin 50 to 100 mg daily or clopidogrel 75 mg daily。
- For children with asymptomatic or symptomatic ischemic-type moyamoya, the source suggests aspirin 2 to 5 mg/kg daily; pediatric cilostazol or clopidogrel data are limited。
- For most hemorrhagic-type moyamoya, antiplatelet therapy is avoided acutely and during recovery。
- After surgical revascularization, antiplatelet therapy is typically started for patients with ischemic or hemorrhagic moyamoya to support bypass graft patency。
- Antiplatelet evidence is mixed: studies suggest improved bypass patency, reduced hemorrhagic stroke and lower mortality, but not clearly reduced ischemic stroke or improved functional independence。
- Long-term anticoagulation is generally contraindicated in children and adults with moyamoya。
- Short-term anticoagulation safety is not established and should be individualized when no alternative exists。
- MMS patients may require treatment of the underlying condition; sickle cell disease-associated MMS typically requires transfusion therapy for primary and secondary stroke prevention。
- Pregnant patients should maintain adequate oral hydration; low-dose aspirin is typically used for primary or secondary stroke prevention。
- Cesarean delivery is commonly selected but has not shown clear outcome benefit over individualized delivery planning in available case-series evidence。
- Revascularization for selected pregnant patients is typically delayed until after delivery and recovery。
- Acute ischemic stroke treatment in moyamoya is mainly supportive: minimize ischemic injury, cerebral edema and secondary complications such as seizures。
- Acute supportive care includes minimizing hyperventilation from pain, excitement or crying, maintaining euvolemia and avoiding hypotension / hypovolemia。
- IV thrombolysis safety and efficacy have not been established in randomized trials; it may be considered individually for selected disabling ischemic stroke without brain hemorrhage history after risk-benefit discussion。
- Acute intracranial hemorrhage management is broadly similar to patients without moyamoya, including ventricular drainage, hematoma removal or aneurysm repair when indicated。
- Hypertension treatment may reduce future ischemic and hemorrhagic complications, but hypotension and hypovolemia should be avoided。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Moyamoya stenosis + fragile collateral vessels
-> ischemic and hemorrhagic risks coexist
-> antiplatelet may support ischemic prevention or bypass patency
-> anticoagulation increases concern for hemorrhage without proven moyamoya-specific benefit
-> pain/agitation/hyperventilation lowers CO2 and can worsen cerebral vasoconstriction
-> hypovolemia or hypotension reduces perfusion through already compromised reserve
-> acute care prioritizes stable perfusion physiology rather than aggressive routine BP lowering
```

#### Inferences

- "Stroke prevention" in moyamoya should not be automatically mapped onto standard noncardioembolic stroke algorithms。
- A hemorrhagic presentation should pause acute antiplatelet use unless a specific postoperative or specialist-directed indication exists。
- During rehabilitation, dehydration, hyperventilation-like episodes and hypotension are not minor comfort issues; they can plausibly worsen cerebral ischemia in moyamoya。

#### Assumptions

- Medication decisions account for contraindications such as heart failure for cilostazol, bleeding risk, age, pregnancy and surgical plan。
- Acute stroke care is coordinated with neurology, neurosurgery and critical care。
- The source's dosing statements are used as knowledge-base facts, not as unsupervised prescribing instructions。

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Antiplatelet data are mostly observational and do not prove reduced ischemic stroke or improved functional independence。
- Pediatric evidence for cilostazol and clopidogrel is limited。
- Thrombolysis evidence is observational and expert practice varies。
- Pregnancy and delivery data are based on limited case reports/series and systematic review of nonrandomized evidence。

## Clinically Useful Points

- Symptomatic moyamoya or impaired perfusion reserve should trigger cerebrovascular / neurosurgical referral, not only medication adjustment。
- Asymptomatic patients with preserved flow still require surveillance; absence of symptoms is not absence of progression。
- Adult ischemic-type moyamoya antiplatelet strategy in this source favors cilostazol, but alternatives and contraindications must be considered。
- Long-term anticoagulation is generally contraindicated unless a separate high-stakes indication forces individualized decision-making。
- Acute and perioperative management should avoid hyperventilation, hypovolemia, hypotension and uncontrolled pain/agitation。
- Pregnancy management is individualized; Cesarean delivery is not automatically evidence-superior。

## Research-Useful Points

- Future studies should separate MMD from MMS, adult from pediatric disease, ischemic from hemorrhagic presentation and direct/indirect/combined surgery。
- Antiplatelet outcomes should distinguish bypass patency, ischemic stroke, hemorrhagic stroke, mortality and functional independence。
- Surveillance research should test imaging interval, modality and perfusion-reserve thresholds rather than treating all asymptomatic moyamoya as homogeneous。

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- 與「moyamoya 只靠 antiplatelet 追蹤即可」衝突；symptomatic or hemodynamically compromised cases usually require surgical referral。
- 與「asymptomatic moyamoya 都不需要 follow-up」衝突；來源要求 serial surveillance。
- 與「moyamoya stroke prevention 可以比照一般 stroke 長期 anticoagulation」衝突；long-term anticoagulation generally contraindicated。
- 與「hemorrhagic moyamoya acute phase 照常 antiplatelet」衝突；來源多數情況避免急性與恢復期 antiplatelet。
- 與「pregnant moyamoya 一律 Cesarean」衝突；現有資料未證明 Cesarean outcome benefit。

# Source Summary: Upper Limb Pain and Dysfunction

## Source Type

- Textbook chapter / rehabilitation and sports medicine chapter.
- Authors listed in source: Michael Baria and Edward Laskowski.
- Single-source ingest only.

## Reliability Level

- Tier 1 in this wiki because it is a textbook-style clinical chapter.
- Strongest for examination logic, nonoperative rehabilitation staging, and referral red flags.
- Procedure-specific injection evidence and surgical indications should still be checked against updated specialty guidelines when used for real clinical decisions.

## One-Sentence Summary

Upper limb pain should be assessed as a kinetic-chain and function problem, while acute full-thickness tendon ruptures, selected wrist ligament injuries, scaphoid fracture, and unstable thumb MCP UCL injury require early surgical referral rather than prolonged conservative care.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Upper limb pain as kinetic-chain dysfunction

#### One-Sentence Definition

Upper limb pain evaluation must localize symptomatic tissue while also identifying scapulothoracic mechanics, trunk / lower limb force transfer, compensation, workload, and task demands.

#### Known Facts

- The chapter emphasizes that the upper extremity is anatomically complex and dynamic.
- Local pathology should be interpreted in the context of the kinetic chain.
- Rehabilitation diagnosis should specify acute vs chronic vs exacerbation, mechanism, overloaded tissue, direct injury, biomechanical deficits, compensation, and muscle substitutions.
- Poor force funneling through the kinetic chain can perpetuate pain or cause secondary injury.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Task demand or trauma
→ local tissue overload or rupture
→ compensation and altered force transfer
→ secondary pain and movement dysfunction
→ staged rehabilitation or urgent referral
```

#### Inferences

- A shoulder or elbow diagnosis that ignores scapular control, trunk position, and workload may treat symptoms but miss the driver.
- Sport and work-specific rehabilitation should occur only after acute pain and basic ROM / strength deficits are addressed.

### Concept: Rehabilitation staging

#### One-Sentence Definition

Upper limb rehabilitation progresses through acute symptom control, recovery of pain-free mobility / strength / proprioception, and then task-specific functional return.

#### Known Facts

- Acute stage goals include symptom reduction and tissue healing, but rest is not absolute.
- The chapter supports maintaining cardiovascular fitness, strength, flexibility, core training, and scapular stabilization when safe.
- Recovery stage emphasizes full pain-free ROM, strengthening, flexibility, strength, proprioception, and open- or closed-chain exercise as appropriate.
- Functional stage includes vocational or sport-specific progression.
- The chapter uses roughly 75-80% strength compared with the uninjured limb and absence of imbalance as a functional progression marker.

#### Inferences

- Return-to-sport or return-to-work should not be based only on time from injury.
- Stage mismatch explains many failures: too much rest delays recovery, while premature high-load task practice can flare tissue irritability.

### Concept: Rotator cuff spectrum

#### One-Sentence Definition

Rotator cuff disease is a spectrum from tendinopathy and partial tears to chronic and acute traumatic full-thickness tears, and the acute traumatic full-thickness subgroup changes referral urgency.

#### Known Facts

- Tendinopathy, partial tears, and chronic full-thickness tears are usually initially managed nonsurgically.
- Acute traumatic full-thickness tears warrant prompt MRI and surgical consultation.
- Delay as short as six weeks may lead to fatty infiltration and worse surgical outcome according to the chapter.
- Subacromial / external impingement can reflect poor scapulothoracic mechanics.
- Full can testing is preferred over empty can in the chapter because empty can may provoke impingement and is less specific for supraspinatus isolation.

#### Inferences

- "Rotator cuff pain" is not a sufficient diagnosis for management; chronicity and tear mechanism matter.

### Concept: Urgent referral patterns

#### One-Sentence Definition

Several upper limb conditions are time-sensitive because delayed diagnosis allows tendon retraction, fibrosis, instability, or degenerative collapse.

#### Known Facts

- The chapter highlights early surgical referral for acute full-thickness rotator cuff tear, pectoralis major tear, distal biceps tendon tear, scaphoid fracture, and scapholunate dissociation.
- Distal biceps rupture often follows sudden eccentric load and may have a positive Hook test; lacertus fibrosus can mimic intact tendon.
- Pectoralis major rupture is associated with eccentric force such as bench press and causes pain, pop, ecchymosis, and deformity.
- Missed scapholunate injury can progress to DISI and SLAC wrist.
- Complete thumb MCP UCL injury or Stener lesion requires surgical evaluation because interposed tissue can prevent healing.

#### Inferences

- A "try therapy first" reflex is unsafe when the source pattern matches time-sensitive tendon or ligament disruption.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Adhesive capsulitis typically restricts external rotation early and often progresses through painful, frozen, and thawing phases.
- Lateral epicondylosis is degenerative angiofibroblastic dysplasia rather than simple inflammation; eccentric wrist extensor strengthening is central.
- Olecranon bursitis should not be routinely aspirated unless septic bursitis or failed conservative care is suspected.
- UCL elbow injury in throwers requires kinetic-chain and medial stabilizer rehabilitation; surgery is not automatic even for all full-thickness injuries.
- de Quervain tenosynovitis is associated with Finkelstein test and often responds to thumb spica and corticosteroid sheath injection.

## Research-Useful Points

- Upper limb injury pages should distinguish local pathology, movement-system driver, tissue irritability, and task-specific load.
- Surgical referral timing is an important concept separate from rehabilitation progression.

## Conflicts / Cautions

- Steroid injection may give short-term pain relief in some tendon disorders but can worsen tendon quality or have poor long-term outcomes depending on condition.
- This chapter is not a replacement for acute fracture imaging rules or hand surgery guidelines.

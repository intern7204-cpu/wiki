## One-Sentence Summary

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Caregiver-facing strategy practice sequence

#### One-Sentence Definition

The participants' guide organizes CST for caregivers as a repeated sequence of learning one strategy set, applying it in everyday activities, reviewing what happened, and setting the next small goal.

#### Known Facts

- The guide is meant for caregivers participating in WHO CST.
- It provides content for core group sessions 1-9.
- It contains illustrated descriptions of key messages and tips, goal-setting activities, questions to check learning, and a local resource template.
- Caregivers who want self-directed learning outside a local CST course are directed to WHO's eLearning CST course.
- CST target audience is caregivers of children aged 2-9 years with developmental delays or disabilities, especially social and communication delays; the child does not need a diagnosis.
- CST is designed as 9 group sessions and 3 home visits delivered by trained and supervised non-specialist facilitators.
- Sessions 1-2 focus on getting and keeping children engaged in daily home activities and play.
- Session 3 focuses on building play and home routines to support engagement, communication, positive behaviour, daily living, and play skills.
- Sessions 4-5 focus on recognizing communication and creating opportunities for communication.
- Session 6 focuses on teaching everyday living skills in small steps and levels of help.
- Sessions 7-8 focus on positive behaviour and reducing challenging behaviours.
- Session 9 focuses on caregiver well-being, problem-solving, and goals for ongoing practice.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Caregiver receives a simple key message and visual tips
-> chooses everyday activities or routines
-> practises for brief repeated periods at home
-> reviews successes, barriers, and next steps
-> adds communication, play, adaptive skill, regulation, and self-care goals over time
```

#### Inferences

- The participants' guide is not a passive handout; it is a caregiver practice workbook embedded in the CST course.
- The practical unit is a daily routine plus a small strategy set, not abstract parenting knowledge.

#### Assumptions

- Caregivers can practise at least some routines at home if barriers are addressed.
- Visual examples, stories, and self-check questions can help caregivers recall and use strategies between sessions.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- This source does not quantify how much independent use of the guide without live CST support changes outcomes.
- It does not replace formal developmental, speech-language, behavioral, medical, or child protection evaluation.

### Concept: Everyday routines as caregiver-mediated learning contexts

#### One-Sentence Definition

The guide repeatedly uses everyday activities, play, and home routines as contexts where caregivers can create shared engagement, communication opportunities, adaptive-skill practice, and behaviour regulation support.

#### Known Facts

- Session 1 asks caregivers to choose two everyday activities that are motivating and occur most days, spend at least 5 minutes, and practise daily.
- Session 1 tips include setting up the space, offering 2-3 motivating choices, following the child's choice, moving in front of the child, getting to the child's level, and placing the activity between caregiver and child.
- Session 2 teaches caregivers to look and listen for the child's interests, motivation, play level, and positive behaviour.
- Session 3 asks caregivers to choose one home routine and one play or game routine, each for about 5 minutes, and use routines they can do every day.
- Session 3 tips include imitating appropriate actions, using Show and say to add a new step, and restarting routines.
- Session 6 asks caregivers to choose one small step within a larger routine and practise that task at least three times per week.
- Session 9 asks caregivers to choose the next 2-3 small steps for home routines and start teaching at least one step in the next week.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Motivating routine that occurs often
-> caregiver sets up the space and follows the child
-> caregiver joins, imitates, shows, says, waits, praises, and restarts
-> child gets repeated supported practice
-> routine expands across communication, play, daily living, and regulation goals
```

#### Inferences

- Routines are the intervention delivery vehicle, not just examples.
- Repetition in ordinary family life is how the guide tries to create practice dose.

#### Assumptions

- The selected routines are safe, motivating, and developmentally matched.
- The caregiver can keep practice short enough to remain feasible and positive.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The guide gives broad examples, but individualized routine selection still depends on child state, family resources, and facilitator support.

### Concept: Communication and challenging behaviour as teachable interaction loops

#### One-Sentence Definition

The participants' guide teaches caregivers to treat communication and challenging behaviour as interaction signals that can be shaped by responding, waiting, creating opportunities, preventing dysregulation, and teaching alternative skills.

#### Known Facts

- Session 4 states that children communicate using words, behaviour without words, and challenging behaviour.
- Session 4 tips include responding with words and gestures to all communication, matching words and gestures to the child's language level, waiting, and talking about what the child is looking at and doing.
- Session 5 tips include Show and say, repeating and expanding the child's words, creating opportunities to request, and creating opportunities to share.
- The guide warns not to withhold food or water in order to make a child request.
- Session 7 uses a behaviour thermometer with hot/red, warm/yellow, and cool/green states.
- Session 7 teaches that children can use challenging behaviour to get access, get attention, avoid or stop something, or get a sensation.
- Session 7 tips include praising appropriate behaviour, looking for signals before challenging behaviour, arranging the environment, giving warnings before transitions, staying calm during meltdown or tantrum, and thinking about the reason for behaviour afterward.
- Session 8 teaches responses based on function: communication skills for access, ignoring attention-seeking behaviour only if safe and appropriate, clear expectations for avoidance, and safe replacement behaviour for sensation-seeking behaviour.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Caregiver notices communication or early dysregulation signal
-> responds at the child's level with words, gestures, waiting, praise, or environmental support
-> creates a safe opportunity to request, share, complete a step, or regulate
-> child has a clearer alternative to challenging behaviour
```

#### Inferences

- The guide frames challenging behaviour as reason-based and teachable, not as moral failure or simple noncompliance.
- Communication intervention begins before fluent speech, because eye contact, gestures, sounds, behaviour, and challenging behaviour are all treated as possible communicative signals.

#### Assumptions

- Caregivers can identify likely behaviour reasons well enough to test prevention and response strategies.
- Safety screening is necessary before planned ignoring or any response that reduces attention.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Severe aggression, self-injury, medical pain, trauma, abuse, sleep disorder, feeding problem, or unsafe behaviour may require specialist evaluation beyond this guide.

### Concept: Caregiver well-being and problem-solving

#### One-Sentence Definition

The guide treats caregiver self-care, social connection, clear problem-solving, and ongoing goal review as part of sustaining child-focused practice.

#### Known Facts

- Session 9 focuses on caregiver well-being, problem-solving, and setting goals for ongoing practice.
- Session 9 asks caregivers to review target skills, update communication goals, identify next routines and small steps, and name one thing to improve their own well-being.
- The guide states that caregivers should take care of themselves by trying to eat well, sleep enough, take care of health, do important or enjoyable activities, and stay connected.
- Session 9 problem-solving steps include: think of many possible solutions, remove unsuitable solutions, compare advantages and disadvantages, choose at least one solution with a clear action plan, and try the plan.
- Home practice asks caregivers to plan at least 5 minutes every other day for an activity important to their own self-care.
- The guide suggests caregivers may continue contact or plan future meetings with other course participants.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Caregiver reviews child and self-care goals
-> identifies a real current problem within some personal control
-> generates and compares possible solutions
-> makes a concrete action plan
-> tries the plan and sustains practice after CST ends
```

#### Inferences

- Caregiver well-being is presented as an implementation condition for sustained child practice, not as a decorative closing topic.
- Problem-solving turns ongoing barriers into an explicit caregiver skill.

#### Assumptions

- The caregiver has at least one self-care activity that is feasible in small amounts.
- Peer support or local service contacts may be available or can be locally adapted.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The guide does not define thresholds for clinical mental health referral; severe caregiver distress still needs clinical or local service response.

## Clinically Useful Points

- CST participant materials should be judged by whether they help caregivers practise in real routines, not by whether they look comprehensive as educational handouts.
- The guide repeatedly converts strategy learning into short daily or near-daily home practice plans.
- Communication goals include requesting and sharing, and the guide treats gesture, eye contact, sounds, words, and behaviour as clinically relevant communication signals.
- For challenging behaviour, the guide asks caregivers to identify the function and respond differently depending on access, attention, avoidance, or sensation.
- Caregiver self-care and problem-solving are part of the course's sustainment strategy.
- The guide includes repeated local resource prompts for illness, abuse concern, material need, home difficulty, other family challenges, and caregiver coping difficulty.

## Research-Useful Points

- Fidelity measures for participant-facing delivery should include whether caregivers receive and use key messages, visual tips, goal-setting activities, home practice plans, and learning-check questions.
- Process evaluation should track whether caregivers can identify routines that occur most days, practise briefly, and review what worked or did not work.
- The guide supports measuring caregiver strategy recall and home-practice completion, but it does not itself validate those measures.
- Because the guide directs self-directed learners to WHO eLearning, studies of guide-only distribution should not assume it is equivalent to the full CST package.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- "The participants' guide is enough to replace CST participation" is not supported; caregivers who want self-directed learning are directed to WHO eLearning CST.
- "CST is just general parenting advice" is not supported; the guide is organized around specific key messages, tips, goal-setting, home practice, and learning checks.
- "Children with developmental delays cannot learn practical skills" is contradicted by the guide's repeated message that children can learn and develop skills with caregiver support.
- "Communication work starts only after speech appears" is not supported; the guide teaches gestures, eye contact, sounds, words, requesting, sharing, and response to unclear communication.
- "Challenging behaviour should be handled mainly by harsh discipline" is not supported; the guide uses regulation, antecedent observation, function, prevention, replacement skills, and safety checks.
- "Caregiver self-care is separate from child intervention" is not supported; the guide links caregiver well-being to sustained support for the child.

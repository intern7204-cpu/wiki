## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: CST home visit assessment-goal-coaching loop

#### One-Sentence Definition

CST home visits are structured contacts where facilitators observe the child and caregiver, set individualized targets, coach real caregiver-child practice, revise goals, and plan continued practice after the group program ends.

#### Known Facts

- The CST target audience is caregivers of children aged 2-9 years with developmental delays or disabilities, with special focus on social and communication domains; a formal child diagnosis is not required.
- The CST package is designed as 9 group sessions plus 3 home visits delivered by trained and supervised non-specialist facilitators.
- Home visit 1 occurs before session 1 and focuses on getting to know the family, assessing needs, interviewing the caregiver, administering FCI, observing CCI, and establishing goals.
- Home visit 2 should preferably occur between sessions 5 and 6, after communication-focused sessions, and focuses on re-evaluating goals and providing tailored support through guided practice and coaching.
- Home visit 3 occurs after session 9 and focuses on re-evaluating goals, consolidating selected strategies, planning longer-term practice, reminding caregiver self-care strategies, and linking the family to other supports.
- CCI is used at each home visit to observe child communication, play, behavior, caregiver interaction style, caregiver progress, and caregiver support needs.
- FCI is obligatory at home visit 1 and optional at home visit 3; it helps observe baseline and possible end-point child communication, play, and behavior.
- Goal-setting uses caregiver interview plus CCI / FCI observations to identify child targets and caregiver targets.
- Coaching is a key adult learning method in which the facilitator actively assists the caregiver during caregiver-child interaction; it differs from CCI because the facilitator does more than observe.
- The coaching component can range from minimal spoken reminders to facilitator-led modeling, depending on caregiver confidence, facilitator certainty, child response, and task difficulty.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Caregiver interview + CCI / FCI observation
-> current child skills and caregiver strategy use are identified
-> child targets and caregiver targets are written in the worksheet
-> play and home routines are selected as practice contexts
-> caregiver practises with tailored coaching and demonstration
-> targets and routines are revised across visits
-> home visit 3 converts the plan into independent ongoing practice
```

#### Inferences

- The active unit of CST home visits is not a worksheet alone, but the loop connecting observation, goal-setting, practice, feedback, revision, and ongoing routine use.
- Home visits are the individualized counterpart to CST group sessions: they translate general strategies into family-specific routines, materials, caregiver strengths, child interests, and practical barriers.
- Removing home visits would likely weaken the pathway from generic group learning to context-sensitive caregiver behavior change.

#### Assumptions

- Facilitators have received practical CST training and ongoing supervision.
- Families can participate in home-based observation and guided practice if access, privacy, timing, and caregiver stress are handled appropriately.
- CCI / FCI observations are used for goal-setting and coaching decisions, not treated as stand-alone diagnostic evaluations.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- This manual does not quantify the independent outcome effect of each home visit component.
- It does not replace formal developmental, speech-language, behavioral, medical, or child protection evaluation when those are clinically indicated.
- The source does not define how to adapt home visits when home access is unsafe, impossible, or culturally unacceptable beyond referring to adaptation and supervision processes.

### Concept: Individualized targets and routines

#### One-Sentence Definition

CST home visit goal-setting converts child communication, play, daily living, behavior regulation, family priorities, and caregiver strategy use into a small set of target routines for practice.

#### Known Facts

- Child target domains include communication, play, daily living skills, and behaviour regulation.
- Caregiver targets focus on use of CST intervention strategies.
- Communication targets include both gestures and spoken language, with separate attention to requesting and sharing.
- Play targets should not be selected as mastered just because a child briefly shows a higher-level play action once; sustained routine-level play matters.
- Daily living targets come from caregiver interview and observed home routines.
- Behaviour regulation targets should focus on high-impact dysregulated or challenging behaviors, with attention to when, what happened before, what happened after, and frequency.
- Family activities and priorities can include meaningful everyday tasks such as washing dishes, setting the table, sorting, watering, or cleaning.
- The intervention strategies chart groups caregiver targets into environmental strategies, look and listen, routine building, understanding communication, responding to communication, teaching new skills, behaviour regulation, response to challenging behaviour, and expanding routines.
- The target routines chart selects three play routines and three home routines, with communication targets embedded in both.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Observed child skill level + caregiver priorities
-> specific child and caregiver targets
-> three play routines and three home routines
-> repeated everyday practice across communication, play, adaptive skill, and regulation goals
```

#### Inferences

- Goal-setting is a scaffolded clinical reasoning process, not simply asking the caregiver for a wish list.
- Routines function as the practice container that lets one activity carry several targets at once.

#### Assumptions

- The chosen routines occur often enough to generate practice dose.
- The target step is neither so easy that the child disengages nor so difficult that the child becomes frustrated or dysregulated.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The guide gives a structured process, but target selection still depends on facilitator skill, caregiver report accuracy, child state on the visit day, and available home materials.

### Concept: Safeguarding and referral boundary in CST home visits

#### One-Sentence Definition

CST home visits include screening for additional family needs and require facilitator supervision or referral when child health, caregiver health, mental health, maltreatment, neglect, abuse, or material deprivation exceeds CST scope.

#### Known Facts

- Each home visit includes assessment of family needs.
- Facilitators are instructed to look for child health or caregiver health issues, caregiver mental health challenges, potential child maltreatment, and material deprivation.
- Facilitators should ask whether the family needs referral to other services.
- The guide lists warning features for physical abuse, sexual abuse, neglect, emotional or other maltreatment, and concerning caregiver-child interaction.
- The guide instructs facilitators to consult their supervisor whenever the guide is insufficient, the family has complex needs, neglect or abuse is suspected or identified, or early facilitator experience requires routine supervision.
- For suspected or identified child abuse, facilitators are told to inform the supervisor and not carry the responsibility alone.
- For neglect concerns, the guide instructs facilitators to inform the supervisor and involve local community health worker follow-up where available.
- If caregiver stress appears severe, the guide recommends referral to a local health worker or health centre.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Home context observation
-> additional needs or safeguarding signs are noticed
-> facilitator consults supervisor
-> family is referred or linked to local supports when needs exceed CST
-> CST remains a caregiver training program, not an unsupported substitute for protection, health, or social services
```

#### Inferences

- The home visit model assumes CST is embedded in a broader service and safeguarding pathway.
- A CST program without supervision and referral routes would be structurally unsafe for high-risk families.

#### Assumptions

- Local referral options, child protection procedures, and health worker pathways exist or have been mapped during implementation.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The manual names warning features and escalation principles, but the exact legal reporting process depends on local jurisdiction and implementation planning.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Do not describe CST home visits as optional social visits; the manual uses them to individualize goals, observe real interaction, coach caregivers, and support ongoing practice.
- CCI / FCI should be interpreted as structured observation tools for planning and supervision, not as a diagnostic substitute.
- Home visit 2 timing matters because it is intended to follow communication sessions; research sites should prefer the same timing across participants and record timing.
- Coaching should be titrated: too little support can make the caregiver struggle, while too much support can feel intrusive.
- Goal-setting should move from broad caregiver hopes to small next steps that fit the child's current communication, play, daily living, and regulation level.
- Maltreatment, neglect, caregiver mental health concern, material deprivation, and severe caregiver distress are supervision/referral issues, not problems for an isolated facilitator to handle alone.

## Research-Useful Points

- Fidelity evaluation should track whether each home visit completed assessment, goal-setting, coached practice, demonstration, target revision, and independent-practice planning.
- Research implementation should record home visit 2 timing because the guide expresses a preferred timing window.
- Video recording is helpful for clinical monitoring and supervision, and required or potentially required in research contexts depending on protocol.
- Process measures should distinguish observation-only CCI from active coaching.
- The goal-setting worksheet can operationalize whether targets are individualized across child domains, caregiver strategy domains, and target routines.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- "CST home visits are optional follow-up" is not supported by this source; they are part of the standard 9 group sessions plus 3 home visits structure.
- "Goal-setting means promising the caregiver's long-term hope" is not supported; the guide emphasizes small next steps matched to observed current skill level.
- "Coaching is just observing caregiver-child interaction" is not supported; coaching requires active facilitator adjustment, modeling, or support.
- "CCI / FCI are diagnostic assessments" is not established; the guide describes them as clinical tools for observing interaction, goal-setting, and supervision.
- "CST can absorb safeguarding needs without referral" is not supported; the guide repeatedly requires supervision and referral when risk or complex needs appear.

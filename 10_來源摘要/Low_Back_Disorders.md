# Source Summary: Low Back Disorders

- Textbook chapter / clinical rehabilitation chapter.
- Authors listed in source: Sanjog S. Pangarkar, Melissa E. Phuphanich, and Udai Nanda.
- Single-source ingest only.

## Reliability Level

## One-Sentence Summary

Low back pain workflow should first triage red and yellow flags, distinguish nonspecific LBP from radiculopathy and stenosis, and reserve imaging or procedures for cases where results will change management.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Low back pain triage

#### One-Sentence Definition

LBP assessment starts with time course, red flags, neurologic pattern, psychosocial risk, function, and clinical context rather than routine early MRI.

#### Known Facts

- LBP affects at least 80% of people and is a leading cause of disability.
- Acute LBP is 0-4 weeks, subacute is 4-12 weeks, and chronic is more than 12 weeks.
- Most acute LBP improves quickly; 75-90% recover or return to work within 3-4 weeks according to the chapter.
- Nonspecific LBP accounts for about 85% of care-seeking cases in the chapter.
- Red flags include young age with significant pain, new onset after age 55, violent trauma, nonmechanical constant / night pain, cancer history, systemic steroids, drug abuse, HIV / immunocompromised status, weight loss, fever / night sweats, severe motion restriction, deformity, bladder / bowel / saddle symptoms, progressive motor weakness, gait disturbance, and inflammatory features.
- Yellow flags include catastrophizing, fear-avoidance, poor sleep, compensation context, anxiety, depression, poor job satisfaction, and low expectation for recovery.

#### Inferences

- A red flag is a triage signal, not proof of serious pathology.
- Yellow flags are not "psychogenic pain"; they are disability and chronicity risk factors.

### Concept: Imaging intent

#### One-Sentence Definition

Lumbar imaging should be ordered when red flags, persistent symptoms, neurologic findings, or treatment decisions make the result actionable.

#### Known Facts

- Guidelines cited in the chapter recommend against MRI for acute uncomplicated LBP.
- MRI has high sensitivity but low specificity because asymptomatic abnormalities are common.
- Early MRI within six weeks is associated in cited studies with more surgery, cost, opioid use, and worse pain.
- Plain radiographs are used for trauma and red-flag contexts but are low-sensitivity / low-specificity screening tools.
- EMG can help distinguish radiculopathy from mononeuropathy or polyneuropathy but does not assess radicular pain or sensory-root dysfunction well.

#### Inferences

- Imaging without a management question can create diagnostic anchoring and overtreatment.
- Imaging reports should be interpreted against symptom location, neurologic exam, and time course.

### Concept: Lumbosacral radiculopathy

#### One-Sentence Definition

Lumbosacral radicular pain is often driven by inflamed or chemically irritated nerve roots, not just mechanical compression visible on imaging.

#### Known Facts

- Radicular symptoms can result from mechanical compression or chemical inflammation.
- Normal nerve roots do not necessarily produce pain with mechanical stimulation, while inflamed roots can.
- Disc protrusion is a common cause.
- Cauda equina syndrome is a surgical emergency with bowel, bladder, sexual dysfunction, saddle anesthesia, or severe neurologic deficits.
- Persistent radicular symptoms despite maximal conservative care for 6-8 weeks, progressive neurologic deficit, or cauda equina syndrome are surgical thresholds described in the chapter.
- Lumbar epidural steroid injections may provide short-term benefit, especially for radicular pain due to disc herniation, but long-term benefit is limited.

#### Inferences

- Lack of perfect dermatomal distribution does not exclude radicular pain.
- Back pain dominant cases have less predictable surgical benefit than leg pain dominant cases.

### Concept: Lumbar spinal stenosis

#### One-Sentence Definition

Lumbar spinal stenosis becomes clinically meaningful when canal narrowing produces neurogenic claudication, classically worsened by standing / walking / extension and relieved by sitting / flexion.

#### Known Facts

- Not all lumbar narrowing is symptomatic.
- Neurogenic claudication is classically back and bilateral leg pain with walking, standing, downhill walking, or extension, relieved by sitting or flexion.
- The "grocery cart sign" is a clinical clue.
- Natural history under conservative management is often stable; the chapter cites four-year conservative outcomes with 70% unchanged, 15% improved, and 15% worsened.
- Conservative care includes pain control, function improvement, flexion-based stabilization, abdominal / pelvic / gluteal strengthening, hip anterior muscle stretching, and aerobic training.

#### Inferences

- Walking tolerance and posture dependence are more clinically relevant than canal diameter alone.
- Axial LBP dominant patients may have poorer surgical response than leg-dominant neurogenic claudication patients.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Education should reassure patients without serious pathology and encourage staying active / returning to work.
- Exercise has moderate-certainty benefit for chronic LBP, but no single best type is established.
- NSAIDs have modest benefit and are first-line pharmacologic therapy for acute / subacute LBP in the chapter; acetaminophen alone has high-certainty evidence of no acute LBP benefit in cited evidence.
- Duloxetine is supported for chronic musculoskeletal LBP and neuropathic pain in the chapter.
- Gabapentin is not effective for chronic axial LBP in the source framing.
- Multidisciplinary biopsychosocial rehabilitation is more effective than isolated standard treatments for chronic LBP.

## Research-Useful Points

- LBP pages should separate nonspecific LBP, radiculopathy, stenosis, facet-mediated pain, discogenic pain, and serious pathology.
- Imaging-related nocebo language is a useful concept for future patient-education notes.

## Conflicts / Cautions

- The source warns that red flags are common and nonspecific; absence of red flags also does not fully exclude malignancy.
- Discography remains controversial and should not be treated as routine.

## One-Sentence Summary

Degenerative movement disorders should first be classified by movement phenomenology; Parkinson disease is a clinical alpha-synucleinopathy syndrome with motor and nonmotor disability, while PSP, MSA, and CBGD require early recognition because they mimic PD but differ in prognosis, levodopa response, autonomic / cognitive / gaze features, and care priorities.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Movement disorder 現象學分類

#### One-Sentence Definition

Movement disorders are neurologic syndromes of excessive movement or paucity of voluntary and autonomic movement, unrelated to weakness or spasticity.

#### Known Facts

- Movement disorders can be classified as hyperkinetic or hypokinetic disorders.
- Hyperkinetic disorders include RLS, tremor, dystonia, myoclonus, chorea, and tics in this chapter.
- Hypokinetic disorders include Parkinson disease and Parkinson-plus syndromes.
- Tremor is rhythmic oscillatory movement from alternating or synchronous contraction of antagonistic muscle pairs.
- Dystonia involves sustained or intermittent painful muscle contractions causing twisting, repetitive movement, or prolonged abnormal posture.
- Chorea is irregular, involuntary, unpredictable, purposeless movement that flows between body parts.
- Functional tremor shows abrupt onset, inconsistency, variability, distractibility, and possible entrainment.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Extrapyramidal / basal ganglia / cerebellar / network dysfunction
-> abnormal movement amount, rhythm, suppressibility, trigger, and task specificity
-> phenomenologic classification
-> targeted differential diagnosis
```

#### Inferences

- Repeated examination and video recording are useful when mixed movements make classification uncertain.
- Correctly naming the movement pattern prevents treating bradykinesia as weakness or rigidity as spasticity.

#### Assumptions

- Observation includes rest, posture, action, gait, distraction, and task-specific triggers.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- This chapter provides a clinical overview, not a full movement disorder diagnostic atlas.

### Concept: Parkinson disease diagnosis and mimic exclusion

#### One-Sentence Definition

PD diagnosis is largely clinical and centers on resting tremor, bradykinesia, rigidity, and postural instability, while excluding drug-induced, toxin-induced, vascular, NPH, ET, and Parkinson-plus mimics.

#### Known Facts

- PD is associated with progressive loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra pars compacta and alpha-synuclein intracellular inclusions called Lewy bodies.
- Cardinal motor signs include resting tremor, bradykinesia, rigidity, and postural instability.
- Bradykinesia is slowness with progressive decrement in speed or amplitude during rapid alternating movement.
- Rigidity is velocity-independent increased tone, unlike spasticity.
- Typical PD gait includes shuffling, reduced arm swing, stooped posture, narrow base, freezing, festination, and postural instability.
- PD diagnosis is largely clinical; the source states no laboratory testing or imaging is definitive.
- Medication review is crucial because antipsychotics, metoclopramide, amiodarone, lithium, and reserpine can cause drug-induced parkinsonism.
- DAT scan may help in rare unclear cases involving ET vs PD, but alpha-synuclein tests cannot distinguish PD from LBD or MSA.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Substantia nigra dopaminergic neuron loss + alpha-synuclein pathology
-> basal ganglia circuit dysfunction
-> bradykinesia, resting tremor, rigidity, gait / axial disturbance
-> clinical PD syndrome
-> mimic exclusion and longitudinal confirmation
```

#### Inferences

- A "PD-like" label without medication review, vascular context, and atypical red flag screen is incomplete.
- Biomarkers can support alpha-synucleinopathy but do not replace syndrome-level diagnosis.

#### Assumptions

- Exam includes direct observation of rest tremor, postural/action tremor, rapid alternating movement decrement, rigidity, gait, turning, and pull test when safe.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Early PD, ET with rest tremor, drug-induced parkinsonism, and atypical parkinsonism may overlap clinically.

### Concept: PD management, rehabilitation, and nonmotor burden

#### One-Sentence Definition

PD management combines levodopa-centered motor symptom treatment, device-aided options in selected patients, rehabilitation, nonmotor symptom treatment, and caregiver support.

#### Known Facts

- Levodopa remains the most effective drug for PD motor symptoms and is considered the gold standard in the source.
- Pharmacologic treatment becomes necessary when PD motor symptoms interfere with ADL and quality of life.
- Levodopa should be prescribed at the lowest effective dose, and patients should be monitored for dyskinesias, nausea, impulse control disorders, hallucinations, excessive daytime sleepiness, and orthostatic hypotension worsening.
- High-protein meals can reduce levodopa absorption and efficacy.
- Wearing-off can occur because of levodopa's short half-life.
- DBS is part of comprehensive management for selected patients with disabling drug-related movements and motor fluctuations after medical management is exhausted.
- Physical therapy is described as likely efficacious and clinically useful for PD motor symptoms in the cited MDS-EBM update.
- Nonmotor symptoms include psychiatric, cognitive, autonomic, sleep, and special sensory symptoms and are major determinants of quality of life, nursing home placement, and disability.
- Caregivers assist with ADL, safety, medication management, organization, transportation, social involvement, history, and treatment observation.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Progressive motor + nonmotor PD burden
-> ADL, gait, swallowing, cognition, mood, autonomic, sleep, and caregiver load
-> medication timing + rehabilitation + safety adaptation + nonmotor treatment
-> preserved participation and home/community living as long as feasible
```

#### Inferences

- Treating only tremor and rigidity misses disability drivers such as depression, executive dysfunction, orthostatic hypotension, dysphagia, and caregiver strain.
- Rehabilitation should be timed around medication response and real ADL problems rather than generic exercise only.

#### Assumptions

- Motor fluctuation and dyskinesia history are tracked in relation to medication schedule.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The chapter cites insufficient or variable evidence for several specific exercise formats, technology-based strategies, OT, and video-assisted swallowing therapy.

### Concept: Parkinson-plus syndrome red flags

#### One-Sentence Definition

Parkinson-plus syndromes mimic PD but are suggested by early falls or gaze palsy in PSP, early severe autonomic failure or cerebellar signs in MSA, and asymmetric apraxia / cortical sensory loss / alien limb features in CBGD.

#### Known Facts

- PSP is an adult-onset rapidly progressive syndrome with axial rigidity, bradykinesia, postural instability with falls, cognitive deficits, and supranuclear vertical gaze palsy.
- PSP is a common cause of levodopa-nonresponsive parkinsonism.
- The most common initial PSP feature is gait disturbance and falling, typically within 12 months of onset.
- MSA is an adult-onset rare, rapidly progressive fatal disorder with autonomic failure, poor levodopa-responsive parkinsonism, cerebellar ataxia, and pyramidal signs.
- MSA has no definitive diagnostic test; central clinical elements are autonomic dysfunction with cerebellar ataxia or poorly levodopa-responsive parkinsonism.
- MSA median survival is 9 years in the source.
- CBGD has tau deposition and neuronal cell loss; clinical features include dystonia, postural instability, athetosis, orofacial dyskinesia, apraxia, cortical sensory loss, alien limb phenomenon, dementia, and frontal lobe reflexes.
- CBGD is notably asymmetric and has no specific treatment in the source.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Non-PD neurodegenerative pathology
-> parkinsonism plus gaze / autonomic / cerebellar / cortical / asymmetric features
-> poor or atypical levodopa response and faster functional decline
-> prognosis-aware rehabilitation and supportive planning
```

#### Inferences

- Early falls, early dysphagia, early autonomic failure, poor levodopa response, and cortical signs should trigger reconsideration of idiopathic PD.
- Parkinson-plus recognition changes counseling, safety planning, referral urgency, and caregiver preparation.

#### Assumptions

- Longitudinal observation is available because early overlap with PD can be substantial.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Definitive PSP diagnosis is neuropathologic; MSA and CBGD also lack a simple definitive clinical test in routine care.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Movement disorder assessment begins with phenomenology: rest vs action tremor, rhythmicity, suppressibility, distractibility, task specificity, gait pattern, autonomic findings, cognition, and medication exposure.
- Bradykinesia may be reported by patients as weakness even when strength is normal.
- Rigidity differs from spasticity because it is velocity independent.
- PD nonmotor symptoms can dominate disability and should be actively screened.
- DBS candidates require levodopa responsiveness and should not have a Parkinson-plus syndrome or major interfering medical conditions in the source.

## Research-Useful Points

- The chapter highlights a lack of evidence-based, patient-specific rehabilitative interventions for movement disorders.
- Anecdotal exercise studies suggest possible quality-of-life benefit, but stronger disease-specific rehabilitation evidence is still needed.

## Conflicts / Cautions

- "Tremor equals PD" is wrong; action tremor, enhanced physiologic tremor, ET, cerebellar tremor, medication tremor, and functional tremor have different patterns.
- "PD diagnosis can be confirmed by one test" is wrong in this source.
- "Levodopa response proves idiopathic PD" is incomplete; some vascular parkinsonism and MSA cases may show atypical or partial response.
- "PSP requires visible vertical gaze palsy" is too strict; the source warns this may delay diagnosis.

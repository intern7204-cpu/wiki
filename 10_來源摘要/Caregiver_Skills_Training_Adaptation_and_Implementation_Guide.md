## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Caregiver Skills Training CST course structure

#### One-Sentence Definition

CST is a caregiver-mediated training package for families of children aged 2-9 years with developmental delays or disabilities, especially social and communication delays, delivered through structured group sessions and home visits.

#### Known Facts

- Intended participants are caregivers of children aged 2-9 years with developmental delays or disabilities.
- The guide has a specific focus on children with delays and impairments in social and communication domains.
- A child does not need to have a diagnosis for the package to be used.
- The course is designed as 9 group sessions and 3 home visits delivered by trained and supervised non-specialist facilitators.
- Additional sessions or home visits may be used for caregiver well-being or children with little or no spoken language.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Structured caregiver training
-> caregivers learn engagement, regulation, play, communication, adaptive-skill, behavior, and problem-solving strategies
-> caregivers practice strategies in group and home contexts
-> child daily routines become more developmentally supportive
```

#### Inferences

- CST should be treated as an implementation package with training, supervision, practice, documentation, and local service mapping, not as a handout or generic parenting advice.
- Because no diagnosis is required, CST may fit service systems where developmental concern is clear but formal diagnosis is delayed.

#### Assumptions

- The local system has access to trained and supervised facilitators or can build that workforce.
- Caregivers can attend if practical barriers are addressed.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- This source does not provide a full efficacy review.
- The age range can be adjusted slightly, but the guide does not define a broad evidence boundary outside the intended 2-9 year range.

### Concept: Cultural adaptation with fidelity

#### One-Sentence Definition

Cultural adaptation means changing CST materials and delivery enough to fit local language, culture, resources, and barriers, without removing the components likely to make the intervention work.

#### Known Facts

- Adaptation should ensure that the package is comprehensible, culturally acceptable, locally relevant, and feasible.
- Recommended material adaptations include translation, local vocabulary, literacy matching, explanation of technical terms, local names, examples, toys, games, activities, and culturally familiar settings.
- Recommended process adaptations include schedule, setting, childcare, transportation support, refreshments, welcome or closing rituals, and other attendance supports.
- The guide repeatedly recommends making only essential adaptations at first, documenting the rationale, and revising after pilot-testing and feedback.
- Significant changes such as changing the number of sessions, removing content, or shifting from group to individual delivery require careful consultation, documentation, and evaluation.
- The adaptation documentation form is based on Bernal framework categories including language, persons, metaphors, content, concepts, goals, methods, and context.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Local adaptation team + stakeholder input + pre-pilot feedback
-> identify language, culture, access, and feasibility barriers
-> make minimal documented changes
-> preserve core strategies and teaching methods
-> improve acceptability without losing intervention fidelity
```

#### Inferences

- Local adaptation should be a controlled implementation process, not ad hoc editing by whoever translates the manual.
- Fidelity and fit are not opposites; fidelity protects the active ingredients, while adaptation protects access and meaning.

#### Assumptions

- The adaptation team can distinguish essential local changes from changes that weaken core components.
- There is enough implementation capacity to document and evaluate adaptations.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The guide gives process safeguards but does not quantify how much adaptation is safe before efficacy changes.

### Concept: Implementation barriers and service integration

#### One-Sentence Definition

CST implementation requires mapping local services, legal frameworks, workforce capacity, referral options, attendance barriers, and stigma before broad delivery.

#### Known Facts

- The project manager should conduct a situational analysis of mental health, family, and child development services.
- Where possible, CST should be embedded into existing services.
- Local legal frameworks, including child protection reporting pathways, should be reviewed.
- A caregiver resource list should include health services, clinics, NGOs, food, housing, respite, charities, child protection, intimate partner violence, substance use, mental health, legal assistance, immigration, and shelter resources as locally relevant.
- Implementation barriers may include transportation, childcare, food, scheduling, caregiver workload, stigma, and lack of second-caregiver involvement.
- Standard practice is to invite any two caregivers per family, not only mothers or only fathers, unless this would disempower the primary caregiver.
- Community awareness, stigma reduction, and disability advocacy may be needed before delivery in some settings.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Service mapping and barrier reduction
-> caregivers can attend and remain in the course
-> facilitators can refer beyond CST when needs exceed the program
-> CST becomes part of a care pathway rather than an isolated class
```

#### Inferences

- Attendance design is part of the intervention, because missed sessions reduce exposure to practice and feedback.
- CST cannot safely operate without referral and safeguarding pathways for illness, maltreatment, family violence, caregiver distress, and material need.

#### Assumptions

- Local services exist or can at least be mapped honestly, including gaps.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The guide does not solve resource scarcity; it only requires that scarcity be identified and considered in implementation planning.

### Concept: Core components and modification limits

#### One-Sentence Definition

Core CST components are the content, teaching methods, and implementation logistics that should not be changed without expert consultation and evaluation.

#### Known Facts

- The guide identifies core components as elements thought to be essential to CST effectiveness.
- Examples of content changes not recommended without consultation include changing or removing key messages or tips, removing group activities, sessions, home visits, guided practice, goal-setting, or home practice.
- Examples of instructional changes not recommended without consultation include altering group discussions, pair work, home-practice intensity, learning activities, or live demonstrations.
- Examples of implementation changes not recommended without consultation include changing facilitator-to-participant ratio, session sequence, or the target population definition.
- If a brief version is unavoidable, sessions 1 and 2 should almost always be delivered because they introduce the course and foundational skills.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Core content + active learning + home practice + guided home visits
-> caregiver skill acquisition and real-life use
-> lower risk that adaptation becomes only informational education
```

#### Inferences

- Removing guided practice or live demonstration likely changes CST from skills training into passive education, which is a different intervention.

#### Assumptions

- The original package's core components are valid active ingredients, as the guide states but does not independently prove.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The source does not provide direct comparative data on every possible adaptation.

## Clinically Useful Points

- CST may be useful when a child has developmental delay or disability, especially social-communication difficulty, even before a formal diagnosis is available.
- A referral or service plan should not simply say "parent education"; it should specify whether there is a structured CST-like program, facilitator training, supervision, home practice, and follow-up.
- If caregivers cannot attend because of childcare, transportation, schedule, stigma, or caregiver overload, the plan has not addressed implementation.
- Child protection, family violence, caregiver mental health, material insecurity, and health-service access should be mapped before CST is treated as enough support.

## Research-Useful Points

- Local CST adaptation should document language, metaphors, concepts, content, methods, context, rationale, evidence source, date, and adaptation phase.
- Pre-pilot testing should include participant attendance, fidelity checklists, participant/facilitator/observer feedback, focus groups or interviews, and analysis of barriers and facilitators.
- Significant structural redesign, including group-to-individual delivery, needs evaluation because it may change active learning and fidelity.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- "CST is just parent advice" is not supported by this source.
- "Cultural adaptation means freely rewriting the program" is not supported.
- "High fidelity means no local adaptation" is not supported.
- "Inviting only mothers is neutral" is not supported; the guide frames single-caregiver invitation as potentially structurally discriminatory unless justified by local power dynamics.
- "Brief delivery is harmless" is not established; the guide warns that core sessions and components should be preserved when possible.

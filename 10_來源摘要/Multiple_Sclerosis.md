## One-Sentence Summary

Multiple sclerosis is a chronic inflammatory and neurodegenerative CNS disorder in which early diagnosis and DMT reduce inflammatory activity, but long-term function depends heavily on rehabilitation management of gait, spasticity, fatigue, heat sensitivity, cognition, bladder / bowel / sexual dysfunction, pain, swallowing, and participation.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: MS 診斷與 mimic 排除

#### One-Sentence Definition

MS diagnosis requires CNS demyelinating events disseminated in space and time, supported by clinical, MRI, and sometimes CSF / evoked potential data, with no better explanation.

#### Known Facts

- MS is a chronic inflammatory neurodegenerative CNS disorder and a common cause of nontraumatic disability in young adults.
- Relapse requires typical CNS demyelinating symptoms lasting at least 24 hours in the absence of fever or infection.
- DIS can be demonstrated by T2 lesions in at least two of five CNS areas in 2017 McDonald criteria: periventricular, juxtacortical, cortical, infratentorial, and spinal cord.
- DIT can be demonstrated by new T2 / gadolinium-enhancing lesion on follow-up MRI or simultaneous enhancing and nonenhancing lesions.
- CSF-specific oligoclonal bands can be used to meet DIT when clinical or MRI criteria are incomplete.
- Diagnosis requires no better explanation.
- NMOSD, ADEM, MOGAD, acute transverse myelitis, Lyme disease, systemic autoimmune disease, migraine, vascular disease, and metabolic disorders can mimic MS.
- EDSS is widely used to monitor disability; EDSS <=4 implies minimal disability with no ambulation restriction, 6-8 moderate disability with assistive device / near nonambulatory progression, and >8 severe disability / bed restriction in the source.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
CNS immune-mediated inflammation and demyelination
→ focal / multifocal neurologic episodes
→ MRI / CSF / evoked potential evidence of DIS and DIT
→ mimic exclusion
→ MS diagnosis and treatment planning
```

#### Inferences

- Meeting imaging criteria is insufficient if the clinical syndrome is atypical or a mimic better explains the presentation.
- MRI also functions as longitudinal treatment-response monitoring, not only initial diagnosis.

#### Assumptions

- Clinical attacks are objectively supported by examination, visual evoked potential, or MRI when historical evidence is used.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- This chapter discusses diagnostic criteria but does not replace specialist judgment for NMOSD / MOGAD / atypical demyelinating syndromes.

### Concept: MS 步態與 spasticity 復健框架

#### One-Sentence Definition

MS mobility care should quantify walking, tone, weakness, endurance, fall risk, and dynamic spasticity, then combine exercise, therapy, orthotic / assistive strategies, pharmacologic options, BoNT, and ITB when needed.

#### Known Facts

- Mobility impairment affects about 75% of PwMS in the source.
- PwMS can show decreased walking speed and step length, increased step width, decreased hip extension in stance, decreased knee flexion in swing, decreased ankle dorsiflexion at initial contact, and decreased plantarflexion in preswing.
- T25FW is a validated measure of walking speed; 20% change is considered clinically meaningful.
- Exercise training has not shown increased relapse or adverse events in cited meta-review / systematic analysis.
- Spasticity affects up to 85% of PwMS; lower limb spasticity is much more common than upper limb spasticity.
- Spasticity assessment should include dynamic observation of gait and cocontraction, not only seated tone.
- Oral baclofen is usual first-line oral agent in the source; BoNT and ITB are important options when systemic side effects or severity limit function.
- ITB has demonstrated improvements in spasticity, quality of life, sleep, bowel / bladder performance, skin integrity, and ambulation in cited studies.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
CNS demyelination and tract injury
→ weakness, spasticity, sensory / cerebellar impairment, fatigue
→ altered gait and mobility restriction
→ deconditioning, falls, reduced independence
→ measured gait + tone management + exercise and equipment strategy
```

#### Inferences

- MS gait management is not just "more walking"; it needs gait metric, spasticity pattern, fatigue, heat tolerance, and assistive technology matching.
- DMT complexity can crowd out symptom management; physiatry fills that gap.

#### Assumptions

- Mobility plan is adjusted to MS subtype, relapse status, disability level, and symptom fluctuation.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Dalfampridine response is limited to a subset; medication decisions need current prescribing criteria and renal / seizure safety review.

### Concept: MS fatigue, heat sensitivity, and exercise dosing

#### One-Sentence Definition

MS fatigue and heat sensitivity require separating primary CNS fatigue from secondary causes, then prescribing activity with cooling, pacing, sleep / mood / medication review, and graded exercise rather than avoidance.

#### Known Facts

- Fatigue is one of the most common and often most disabling MS symptoms.
- Fatigue may be peripheral fatigability or central subjective exhaustion / lassitude.
- Secondary fatigue factors include thermosensitivity, mood disorder, anxiety, sleep disturbance, infection, thyroid dysfunction, anemia, and medication effects.
- Fatigue Severity Scale, Fatigue Impact Scale, and Modified Fatigue Impact Scale are commonly used.
- Heat sensitivity / Uhthoff phenomenon is transient worsening of neurologic function with heat exposure, physical exhaustion, infection, or dehydration.
- Heat-induced fatigue affects approximately 58-93% of PwMS in the source.
- Exercise improves quality of life in PwMS with no evidence of deleterious effects in the cited review.
- Recommended exercise must account for symptoms, heat intolerance, fall risk, strength, and endurance.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
CNS demyelination + impaired thermoregulation + secondary contributors
→ reduced conduction reserve, fatigue, cognitive / motor worsening
→ activity avoidance or overexertion cycles
→ pacing, cooling, secondary-cause treatment, graded exercise
→ better participation and quality of life
```

#### Inferences

- Treating depression, sleep disorder, nocturia, infection, anemia, thyroid disease, or medication side effects may improve "MS fatigue" without changing DMT.
- Exercise avoidance due to old fear of overheating can worsen deconditioning.

#### Assumptions

- Relapse, infection, and medication adverse effects have been checked when fatigue suddenly worsens.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Pharmacologic fatigue management has variable response and requires patient-specific trial and monitoring.

## Clinically Useful Points

- DMT should be offered to everyone diagnosed with MS, especially RRMS, with goals of preventing relapse, reducing neurologic impairment accumulation, and reducing neuronal injury.
- DMT selection is shaped by poor-prognosis features, patient values, route, risk tolerance, adherence likelihood, infection / PML risk, pregnancy planning, and monitoring burden.
- MRI with T1 with and without contrast, T2, and FLAIR helps diagnosis and monitoring; STIR can better show spinal cord lesions.
- Gadolinium-enhancing lesions show active blood-brain barrier disruption but correlate weakly with disability.
- T1 black holes correlate with chronic axonal damage and cognitive function.
- NLUTD affects up to 75% of PwMS; screen with history and postvoid residual.
- Sexual dysfunction is common and under-asked; clinicians should ask directly.
- Dysphagia may occur at any MS stage and clinical assessment alone may underestimate aspiration risk.

## Research-Useful Points

- Progressive disability remains a major cost driver and rehabilitation target.
- Cognitive reserve and brain reserve may modify cognitive impairment.
- Pediatric MS has larger lesion burden, more brainstem / cerebellar involvement, higher relapse rate, and rapid recovery, but DMT data remain limited.

## Conflicts / Cautions

- McDonald criteria can be met by mimics; "no better explanation" is mandatory.
- MS can be diagnosed clinically in classic cases, but negative imaging / CSF should prompt extreme caution before diagnosis.
- Exercise is beneficial and generally safe, but heat sensitivity and fall risk must be actively managed.
- Progressive MS without active disease in older adults may be managed symptomatically in some cases according to the source, but this is specialist-level decision making.

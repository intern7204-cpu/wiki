# Source Summary: Treatment of vascular cognitive impairment and dementia

## Source Type

UpToDate topic review。Literature review current through 2026-04；topic last updated 2024-09-17。

## Reliability Level

Tier 6。此來源適合整理 adult vascular cognitive impairment / vascular dementia 的 practical management boundary。它不是 full dementia guideline，也不是 stroke secondary prevention guideline；文中多處把詳細 stroke prevention、dementia supportive care、behavioral symptom management 轉介到其他 topic。

## One-Sentence Summary

Vascular cognitive impairment / vascular dementia management 應先以 cerebrovascular pathology 為核心處理 vascular risk and recurrent stroke prevention，再非常謹慎地選擇 antithrombotic 與 cognitive medication；cholinesterase inhibitors 只有 slight / uncertain benefit，memantine 不建議 routine 使用，nonpharmacologic / rehabilitation evidence 對 cognition 不強，prognosis heterogeneous。

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: VCI vascular risk and antithrombotic boundary

#### One-Sentence Definition

在 vascular cognitive impairment / vascular dementia 中，vascular risk modification 和 stroke subtype-based secondary prevention 是核心，但 antithrombotic therapy 要依 clinical stroke/TIA 或 imaging infarction 分流，不能因 white matter lesions alone 就使用。

#### Known Facts

- VaD refers to dementia primarily caused by cerebrovascular disease or impaired cerebral blood flow。
- Vascular cognitive impairment includes all cognitive disorders in which cerebrovascular disease or impaired cerebral blood flow contributes causally。
- Patients with cognitive impairment and clinical or radiologic evidence of cerebrovascular pathology should be screened and treated for vascular risk factors, especially hypertension。
- Recurrent stroke is associated with greater risk of cognitive decline。
- Poststroke dementia is associated with higher mortality。
- For patients with VaD, the source generally follows secondary stroke prevention recommendations。
- In symptomatic VaD, the source does not suggest intensively lowering systolic blood pressure to less than 120 mmHg because of concern for cerebral perfusion compromise。
- Patients with VaD and prior clinical ischemic stroke or TIA should receive appropriate antithrombotic therapy according to stroke subtype。
- If brain imaging shows white matter lesions only, without symptomatic ischemic stroke or imaging evidence of brain infarction, the source does not use antithrombotics。
- If imaging shows brain infarction without clinical stroke history, aspirin 50-100 mg daily is often used despite limited evidence, but bleeding risk / contraindications may lead to avoiding antiplatelet therapy。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Cerebrovascular disease / impaired cerebral blood flow
-> cognitive impairment or dementia
-> recurrent stroke or ongoing vascular injury increases cognitive decline risk
-> treat hypertension, diabetes, lipid risk and stroke mechanism
-> choose antithrombotic only when stroke/TIA or infarction context supports it
```

#### Inferences

- Vascular risk treatment is stronger as prevention of further injury than as reversal of established dementia。
- Antithrombotic choice should not be driven by cognitive diagnosis alone; it depends on stroke mechanism and bleeding risk。
- White matter disease should trigger vascular risk workup and management, not automatic aspirin。

#### Assumptions

- The patient is an adult with cognitive impairment plus clinical or imaging evidence of cerebrovascular pathology。
- Stroke subtype, imaging findings, hemorrhage markers, and bleeding contraindications can be assessed。

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Blood pressure lowering has not been specifically tested as treatment of established VaD。
- Aspirin for silent infarction / silent cerebrovascular disease progression has limited and indirect evidence。
- Mixed Alzheimer disease pathology often cannot be confidently excluded。

### Concept: VaD cognitive medication and prognosis limits

#### One-Sentence Definition

Vascular dementia cognitive medication has limited effect: donepezil or galantamine may be considered when progressive decline is not directly attributable to a clinical stroke, but expected benefit is slight and clinically uncertain; prognosis is heterogeneous。

#### Known Facts

- The source suggests cholinesterase inhibitor therapy in VaD patients with progressive cognitive decline not directly attributable to a clinical stroke。
- The source does not initiate cholinesterase inhibitor therapy when dementia is diagnosed after stroke but there is no progressive cognitive decline。
- Many VaD patients have concomitant Alzheimer disease, and AD pathology is difficult to confirm or exclude。
- A meta-analysis of six cholinesterase inhibitor trials found statistically significant cognitive-scale benefit, but the effect was small, about two ADAS-Cog points, with uncertain clinical significance。
- Donepezil or galantamine has somewhat better evidence than rivastigmine。
- The source suggests against memantine use in VaD because benefit evidence is not compelling。
- Memantine trials showed benefit on cognitive scales but not clinical global impression or activities of daily living。
- Investigational agents generally have inconclusive evidence; isosorbide mononitrate / cilostazol findings after recent lacunar infarction need confirmation before recommendation。
- Exercise and social interactions are suggested, but high-quality evidence for cognition benefit in established VaD is lacking。
- Some poststroke rehabilitation programs include cognitive and physical rehabilitation, but evidence is limited by inconsistent quality and nonstandardized interventions。
- In acute stroke, some cognitive recovery is expected; after initial recovery, some patients remain stable while others decline。
- White matter changes, medial temporal lobe atrophy, recurrent strokes and impaired baseline cognition before stroke are poor prognostic factors in this source。
- Population-based studies suggest increased mortality for vascular cognitive impairment, VaD and dementia after stroke。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Vascular brain injury +/- mixed Alzheimer disease pathology
-> cognitive impairment and functional decline
-> small possible symptomatic response to cholinesterase inhibitors in selected progressive cases
-> no robust disease-reversing medication effect
-> prognosis depends on stroke recovery, recurrent injury, white matter burden and mixed pathology
```

#### Inferences

- Dementia medication should be framed as a limited trial with function / cognition monitoring, not a disease-modifying treatment for VaD。
- If cognition is stable after a single stroke syndrome, cholinesterase inhibitor initiation is less supported by this source。
- Nonpharmacologic care remains important for function and caregiver support even when cognitive benefit is uncertain。

#### Assumptions

- Cognitive trajectory can be separated into acute poststroke recovery, stable residual impairment, and ongoing progressive decline。
- Medication decisions include bradycardia, syncope, GI effects, polypharmacy and goals of care, though detailed dosing is outside this source summary。

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Mixed AD + vascular pathology is common and hard to prove or exclude clinically。
- Cognitive medication effect sizes are small and may not translate into meaningful daily function。
- Prognosis is heterogeneous and not predictable for an individual patient from this source alone。

## Clinically Useful Points

- Do not treat VaD as only a dementia medication problem；start from vascular risk modification and recurrent stroke prevention。
- Avoid systolic BP <120 mmHg as an intensive target in symptomatic VaD based on this source's concern about cerebral perfusion。
- Antithrombotic use requires stroke/TIA or infarction context；white matter lesions alone are not enough。
- Cholinesterase inhibitor trial is most source-consistent when decline is progressive and not simply fixed after a clinical stroke。
- Memantine should not be routine VaD treatment in this source。
- Nonpharmacologic rehabilitation / exercise may help function, but cognitive benefit evidence in established VaD is weak。

## Research-Useful Points

- Vascular risk factor treatment is plausible but not well tested as direct treatment of established VaD。
- Antiplatelet effect on cognitive outcomes has not been clearly demonstrated in trials。
- Cholinesterase inhibitor trials show small cognitive scale effects without consistent global / neuropsychiatric benefit。
- Lifestyle / exercise trials in VaD are small and short-term; durable cognitive benefit is not established。

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- 與「white matter lesions = aspirin indication」衝突。
- 與「vascular dementia 要照 Alzheimer disease 直接開 donepezil / memantine」衝突。
- 與「越 aggressive 降 BP 越能救 cognition」衝突；來源不建議 symptomatic VaD intensively lowering SBP <120 mmHg。
- 與「poststroke dementia 都會持續惡化」衝突；acute stroke 後可有 initial recovery，也可能 stable 或 decline。
- 與「cognitive rehab / exercise 已證實可改善 VaD cognition」衝突；來源認為 established VaD 的 high-quality evidence 不足。

# Source Summary: Routine Upper Extremity, Facial, and Phrenic Nerve Conduction Techniques

## Source Type

- Textbook chapter describing routine upper-extremity, proximal upper-extremity, facial, blink reflex, and phrenic nerve conduction techniques.
- Single-source ingest only; this page does not synthesize with routine lower-extremity NCS, disease-specific CTS / UNE / plexopathy criteria, blink-reflex specialty chapters, or phrenic disease sources.

## Reliability Level

- Tier 1 within this wiki's convention for textbook clinical chapters.
- Strongest for technique: recording sites, stimulation sites, standard distances, internal comparison studies, and common technical pitfalls.
- Limited for disease diagnosis: the chapter names useful studies for CTS, UNE, plexopathy, facial palsy, demyelinating neuropathy, and brainstem lesions, but does not provide full disease-specific diagnostic criteria.
- Normal value tables are one set of adult values; the source explicitly states that local laboratories ideally develop their own normal values.

## One-Sentence Summary

Routine NCS is only routine when recording montage, stimulation site, distance, limb position, side-to-side comparison, and internal comparison rules are standardized before assigning focal neuropathy, conduction block, or normality.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Upper extremity NCS 常規技術與陷阱

#### One-Sentence Definition

Upper extremity NCS technique is a standardized set of nerve-specific recording sites, stimulation sites, distances, internal comparisons, and artifact checks that converts waveform numbers into interpretable localization data.

#### Known Facts

- Median motor study records APB with G1 over muscle belly and G2 over the first metacarpophalangeal joint; distal wrist distance is 7 cm.
- Median motor wrist stimulation is slightly lateral to mid-wrist between flexor carpi radialis and palmaris longus tendons; antecubital fossa stimulation is just medial to the brachial artery pulse.
- Excessive median motor stimulation at the wrist or antecubital fossa can co-stimulate the ulnar nerve.
- If median motor CMAP amplitude is larger at the antecubital fossa than at the wrist, the source says to consider MGA.
- Median motor palmar study uses wrist and palm stimulation while recording APB; palm/wrist CMAP amplitude ratio >1.2 implies some conduction block across the wrist.
- Median motor palmar conduction velocity is not reliable because of short distances and the recurrent thenar branch course.
- Median sensory study records digit 2 or 3 with 13 cm distal distance and antidromic technique described.
- Median proximal sensory response from antecubital fossa is normally smaller and more difficult due to temporal dispersion and phase cancellation.
- Median sensory palmar study compares wrist-to-digit and palm-to-digit segments; palm/wrist SNAP amplitude ratio >1.6 implies some conduction block across the wrist.
- In median sensory palmar study, wrist-to-palm conduction velocity is normally faster than palm-to-digit 3; in CTS, the pattern reverses with relative wrist-to-palm slowing.
- Ulnar motor study records ADM; wrist distance is 7 cm.
- Ulnar below-elbow stimulation is 3 cm distal to the medial epicondyle; above-elbow stimulation is 10-12 cm from below-elbow.
- Ulnar motor study should be performed with elbow flexed 90-135 degrees; straight-elbow position causes factitious across-elbow slowing by underestimating true nerve length.
- Below-elbow stimulation usually needs higher current than wrist or above-elbow stimulation because the nerve lies deep to flexor carpi ulnaris.
- Ulnar below-elbow stimulation must be at least 3 cm distal to the medial epicondyle to be distal to the cubital tunnel, but if it is too distal (>4 cm), the nerve becomes very deep and hard to stimulate.
- Ulnar motor study should always include wrist, below-elbow, and above-elbow stimulation; wrist plus above-elbow alone can miss focal slowing across the elbow.
- Across-elbow distance should be measured along a curved line with elbow flexed, not a straight line.
- If ulnar below-elbow CMAP amplitude is more than 10% smaller than wrist CMAP, consider MGA.
- Ulnar sensory study records digit 5 with 11 cm distal distance; it may be abnormal in ulnar neuropathy or lower trunk brachial plexopathy.
- Dorsal ulnar cutaneous sensory study is spared in Guyon's canal lesions and may be abnormal in some but not all UNE.
- If dorsal ulnar cutaneous response is absent, the source recommends keeping recording electrodes in place and stimulating superficial radial nerve to check rare anomalous dorsal hand innervation.
- Deep ulnar motor branch study records FDI and often helps Guyon's canal lesions; FDI may be more useful than ADM for demonstrating focal ulnar slowing across the elbow.
- In FDI recording, G2 must be on the thumb metacarpophalangeal joint; if placed on index finger metacarpophalangeal joint, CMAP always has initial positive deflection.
- Median-ulnar lumbrical-interossei motor study uses same recording electrodes and same distance for median second lumbrical and ulnar first palmar interosseous responses.
- Median-ulnar lumbrical-interossei distal latency difference is normally <0.5 ms; 0.5 ms is borderline, >0.5 ms definitely abnormal in the source.
- Lumbrical-interossei study is useful for CTS or ulnar neuropathy at Guyon's canal and especially useful for CTS with coexistent polyneuropathy when sensory / mixed potentials may be absent.
- Median-ulnar digit 4 sensory comparison normally has latency difference <0.5 ms with same distance and can demonstrate median neuropathy at the wrist.
- Median-radial digit 1 sensory comparison normally has latency difference <0.5 ms with same distance and can demonstrate median neuropathy at the wrist.
- Median-ulnar palmar mixed studies normally have latency difference <0.4 ms at same 8 cm distance and can show subtle median slowing across the wrist.
- Radial motor study records EIP; radial CMAP usually has an initial positive deflection from nearby radial-innervated muscles, so the active electrode should not be chased to eliminate it.
- Radial motor surface distances can be inaccurate, especially at proximal sites; below and above spiral groove distances are best measured with obstetric calipers.
- Radial sensory study may be abnormal in radial neuropathy or posterior cord / upper or middle trunk plexus lesions and is spared in posterior interosseous neuropathy.
- Medial antebrachial cutaneous sensory study may be abnormal in medial cord or lower trunk plexus lesions and is typically absent or very low in true neurogenic thoracic outlet syndrome.
- Lateral antebrachial cutaneous sensory study may be abnormal in musculocutaneous nerve, lateral cord, or upper trunk lesions.
- Antebrachial cutaneous studies are superficial and often use low stimulation intensities; side-to-side comparisons of amplitude and latency are useful.
- Proximal upper-extremity stimulation can use Erb's point or cervical root stimulation, but supramaximal stimulation may be difficult.
- For proximal motor studies, side-to-side amplitude and latency comparisons are necessary; proximal distances are best measured with obstetric calipers.
- Root stimulation with improper needle placement too laterally has rare reported pneumothorax risk.
- Phrenic motor study records diaphragm with G1 two fingerbreadths above xiphoid and G2 over anterior costal margin 16 cm from G1.
- Phrenic stimulation can accidentally activate spinal accessory nerve or brachial plexus if the stimulator is misplaced.
- Phrenic study is difficult in obese individuals, and amplitudes are slightly larger during inspiration.
- The source warns not to perform phrenic study in ICU patients with an external pacemaker and to use caution near internal jugular catheter, implanted cardiac pacemaker, or cardioverter-defibrillator.
- Facial motor study records nasalis and stimulates anterior to tragus; excessive stimulus may directly stimulate masseter.
- Separate facial branch stimulation is technically easier and more comfortable than stimulating the entire facial nerve at stylomastoid foramen.
- Facial branch studies should always be done bilaterally because contralateral normal side often serves as control.
- Blink reflex records bilateral orbicularis oculi and stimulates supraorbital notch; it is useful for facial nerve palsies, demyelinating neuropathies, and brainstem lesions.
- Normal value tables assume controlled temperature and standard distances.
- Motor and sensory amplitudes are measured baseline to negative peak.
- Sensory and mixed distal latencies are peak latencies, while sensory and mixed conduction velocities are calculated from onset latency.
- Some values need adjustment for extremes of height or age.
- Affected-unaffected limb comparison may be more useful than normal value tables.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Clinical localization question
→ choose nerve-specific NCS protocol
→ standardized recording site + stimulation site + distance + limb position
→ verify stimulation adequacy and avoid co-stimulation
→ use internal comparison / side-to-side comparison when needed
→ interpret latency, amplitude, conduction velocity, and morphology within protocol assumptions
→ only then assign focal slowing, conduction block, plexopathy clue, or technical uncertainty
```

#### Inferences

- NCS protocol is not clerical setup; it determines whether a waveform can be interpreted physiologically.
- Internal comparison studies are a practical way to reduce false interpretation when absolute responses are limited by polyneuropathy or side-specific symptoms.
- A technically correct but incomplete ulnar motor study can still miss the clinically relevant segment.

#### Assumptions

- The lab can maintain controlled temperature, standard distance, consistent electrode placement, and adequate stimulation.
- Local normal values or appropriate lab references are available, especially for height, age, and side-to-side comparisons.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The chapter gives technique and one set of normal values, not final disease-specific criteria.
- Phrenic, proximal root, and some radial motor studies are technically challenging and may be limited by body habitus, stimulation discomfort, or safety constraints.
- Facial / blink reflex sections are brief and should not replace a dedicated blink reflex or cranial neuropathy source.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Median and ulnar internal comparison studies depend on identical distances; small distance errors can create false abnormality.
- Ulnar across-elbow studies require elbow flexion and curved-distance measurement.
- Dorsal ulnar cutaneous response helps separate Guyon's canal from more proximal ulnar lesions, but absence may also reflect rare anomalous dorsal hand innervation.
- Lumbrical-interossei study is especially useful for CTS evaluation when coexistent polyneuropathy makes sensory / mixed responses absent.
- Radial sensory sparing supports posterior interosseous neuropathy rather than a more proximal radial or plexus lesion.
- Antebrachial cutaneous SNAPs are useful for brachial plexus localization, especially medial cord / lower trunk and lateral cord / upper trunk patterns.
- Phrenic NCS requires explicit safety screening for external pacemaker and nearby catheter / implanted device context.

## Research-Useful Points

- Automated NCS interpretation should encode protocol metadata: distance, recording muscle, stimulation site, elbow position, comparison side, and whether onset or peak latency was used.
- Diagnostic threshold studies should separate absolute normal-table cutoffs from internal comparison cutoffs.
- Protocol deviations should be captured as structured data because they can directly change false positive rates.

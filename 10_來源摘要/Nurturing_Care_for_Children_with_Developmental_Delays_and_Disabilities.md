# Source Summary: Nurturing Care for Children with Developmental Delays and Disabilities

## Source Type

- UNICEF / WHO thematic brief.
- Publication year: 2025.
- Intended audience: policy-makers and programme designers.
- Main population: children at risk of or with developmental delays and disabilities, especially children up to 5 years of age, and their caregivers.
- Development process: UNICEF-WHO task team, reference group, caregiver focus group consultations in multiple countries, desk review of peer-reviewed and grey literature, iterative review, and external declarations of interest.

## Reliability Level

- Source tier in this wiki: 7, because this is an official institutional thematic brief, not a formal guideline, textbook chapter, systematic review, or primary trial.
- Strongest for rights-based framing, programme design, disability-inclusive implementation, and identifying service-system actions.
- Weaker for causal effect sizes, clinical treatment thresholds, diagnostic algorithms, and recommendation grading.
- Country examples and programme examples in the brief are useful implementation illustrations, but should not be treated as independently ingested evidence unless their original sources are processed separately.

## One-Sentence Summary

Children with developmental delays or disabilities require disability-inclusive nurturing care through individualized, family-centred, and coordinated systems that combine inclusive universal services with targeted or indicated support, caregiver well-being, community inclusion, assistive technology, early childhood intervention, and intersectoral referral pathways.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Disability-inclusive nurturing care

#### One-Sentence Definition

Disability-inclusive nurturing care means ensuring that children at risk of or with developmental delays and disabilities can access the same five nurturing care components as other children, while receiving individualized, family-centred, and coordinated support that removes barriers to participation.

#### Known Facts

- `Nurturing care` includes five interrelated components: good health, adequate nutrition, safety and security, responsive caregiving, and opportunities for early learning.
- The brief states that nurturing care is relevant to children with developmental delays and disabilities, because it can protect children exposed to developmental risk and support functioning and long-term outcomes.
- Children with developmental delays or disabilities are diverse; they may have congenital conditions, acquired impairments, chronic health or genetic conditions, prematurity, low birth weight, malnutrition, infections, neurotoxic exposure, poverty, violence, or poor caregiver-child interaction histories.
- `Developmental delay` refers to significant variation in milestone achievement for actual or adjusted age.
- `Developmental disability` refers to health conditions affecting the developing nervous system and impairing motor, cognitive, language, behaviour, or sensory functioning, with participation restricted by interaction with barriers and context.
- The brief aligns with the ICF view that disability is not simply a health condition; it results from the interaction between impairment and societal, physical, or environmental barriers.
- Children with disabilities have higher risk of missed vaccinations or services, childhood illnesses, undernutrition, severe physical punishment, lower birth registration, lower access to sanitation resources, lower home developmental activities, lower early childhood education participation, and lower early stimulation / responsive care.
- Caregivers of these children may face stigma, social isolation, livelihood loss, inaccessible information, limited services, high expenses, and mental health stress.
- The brief names three guiding principles: individualized care, family-centred care, and coordinated care.
- Service design should use a twin-track approach: inclusive mainstream / universal services for all children plus targeted or indicated support for children and caregivers needing additional help.
- Early childhood intervention (ECI) services are described as birth-onward, family-centred, strengths-based, home- and routine-grounded, multisectoral, integrated, and transdisciplinary supports.
- Developmental monitoring is broader than milestone checking; it interprets child functioning and the home environment and links families to individualized support when needed.
- The brief states that early identification should enable timely intervention, but access to family-centred services should not wait for a formal diagnosis.
- Assistive technologies can improve functioning and participation, but require affordability, procurement, maintenance, trained providers, and caregiver support.
- Community stigma, myths, inaccessible spaces, and exclusion from childcare, early learning, clinics, worship spaces, parks, libraries, and other shared settings can reduce participation.
- Caregiver-mediated interventions are presented as ways to build caregiver confidence, child communication, movement, daily living skills, positive identity, caregiver mental health, self-care, and peer support.

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Developmental delay / disability + social and service barriers
-> child and caregiver miss ordinary nurturing care opportunities
-> caregiver stress, isolation, financial strain, and inaccessible information increase
-> responsive caregiving, early learning, health access, nutrition, safety, and participation weaken
-> functioning and long-term participation are limited beyond the impairment itself

Disability-inclusive system design
-> inclusive universal services + targeted / indicated support + caregiver resources
-> barriers are reduced and caregiver capability increases
-> child receives more responsive, safe, stimulating, and accessible daily care
-> functioning, participation, family quality of life, and service continuity can improve
```

#### Inferences

- A child with developmental disability may need both condition-specific rehabilitation and ordinary nurturing care access; one does not replace the other.
- A developmental clinic that only provides diagnosis without caregiver coaching, referral coordination, social support, and participation planning leaves the main nurturing care mechanism incomplete.
- Requiring a formal diagnosis before support is risky when service systems already know that caregiver coaching, developmental monitoring, and inclusive routines can begin earlier.
- Care coordination is not administrative decoration; it is part of the causal pathway that lets families actually use services.

#### Assumptions

- Health, education, social protection, child protection, rehabilitation, and community systems can coordinate rather than operate as isolated silos.
- Providers have training and supervision to use a family-centred, nonjudgmental, culturally and linguistically appropriate approach.
- Referral and follow-up systems exist, or programme planners are actively building them.
- Local adaptation accounts for poverty, language, geography, disability access, humanitarian context, and caregiver mental health.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The source is not a GRADE guideline and does not grade each action by evidence certainty.
- Programme examples such as CST, LEAP-CP, Baby Ubuntu, AAC projects, and deinstitutionalization examples are summarized as examples; their original evidence was not independently ingested in this page.
- Effect size, cost-effectiveness, workforce feasibility, and long-term outcome strength vary by programme and setting.
- Some cited trials or evaluations were ongoing at the time of the brief; results should be checked in the original sources before treating them as established evidence.
- The brief focuses on programme and policy actions; it does not replace condition-specific diagnostic workup, pediatric rehabilitation, mental health care, child protection intervention, or assistive technology prescription.

## Clinically Useful Points

- When a child has developmental delay or disability, clinical reasoning should ask not only "what is the diagnosis?" but also "what barriers are blocking nurturing care?"
- Useful service questions include: caregiver mental health and safety, financial strain, transportation, information access, stigma, school / childcare inclusion, assistive technology access, hearing / vision / feeding needs, and referral completion.
- Early family-centred support should not be delayed solely because the child lacks a final diagnosis.
- Developmental monitoring should include function, participation, caregiver-child interaction, family strengths, risk factors, and referral needs, not just a milestone checklist.
- Caregiver-mediated coaching should be practical and routine-based: communication, play, responsive feeding, self-care, inclusion, and safe participation.

## Research-Useful Points

- Data systems should identify children needing support and track service access, disaggregated by age, sex, impairment type, socioeconomic status, and other equity variables.
- Research gaps include costed service models, workforce and task-sharing implementation, AT access, caregiver mental health integration, inclusive community participation, and long-term outcomes.
- Future synthesis should separate evidence for the thematic brief itself from evidence for the programmes it cites.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Stroke Etiologic Classification

#### One-Sentence Definition

Stroke etiologic classification is the mechanism-based assignment of stroke into ischemic or hemorrhagic categories and, for ischemic stroke, into large artery atherosclerosis / thrombosis, cardioaortic or artery-to-artery embolism, small vessel disease, systemic hypoperfusion, other determined causes, or undetermined causes.

#### Known Facts

- Stroke is broadly divided into brain ischemia and brain hemorrhage.
- Brain ischemia in this source is organized around thrombosis, embolism, and systemic hypoperfusion.
- Brain hemorrhage is organized around intracerebral hemorrhage and subarachnoid hemorrhage.
- Thrombotic stroke can arise from large vessel disease or small vessel / penetrating artery disease.
- Large vessel lesions may cause low flow, artery-to-artery embolism, or both.
- Small vessel disease commonly involves penetrating arteries supplying deep brain structures; lipohyalinosis and branch atheromatous disease are important mechanisms.
- Embolic stroke is classified by source: definite cardiac, possible cardiac / aortic, arterial source, or truly unknown source after testing.
- Systemic hypoperfusion produces a more global circulatory problem and can injure watershed regions.
- Blood and coagulation disorders are uncommon primary causes of arterial stroke, but matter in selected patients such as young stroke, cryptogenic stroke, or prior clotting history.
- TOAST classifies acute ischemic stroke into five subtypes: large artery atherosclerosis, cardioembolism, small vessel occlusion, other determined etiology, and undetermined etiology.
- SSS-TOAST and CCS add evident / probable / possible confidence levels to reduce overuse of undetermined categories when multiple candidate mechanisms are found.
- The source states original TOAST and CCS agreement is only moderate at best.
- ISPS25 keeps similar mechanism categories but emphasizes a broader minimum diagnostic evaluation to increase certainty and guide secondary prevention.
- ISPS25 minimum diagnostic evaluation includes history / physical examination, brain plus intracranial and extracranial vascular imaging, ECG and at least 24 hours rhythm monitoring if AF is not already seen, selected echocardiography, and laboratory testing.

#### Mechanism Chain

1. Acute neurologic injury occurs because brain tissue loses blood supply, receives blood under pathologic bleeding, or is affected by global circulatory failure.
2. Broad classification separates too little blood / oxygen delivery from too much blood in the cranial compartment.
3. Ischemic classification then asks whether the lesion is local arterial disease, embolus from elsewhere, global hypoperfusion, blood disorder, or other rare cause.
4. Imaging, vascular studies, rhythm monitoring, echocardiography, and selective laboratory testing assign diagnostic confidence.
5. The resulting mechanism label determines secondary prevention logic and identifies when uncertainty remains.

#### Inferences

- Etiologic classification is downstream of bedside subtype suspicion and upstream of secondary prevention.
- A single finding such as AF, PFO, carotid plaque, or lacunar syndrome should be treated as a candidate mechanism until competing mechanisms have been evaluated.
- Documentation should distinguish "clinical pattern," "imaging pattern," "candidate etiology," and "final adjudicated etiology."

#### Assumptions

- The source mainly applies to adult stroke.
- Diagnostic infrastructure such as brain imaging, vascular imaging, ECG / rhythm monitoring, echocardiography, and selected labs is available.
- Local stroke systems may sequence tests differently depending on acute reperfusion eligibility.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- TOAST, CCS, and ISPS25 are not interchangeable even when labels look similar.
- Potential cardiac / aortic sources may be incidental rather than causal.
- Hypercoagulable states are uncommon primary arterial stroke causes and require pretest-probability framing.
- Pediatric, pregnancy-related, genetic, cancer-associated, and cerebral venous thrombosis mechanisms require separate sources.

### Concept: Stroke Epidemiology and Population Distribution

#### One-Sentence Definition

Stroke epidemiology describes how stroke subtype proportions, incidence, mortality, disability burden, and population risk differ by country income level, geography, sex, and race / ethnicity.

#### Known Facts

- The source states that stroke is a major global cause of mortality and disability.
- Adult lifetime stroke risk from age 25 years and older is approximately 25 percent in the cited global estimate.
- The source states that East Asia, Central Europe, and Eastern Europe have high stroke risk.
- In the United States, the source gives approximate subtype distribution as ischemia 87 percent, ICH 10 percent, and SAH 3 percent.
- The source gives United States annual new or recurrent stroke count at approximately 795,000, including approximately 610,000 first-ever strokes and 185,000 recurrent strokes.
- The source describes higher stroke incidence, prevalence, and mortality in the southeastern United States than in the rest of the country.
- The source states lifetime stroke risk is higher for females than males.
- Black and Hispanic Americans have higher stroke risk than White Americans in cited cohort observations.
- The source describes subtype disparities, including higher relative rates of intracranial atherosclerosis, extracranial atherosclerosis, lacunes, and cardioembolism among Black and Hispanic Americans compared with White Americans in Northern Manhattan Study data.

#### Mechanism Chain

Population risk profile -> vascular risk factor distribution and health system context -> different ischemic / hemorrhagic subtype proportions -> different mortality, disability, and recurrence burden -> different prevention priorities.

#### Inferences

- A United States subtype distribution should not be assumed to represent low- and middle-income countries.
- Rehabilitation and prevention planning should treat population risk as context, not as proof of individual mechanism.
- Epidemiologic disparity data should prompt better risk-factor detection and access-to-care analysis rather than biologic overinterpretation.

#### Assumptions

- Epidemiologic numbers depend on surveillance definitions, geography, era, and data source.
- Race / ethnicity categories in United States studies are social / epidemiologic categories and should not be reduced to deterministic biology.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The epidemiology section and the summary give different global stroke subtype proportions; this should be treated as source-internal inconsistency.
- Secular trends differ by country income level and time period.
- This source is not a Taiwan-specific epidemiology source.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Do not use bedside course alone as final etiology; it is only a prior probability.
- Classify ischemic stroke mechanism before selecting long-term antithrombotic logic.
- Treat cryptogenic / undetermined stroke as a diagnosis after adequate evaluation, not as a label for "not yet worked up."
- Potential cardiac or aortic sources such as PFO, atrial septal aneurysm, or complex aortic plaque require causality judgment rather than automatic attribution.
- Young stroke, recurrent cryptogenic stroke, systemic thrombosis history, or family clotting history should raise the threshold for targeted uncommon-cause testing.

## Research-Useful Points

- Stroke studies should state the classification system used, because TOAST and CCS may not categorize cases identically.
- "Undetermined" should be separated into negative evaluation, incomplete evaluation, and more than one plausible cause.
- Epidemiologic comparisons should specify country, time period, surveillance method, and subtype definition.
- Disparity analyses should avoid mixing incidence, prevalence, mortality, subtype distribution, and access-to-care mechanisms.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- Conflicts with treating "stroke" as a single mechanism for secondary prevention.
- Conflicts with equating bedside subtype recognition with final etiologic classification.
- Conflicts with treating AF, PFO, carotid stenosis, or lacunar syndrome as automatically causal without competing-mechanism review.
- Conflicts with routine hypercoagulable testing for every arterial ischemic stroke.
- Conflicts with assuming United States ischemic / hemorrhagic proportions apply globally.

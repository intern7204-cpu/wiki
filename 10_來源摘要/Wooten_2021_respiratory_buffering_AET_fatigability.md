# Source Summary: Wooten et al. 2021 - Role of Respiratory Buffering in the Relationship Between Recovery and Performance Fatigability Following Aerobic Exercise Training

## Source Type

- Original article.
- Single-arm, pre-experimental longitudinal pilot study.
- Journal context: *Cardiopulmonary Physical Therapy Journal* 2021;32(1):11-19.
- Raw source: `C:\原始資料\nihms-1917261\nihms-1917261.md`

## Reliability Level

- Tier 5: original pilot / observational study.
- Evidence strength: low to medium for within-subject association and hypothesis generation.
- Not guideline-level evidence, not randomized intervention evidence, and not a direct mechanistic acid-base validation study.

## One-Sentence Summary

Wooten et al. 2021 found that a 4-week supervised vigorous aerobic cycling program in 20 apparently healthy adults improved peak exercise capacity, endurance-bout time, estimated excess VCO2, and VCO2 recovery off-kinetics, while statistical relationships between recovery and performance fatigability weakened after accounting for estimated excess VCO2, supporting but not proving a buffering-related moderator hypothesis.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Estimated Excess VCO2 as a Moderator Between Recovery and Performance Fatigability

#### One-Sentence Definition

Estimated excess VCO2 is a CPET-derived proxy for respiratory buffering demand that Wooten et al. tested as a possible moderator between recovery off-kinetics and performance fatigability after aerobic exercise training.

#### Known Facts

- Fifty-nine individuals were pre-screened.
- Twenty-one individuals were enrolled.
- Twenty subjects completed all portions of the protocol.
- Participants had median age 52 years, IQR 46-55.
- Eleven participants were female and nine were male.
- Median BMI was 26.5 kg/m2, IQR 23.1-29.7.
- Baseline reported physical activity ranged from sedentary to highly active recreational activity.
- Median moderate-intensity activity was 120 min/week and median vigorous-intensity activity was 32.5 min/week.
- Only one highly active subject was regularly engaged primarily in aerobic cycling exercise.
- The intervention was 4 weeks of supervised aerobic exercise training.
- Training frequency was 4 sessions/week.
- Training used stationary cycling for 45 minutes at 70 +/- 5% heart rate reserve.
- Reported adherence was 99.7%, with only one missed session.
- Peak CPX used cycle ergometry with 25 W/min increments and 60 rpm target cadence.
- End1 was performed after peak CPX recovery at 70% of peak wattage.
- Visit 2 used three 6-min work bouts at 80% of AT with 8-min passive rests, followed by End2.
- AT was determined by V-slope method.
- Recovery VO2 and VCO2 off-kinetics were modeled with mono-exponential fitting after excluding the first 20 seconds of recovery.
- Excess VCO2 was estimated as total VCO2 minus estimated metabolic VCO2 above AT.
- Peak VO2 increased from 2181 (705) to 2484 (832) mL/min; change 304 mL/min, p = .000.
- Peak VCO2 increased from 2817 (868) to 3038 (995) mL/min; change 221 mL/min, p = .020.
- AT-VO2 increased from 1270 (401) to 1360 (423) mL/min; change 89 mL/min, p = .008.
- Estimated excess VCO2 increased from 2536 (1478) to 3144 (1680) mL; change 608 mL, p = .001.
- Pk-Time increased from 519 (151) to 582 (147) seconds; change 63 seconds, p = .000.
- Pk-Watts increased from 209 (61) to 233 (59) W; change 24 W, p = .000.
- End1 time increased from 453 (295) to 718 (470) seconds; change 265 seconds, p = .002.
- End2 time increased from 734 (484) to 1054 (776) seconds; change 321 seconds, p < .002.
- End2 improvement occurred despite a 12% increase in End2 wattage from 146 (43) to 163 (41) W.
- VCO2 off-kinetic tau decreased from 81.0 (10.9) to 73.3 (10.6) seconds; change -7.70 seconds, p = .009.
- VCO2 off-kinetic MRT decreased from 102.8 (11.2) to 95.7 (10.5) seconds; change -7.05 seconds, p = .015.
- VO2 off-kinetic tau and MRT did not reach statistical significance in Table 3.
- Recovery-fatigability correlations weakened after covarying estimated excess VCO2.
- For VO2-off ORI, correlation reductions after covarying excess VCO2 ranged from -0.228 to -0.296.
- For VCO2-off ORI, correlation reductions after covarying excess VCO2 ranged from -0.176 to -0.326.

#### Mechanism Chain

1. Exercise above AT increases reliance on non-oxidative energy contribution and raises H+ handling demand.
2. Bicarbonate buffering can generate additional expired CO2 beyond CO2 expected from oxidative metabolism.
3. This additional expired CO2 is estimated as excess VCO2 above the V-slope AT anchor.
4. If aerobic exercise training improves tolerance to above-AT work, buffering and recovery dynamics may change.
5. Faster recovery off-kinetics and lower fatigability may therefore partly share a buffering-related pathway.
6. In this source, covarying estimated excess VCO2 weakened recovery-fatigability correlations, consistent with a moderator hypothesis.
7. Because lactate and pH were not measured, the acid-base mechanism remains inferred rather than directly demonstrated.

#### Inferences

- Estimated excess VCO2 may capture a recovery-relevant physiological dimension that peak VO2 and AT-VO2 alone do not fully represent.
- The observed correlation attenuation after covarying excess VCO2 is compatible with moderation, but it does not prove causal mediation.
- The increase in excess VCO2 after training likely reflects greater above-AT work capacity and altered buffering demand, not necessarily a simple isolated increase in acid production.
- The intervention signal is best interpreted as hypothesis-generating for rehabilitation and fatigue research.

#### Assumptions

- The gas-exchange geometry used by the authors estimates metabolic VCO2 above AT well enough to isolate an excess VCO2 component.
- V-slope AT is an acceptable operational anchor for this estimation.
- End1 and End2 time-to-exhaustion measures are meaningful performance fatigability outcomes in this protocol.
- Recovery VO2 / VCO2 off-kinetics reflect recovery physiology rather than only ventilatory pattern or modeling artifact.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- The study was single-arm and nonrandomized.
- Repeated testing, learning, expectation, or regression effects cannot be fully separated from training effects.
- The sample was a convenience sample of apparently healthy adults with normal BMI range.
- Generalization to highly sedentary, clinical, older frail, cardiopulmonary, neuromuscular, or metabolic disease populations is uncertain.
- Excess VCO2 was estimated, not directly measured.
- Plasma lactate and pH were not measured.
- The study does not provide a validated clinical cutoff for fatigability, recovery impairment, or buffering limitation.
- The source should not be used to prescribe vigorous exercise in high-risk patients without standard screening and monitoring.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Fatigability should be separated from perceived fatigue and linked to a defined task, work rate, duration, and recovery context.
- Recovery after exertion may be clinically relevant, not just peak performance during the exercise test.
- Estimated excess VCO2 and VCO2 off-kinetics remain research-level signals; they should not drive standalone clinical decisions.
- Vigorous cycling at 70 +/- 5% HRR for 4 weeks was feasible in this screened healthy sample, but this does not establish safety for disease populations.

## Research-Useful Points

- Future studies should include direct acid-base markers such as lactate, bicarbonate, and pH if they want to validate the buffering mechanism.
- Randomized or controlled designs are needed before attributing observed changes specifically to the training intervention.
- Reporting should distinguish estimated excess VCO2 from directly measured biochemical buffering.
- Fatigability outcomes should specify whether they measure peak test duration, fixed-intensity endurance time, walk-test velocity decline, or recovery kinetics.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- Conflicts with treating estimated excess VCO2 as a direct lactate, pH, or bicarbonate measurement.
- Conflicts with interpreting correlation attenuation as proof of causal mediation.
- Conflicts with using this small single-arm pilot as routine clinical validation of a CPET biomarker.
- Conflicts with merging this source into the separate Wooten 2021 cross-sectional feasibility pilot without explicit source separation.

## One-Sentence Summary

Acute stroke complications are common and outcome-relevant; rehab / stroke-unit care must actively screen for dysphagia, aspiration pneumonia, VTE risk, infection, cardiac complications, pulmonary complications, GI bleeding, urinary problems, falls, depression, and neurologic deterioration rather than treating rehabilitation as pure motor training.

## Core Concepts Extracted

### Concept: Poststroke complication surveillance

#### One-Sentence Definition

Poststroke complication surveillance 是在 acute stroke 與 early rehabilitation period 主動監測 medical、pulmonary、cardiac、GI、urinary、fall、psychiatric 與 neurologic complications，因為這些併發症會直接改變 mortality、readmission、functional recovery 與 therapy safety。

#### Known Facts

- Acute stroke 的 medical complications 很常見，且會增加 poor clinical outcome 的風險。
- 嚴重 complication 包括 pneumonia、urinary tract infection、GI bleeding、myocardial infarction、DVT 與 pulmonary embolism。
- Any in-hospital medical complication 與 30-day readmission 風險上升相關（adjusted HR 1.68, 95% CI 1.04–2.73）。
- Table 1 common medical complications：depression 29%、pneumonia 12%、UTI 8%、shoulder pain 8%、sepsis 5%、VTE 3%、falls 2%。
- Table 2 serious medical complications：all pneumonias 5%、aspiration pneumonia 3%、heart failure 3%、GI bleeding 3%、cardiac arrest 2%、angina/MI/cardiac ischemia 1%、DVT 1%、PE 1%、hypoxia 1%、UTI 1%、sepsis 1%。
- RANTTAS placebo-group prospective data：至少一個 serious medical complication 發生於 24% patients。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Acute stroke → immobility / dysphagia / impaired consciousness / autonomic dysregulation / bladder dysfunction / cognitive-perceptual deficits → medical and neurologic complications → reduced therapy tolerance + higher morbidity/mortality + worse functional outcome
```

#### Inferences

- Stroke rehabilitation 的 early plan 若只聚焦 motor training，會漏掉真正限制訓練量與安全性的 systemic complications。
- Complication surveillance 應被視為 rehabilitation readiness 與 daily therapy dosing 的前置條件。

#### Assumptions

- UpToDate 整理的成人 acute stroke complication data 可作為 adult rehab setting 的初步 surveillance framework。
- 不同 health system 與 stroke-unit quality 會改變 complication incidence。

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- 各 complication 的 incidence 受 screening intensity 影響，例如 dysphagia 用 water swallow、trained clinician 或 VFSS 偵測會得到不同比例。
- Pediatric stroke、新生兒 stroke、SAH / ICH / ischemic stroke 的 complication profile 不應直接合併外推。

### Concept: Dysphagia–aspiration–pneumonia chain after stroke

#### One-Sentence Definition

Poststroke dysphagia is an oropharyngeal swallowing impairment that increases aspiration risk and can lead to aspiration pneumonia, making early swallowing screening a safety-critical step before oral medication or food.

#### Known Facts

- Dysphagia is common after stroke and is a major risk factor for aspiration pneumonia。
- Oropharyngeal dysphagia includes impaired swallowing efficiency and safety, delayed timing, reduced movement range, and frank aspiration。
- Systematic review pooled data：pneumonia risk increased with dysphagia versus no dysphagia（RR 3.17, 95% CI 2.07–4.87），and especially with aspiration versus no aspiration（RR 11.56, 95% CI 3.36–39.77）。
- Dysphagia incidence after stroke depends on assessment method：screening methods 37–45%；trained swallowing clinician 51–55%；instrumental testing, especially VFSS, 64–78%。
- Initial predictors include facial palsy、aphasia、age >70、higher NIHSS、impaired pharyngeal response、incomplete oral clearance、palatal weakness / asymmetry。
- Approximately 90% return to safe swallowing by two weeks, but a significant minority have persistent dysphagia。
- UpToDate recommends water swallow test at admission for awake and alert acute stroke patients before oral medications or food。
- Formal dysphagia screening protocol was associated with lower aspiration pneumonia risk（adjusted OR 0.10, 95% CI 0.03–0.45；pneumonia 2.4% vs 5.4%）。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke lesion / impaired consciousness → oropharyngeal dysphagia + impaired cough / airway protection → aspiration of fluid, food, or secretions → aspiration pneumonia → mortality and poor functional outcome risk rises
```

#### Inferences

- Water swallow screening is not definitive diagnosis; it is a triage tool that reduces unsafe oral intake before full assessment.
- Severe dysphagia and aspiration should directly modify rehab positioning, nutrition route, medication route, pulmonary care, and therapy timing.

#### Assumptions

- Screening protocols are implemented correctly, with NPO and escalation pathways when failed.
- Instrumental findings need clinical interpretation because VFSS may detect age-related movement patterns that are not necessarily pathologic dysphagia.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Bedside water swallow tests have lower sensitivity than comprehensive testing; systematic reviews report variable sensitivity / specificity.
- No single bedside test fully replaces comprehensive swallowing assessment when aspiration risk remains clinically important.

### Concept: Stroke complication prevention as risk tradeoff

#### One-Sentence Definition

Stroke complication prevention is not simply adding prophylaxis; it requires selecting interventions whose benefits exceed harms in the patient’s current neurologic, respiratory, urinary, cardiac, GI, mobility, and cognition context.

#### Known Facts

- VTE prophylaxis is indicated for all acute stroke patients with restricted mobility。
- Prophylactic antibiotics may reduce overall infection but do not reduce mortality or improve functional outcome in general acute stroke populations。
- In intubated acute brain injury patients, a single dose of ceftriaxone 2 g IV within 12 hours of intubation reduced VAP within 7 days（14% vs 32%；HR 0.6, 95% CI 0.38–0.95）and 28-day mortality（15% vs 25%；HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.39–0.97）in one RCT including many stroke patients。
- UTI occurs in 11–15% followed up to three months; risk factors include female sex、older age、higher mRS、PVR >100 mL。
- Indwelling urinary catheter duration is directly related to UTI risk and should be avoided when possible。
- GI hemorrhage affects 1.1–3% of acute stroke patients; stress ulcer prophylaxis is not routine and is reserved for high-risk ICU or other high-risk patients because acid suppression may increase nosocomial pneumonia risk。
- Falls after stroke can cause serious injury; fall precautions are recommended for all stroke patients, especially with hemiparesis、cognitive impairment、neglect、anosognosia、polypharmacy。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke-related impairment → predictable complication risk → preventive intervention → benefit only if targeted to the risk mechanism; otherwise added intervention can introduce harm (infection, pneumonia, resistance, restraint injury, underfeeding, etc.)
```

#### Inferences

- “Routine prevention” should mean routine risk assessment, not routine use of every preventive drug or device.
- Rehab teams should repeatedly reassess catheter need, swallowing status, mobility restriction, respiratory status, and fall risk because the risk-benefit balance changes over days.

#### Assumptions

- Preventive measures are coordinated across acute care, rehab, nursing, SLP, PT/OT, nutrition, and medicine teams.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Many measures proposed to prevent aspiration in older adults have not been trialed specifically in stroke patients.
- Stroke-specific fall prevention evidence is limited despite broad fall-precaution recommendations.

### Concept: Stroke-heart and neurogenic cardiopulmonary complications

#### One-Sentence Definition

Acute stroke can produce cardiac and pulmonary complications not only through comorbidity but also through neurologic-autonomic mechanisms, especially insular involvement and subarachnoid hemorrhage contexts.

#### Known Facts

- MI、cardiac arrhythmias、and neurogenic cardiac injury are potential short- and long-term complications of acute stroke。
- All acute stroke patients should have ECG and troponin on admission, and continuous cardiac monitoring for at least the first 24 hours to detect arrhythmias, especially atrial fibrillation。
- MI occurs in approximately 1–2% during initial hospitalization and is associated with poor outcome。
- Troponin is not specific for acute thrombotic coronary occlusion and may be elevated after acute stroke due to autonomic dysfunction or nonischemic myocardial injury。
- Serious arrhythmia events occurred in 25% during the first 72 hours in one monitored stroke-unit prospective study。
- Neurogenic cardiac damage is uncommon but described, especially with SAH and less often other stroke types；it may mimic MI / takotsubo cardiomyopathy。
- Mechanism is most likely centrally mediated catecholamine release from stroke-related brain injury。
- Insular cortex stroke may be associated with repolarization abnormalities、arrhythmias、neurogenic cardiac damage、heart failure、sudden death。
- Pulmonary complications include pneumonia、neurogenic pulmonary edema、need for intubation / mechanical ventilation、abnormal respiratory patterns。

#### Mechanism Chain

```text
Stroke involving autonomic control networks (especially insula / SAH contexts) → sympathetic-catecholamine surge and autonomic imbalance → ECG changes, troponin elevation, arrhythmias, transient LV dysfunction, pulmonary edema or abnormal breathing → acute medical instability and rehab safety constraints
```

#### Inferences

- Troponin elevation after stroke should not automatically be labeled coronary occlusion; it requires ACS features, ECG pattern, biomarkers, hemodynamics, and clinical context.
- Rehab intensity and mobilization decisions should consider cardiac rhythm, oxygenation, ventilation, and evolving neurologic status.

#### Assumptions

- Acute care has access to ECG, telemetry, troponin interpretation, and cardiology / ICU escalation when needed.

#### Uncertainties / Limitations

- Stroke-heart syndrome mechanisms are biologically plausible but patient-level prediction remains incomplete.
- ECG abnormalities after stroke may reflect stroke-mediated autonomic effects, pre-existing coronary disease, or true ACS.

## Clinically Useful Points

- Swallow screening before oral medication / feeding is a safety step, not an optional rehab detail.
- Aspiration pneumonia prevention begins with dysphagia screening, NPO when needed, diet modification, pulmonary care, and mobilization when neurologically stable.
- Avoid indwelling urinary catheters whenever possible; do not treat asymptomatic bacteriuria except in special indications.
- Stress ulcer prophylaxis is not routine in all stroke patients; reserve for high-risk patients because acid suppression may increase pneumonia risk.
- Cardiac monitoring is part of acute stroke management; arrhythmias and stroke-heart syndrome can change rehab readiness.
- Falls and fractures are not minor events after stroke; paretic-side fracture risk and impaired protective responses matter.

## Research-Useful Points

- Complication incidence is highly method-dependent; dysphagia incidence varies from 37–45% by screening to 64–78% by VFSS.
- Recent data include 2023–2024 references: US NIS trends, poststroke fracture risk, prophylactic antibiotics meta-analysis, ceftriaxone VAP RCT, and stroke-heart outcomes.
- Future synthesis should separate ischemic stroke, ICH, and SAH because neurologic deterioration, cardiac effects, and pulmonary complications differ by subtype.

## Conflicts With Existing Knowledge

- Potential conflict with simplistic rehab framing: “stroke rehab = motor recovery” is incomplete; this source frames complication surveillance as outcome-relevant.
- Potential conflict with routine prophylaxis thinking: prophylactic antibiotics and stress ulcer prophylaxis should not be generalized to all stroke patients.

# Zone 2 Training 證據邊界

## One-Sentence Definition

Zone 2 training 是通常位於 LT1 / GET 以下、接近 Fatmax 或可維持 conversation 的低到中等強度運動；目前證據支持 low-intensity endurance training 可改善 untrained adults 的 aerobic fitness，但不支持 Zone 2 是改善 mitochondrial capacity、FAO 或 CRF 的唯一或普遍最佳強度。

## Definition and Boundary

本頁整合 Storoschuk et al. 2025 對 Zone 2 public claim 的 narrative evidence boundary，以及 Nuuttila et al. 2026 對 healthy working-age sedentary / untrained adults 的 LIT systematic review / meta-analysis。它不是完整耐力訓練處方，也不是把 Zone 2 否定為無效；重點是防止把「可用」誤寫成「最優」或「不可超過」。

Sitko et al. 2025 expert viewpoint 提供 practical definition 與 monitoring language；單一來源拆頁見 [[Zone_2_Training_實務定義與監測]]。該來源可補強「怎麼操作 Zone 2」，但不能提升為 efficacy / superiority evidence。

Zone 2 在來源中約等於：

- Below LT1 / GET。
- BLa < 2.0 mmol/L 或約 1.7-2.0 mmol/L。
- Below ventilatory threshold 1。
- Around / below Fatmax。
- Talk Test 可維持 comfortable conversation。
- Exercise physiology 三域中的 [[../04_CPET/Exercise_Intensity_Domains|moderate-intensity domain]]。

## Why It Matters

Zone 2 被 popular media 包裝成改善 mitochondria、fat oxidation、metabolic health 與 longevity 的核心強度。問題不是 Zone 2 有沒有用，而是把 elite endurance athlete 的低強度高訓練量、Fatmax、生理閾值與一般人的健康處方混成同一個簡化規則，會讓 time-limited 的人少做足以改善 CRF 的強度。

## Preconditions or Conditions

- 必須先說明 Zone 2 的操作定義：LT1、GET、BLa、Fatmax、Talk Test 或 coaching zone。
- 必須區分目標 outcome：mitochondrial capacity、FAO capacity、CRF、glucose tolerance、adherence、recovery 或 sport-specific performance。
- 必須標示族群：sedentary / untrained、obese、type 2 diabetes、trained athlete、elite athlete 或 rehabilitation population。
- 若未實測 threshold，用 %HRmax、%VO2max 或 pace 估 Zone 2 時，誤分類風險高。

## Mechanism

```text
Zone 2 / moderate domain below LT1
  -> lower glycolytic flux and lower H+ / lactate accumulation
  -> lower AMP/ADP perturbation and smaller PCr disturbance
  -> tolerable and repeatable stimulus
  -> possible glycogen depletion if duration is long enough
  -> possible but inconsistent AMPK / PGC-1alpha / CaMKII signaling
  -> FAO adaptation may occur in untrained or metabolic-risk populations
  -> mitochondrial capacity / CRF improvement may be weaker than higher-intensity work when time is limited
```

## Observable Patterns

- LT1 / Zone 2 boundary can vary widely by fitness; the review's example showed LT1 at 23%, 45%, and 57% of WRpeak in three individuals.
- Sitko et al. 2025 expert panel preferred defining practical Zone 2 as immediately below LT1 / VT1, with markers including lactate around 1-2 mmol/L, HR about 70-80% HRmax or 80-90% LT1 HR, Borg RPE around 10, and power around 75-80% critical power.
- Sitko et al. 2025 also emphasized that HR/RPE should be monitored when using power or other external-load prescription, because long sessions can show cardiac drift and internal/external load decoupling.
- Acute Zone 2 studies show small and inconsistent mitochondrial biogenic signaling.
- Few studies directly test Zone 2 as popularly defined.
- Zone 2 may improve FAO capacity, especially in sedentary / untrained or obesity / type 2 diabetes contexts.
- Evidence does not show that Zone 2 is superior to higher intensities for FAO.
- For mitochondrial capacity, evidence more strongly supports exercise above Zone 2, especially when volume is limited.
- For CRF, higher intensities often produce equal or greater gains, and in active / trained individuals may be required.

## Clinical / Research Implication

- Clinical implication: Zone 2 can be prescribed when tolerability, adherence, recovery, or low-risk volume accumulation matters, but should not automatically replace all vigorous work.
- Public-health implication: for people with limited weekly exercise time, avoiding all above-Zone-2 intensity may reduce CRF improvement.
- Nuuttila et al. 2026 補充：在 healthy sedentary / untrained adults，LIT 對 relative VO2max、absolute VO2max、Pmax 與 VT1 有正向效果；但 higher intensity within LIT 主要只和 VO2max response 有一致關聯，cardiometabolic biomarkers 的效果較小且異質性較高。
- Research implication: future studies need explicit Zone 2 verification and separate outcomes for mitochondrial content, respiration, FAO, CRF, and health markers.

## Fact

- Storoschuk et al. 2025 is a narrative review, not a systematic review.
- The review targeted the general population, not elite endurance athletes.
- The authors did not find substantive evidence that Zone 2 is superior to higher intensities for mitochondrial or fat oxidative capacity.
- The authors reported that Zone 2 may fall below moderate-to-vigorous physical activity guideline intensity for some unfit individuals.
- The review states that few studies explicitly examined Zone 2 training.
- The review states that Zone 2 training can improve FAO capacity, likely mainly in sedentary / untrained populations.
- The review states that CRF is robustly linked to cardiometabolic disease and all-cause mortality risk, whereas the mitochondrial-capacity-to-mortality link is less established.
- The review argues that prioritizing higher intensity appears important for improving CRF when time is limited.
- Sitko et al. 2025 expert viewpoint states that expected adaptations are likely not unique to Zone 2 and can also occur with slightly higher or lower intensities.
- Nuuttila et al. 2026 included 50 RCTs with 824 intervention participants and 708 control participants.
- In Nuuttila et al. 2026, included participants were mainly sedentary or untrained healthy adults aged 18-65 years; no included study reported trained / competitive athlete results.
- In Nuuttila et al. 2026, only one study used LT1 or VT1 to determine exercise intensity.
- Nuuttila et al. 2026 found large effects on relative VO2max, absolute VO2max, and Pmax, a moderate effect on VT1, small effects on several lipid and blood pressure outcomes, and trivial effect on glucose.

## Inference

- A reasonable exercise plan may include Zone 2, but should usually not be limited to Zone 2 unless there is a safety, recovery, adherence, or periodization reason.
- If a patient or athlete claims "Zone 2 is best for mitochondria," the precise correction is: Zone 2 can contribute, but evidence does not support superiority over above-Zone-2 intensities.
- If FAO is the target, Zone 2 may be reasonable in untrained or metabolic-risk populations, but outcome tracking should include function, CRF, glycemic markers, body composition, or measured substrate oxidation rather than claims about mitochondria alone.

## Assumption

- LT1 / GET and moderate-intensity domain can reasonably approximate the popular Zone 2 construct.
- A practical prescription should weigh time budget, safety, adherence, recovery, and outcome priority more than zone branding.

## Uncertainty

- The optimal training intensity for FAO capacity remains unclear.
- Disease-specific safety and progression rules cannot be derived from these sources alone.
- The degree to which Zone 2 improves mitochondrial respiration in different sexes, ages, medication contexts, and chronic disease groups remains uncertain.
- Storoschuk et al. 2025 is narrative, so evidence selection may be incomplete.
- Nuuttila et al. 2026 has high heterogeneity and most included studies did not use LT1 / VT1 prescription.
- Sitko et al. 2025 is an expert viewpoint with a male, cycling-focused panel, and its adaptation claims require direct testing.

## Limitations and Misreadings

- 不可把 Zone 2 寫成無效；來源反駁的是 superiority claim。
- 不可把 elite athlete 的 high-volume low-intensity training 直接外推到一般人。
- 不可把 Fatmax、LT1、GET、Talk Test 與固定心率區間視為完全相同。
- 不可把 expert viewpoint 的 practical consensus 當成 Zone 2 superiority evidence。
- 不可宣稱超過 Zone 2 會失去 mitochondrial benefit。
- 不可把 mitochondrial capacity、FAO capacity、CRF 與 cardiometabolic health 混成同一個 outcome。

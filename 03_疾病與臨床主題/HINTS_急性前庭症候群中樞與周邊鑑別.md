# HINTS 急性前庭症候群中樞與周邊鑑別

## One-Sentence Definition

HINTS 是 Head-Impulse、Nystagmus、Test of Skew 三段 bedside oculomotor exam，用於正在持續 vertigo/dizziness 的 acute vestibular syndrome，以區分可能的 brainstem/cerebellar ischemia 與 peripheral vestibular lesion。

## Definition and Boundary

本頁只處理 HINTS 在 acute continuous vertigo/dizziness 中辨識 posterior circulation stroke 的角色。

不處理所有 vertigo differential diagnosis，不處理 benign positional vertigo 操作治療，也不取代 [[Acute_Stroke_Initial_Assessment與Stabilization]] 或 [[Stroke_Evaluation_病因確認與Directed_Diagnostic_Testing]]。HINTS 不適用於 momentary position-related transient vertigo，也不適用於 TIA 病人檢查時已不 dizziness 的情境。

## Why It Matters

Posterior circulation stroke 的早期 CT 和 DWI MRI 可能陰性，且 cerebellar / brainstem ischemia 可表現為看似 peripheral vertigo。HINTS 的價值在於當病人符合 continuous acute vestibular syndrome 時，用眼動檢查抓出 central pattern；錯用 HINTS 則會讓 false reassurance 變高風險。

## Preconditions or Conditions

- 病人目前有 continuous acute vertigo or dizziness。
- 可配合注視目標與眼動檢查。
- Examiner 受過足夠訓練，能判讀 head impulse、nystagmus direction and skew deviation。
- 若有 focal neurologic sign、severe gait/truncal ataxia、new hearing loss 或高風險 posterior circulation pattern，仍需 stroke evaluation。

## Mechanism

```text
acute continuous vestibular syndrome
-> inspect vestibulo-ocular reflex with head impulse
-> inspect nystagmus direction across gaze
-> inspect vertical ocular realignment with cover-uncover skew test
-> central pattern suggests brainstem/cerebellar lesion
-> peripheral pattern suggests vestibular nerve/inner ear lesion, except AICA/internal auditory artery infarction caveat
```

## Observable Patterns

### Pattern Suggesting Brainstem or Cerebellar Lesion

Any of the following, whether present or untestable in the source, suggests a central lesion:

- Normal head impulse test on both sides。
- Direction-changing nystagmus。
- Skew deviation。

### Pattern Suggesting Peripheral Vestibular Lesion

All of the following together suggest a peripheral lesion:

- Abnormal head impulse test on one side。
- Unidirectional horizontal-torsional nystagmus that increases when gazing toward the fast phase。
- Absent skew。

### Caveats

- HINTS is not useful for brief positional vertigo or resolved TIA symptoms.
- Early CT or MRI can be normal during acute ischemic symptoms.
- AICA / internal auditory artery infarction can produce acute vestibular syndrome with a peripheral-appearing HINTS pattern.
- New ipsilateral hearing loss may be the clue that the mechanism is stroke rather than benign peripheral vestibulopathy.

## Clinical / Research Implication

- Clinical: document whether the patient truly met acute vestibular syndrome criteria before documenting HINTS.
- Clinical: a central HINTS sign should trigger posterior circulation stroke evaluation even if early MRI is negative.
- Clinical: new hearing loss with acute vestibular syndrome should not be dismissed after a peripheral-appearing HINTS result.
- Research: studies of HINTS should report examiner training, symptom timing, continuous vs transient vestibular syndrome and stroke location.

## Fact

- The source states HINTS is useful for distinguishing brainstem and cerebellar ischemia from vestibular neuritis or other peripheral causes of vertigo.
- The source states HINTS is most helpful in patients with continuous vertigo or dizziness.
- The source states HINTS is not useful for momentary position-related transient vertigo or TIA when patients are not dizzy at examination.
- The source states normal head impulse bilaterally, direction-changing nystagmus or skew deviation suggests a brainstem or cerebellar lesion.
- The source states abnormal unilateral head impulse, unidirectional horizontal-torsional nystagmus and absent skew together suggest a peripheral lesion.
- The source states HINTS is more sensitive than brain MRI within the first two days after symptom onset for acute stroke diagnosis in this context.
- The source states inner ear infarction, usually internal auditory artery / AICA branch occlusion, can mimic peripheral vestibular causes and is typically associated with new hearing loss.

## Inference

- HINTS should be used as a high-skill, context-specific neurologic exam rather than a checkbox.
- If the clinical syndrome is not continuous acute vestibular syndrome, HINTS result should not be used to exclude stroke.
- New hearing loss shifts the interpretation toward AICA/internal auditory artery infarction risk, even when eye movements look peripheral.

## Assumption

- Adult patient with acute continuous vestibular symptoms.
- Examiner can reliably identify skew and direction-changing nystagmus.
- The clinical system can escalate to stroke imaging and vascular evaluation when central signs appear.

## Uncertainty

- Real-world HINTS accuracy varies with examiner training and stroke location.
- The source does not provide a universal rule for all emergency dizziness presentations.
- HINTS does not classify stroke mechanism; it only raises or lowers suspicion for central vestibular pathology.

## Limitations and Misreadings

- 不可把 HINTS 當成所有 dizziness 的篩檢表。
- 不可在病人不 dizziness 時用 HINTS 排除 TIA。
- 不可把 peripheral-appearing HINTS 加 new hearing loss 解讀成一定 benign。
- 不可用早期 MRI negative 推翻明顯 central HINTS signs。
- 不可把 HINTS 當作不需 vascular imaging 或 stroke pathway 的理由。

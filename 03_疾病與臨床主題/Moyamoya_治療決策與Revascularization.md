# Moyamoya 治療決策與 Revascularization

## One-Sentence Definition

Moyamoya 治療決策與 revascularization 是用 symptoms、cerebral blood flow、hemodynamic perfusion reserve、progression risk、surgical risk and patient values，決定 referral for surgical revascularization 或 structured surveillance 的分流框架。

## Definition and Boundary

本頁只處理 moyamoya treatment decision、surgical referral、surveillance、revascularization techniques、perioperative complications and prognosis。Antiplatelet、anticoagulation、pregnancy、acute supportive management 見 [[Moyamoya_Antithrombotic與急性支持管理]]；病因與診斷見 [[Moyamoya_Disease與Moyamoya_Syndrome_病因分層]]、[[Moyamoya_臨床辨識與診斷影像]]。

## Why It Matters

Moyamoya 不是單純 stenosis，也不是一般 noncardioembolic stroke 的藥物預防問題。核心風險在於 progressive stenosis、impaired perfusion reserve 與 fragile collateral vessels；因此治療決策必須問：此人是否已 symptomatic 或 hemodynamically compromised？若是，藥物與追蹤不能取代 surgical referral。

## Preconditions or Conditions

- 已確認或高度懷疑 moyamoya vasculopathy。
- 已區分或正在評估 [[Moyamoya_Disease與Moyamoya_Syndrome_病因分層|MMD vs MMS]]。
- 有 clinical symptom review：TIA、ischemic stroke、cognitive decline、hemorrhage、dystonia、seizure or provoked transient weakness。
- 有 vascular imaging and, when needed, perfusion / reserve assessment。
- 有 patient-level surgical risk、comorbidity、pregnancy status、smoking status and neurologic recovery potential 評估。

## Mechanism

```text
Progressive distal intracranial stenosis
-> reduced resting cerebral blood flow or impaired perfusion reserve
-> recurrent ischemia or collateral-vessel hemorrhage
-> revascularization augments flow to ischemic territory
-> collateral pressure and recurrent event risk may decrease
-> perioperative period introduces hypoperfusion, thrombosis and hyperperfusion risks
```

## Observable Patterns

- Symptomatic moyamoya：TIA、ischemic stroke、cognitive decline or hemorrhage usually supports surgical referral if no contraindication。
- Asymptomatic but hemodynamically compromised moyamoya：decreased regional cerebral blood flow or poor perfusion reserve also supports surgical referral。
- Asymptomatic with preserved flow：medical management plus serial surveillance is reasonable。
- Imaging progression during surveillance shifts the patient toward surgical evaluation。
- Younger age at diagnosis、MMD、family history and bilateral vasculopathy may imply higher progression risk。
- Pregnancy and tobacco use may alter timing rather than eliminate treatment consideration。

## Clinical / Research Implication

- The key clinical endpoint is not "stenosis present" but "symptoms or hemodynamic compromise present"。
- Conservative management must specify follow-up plan, not just "observe"。
- Surgical comparisons should stratify direct、indirect and combined procedures by age, anatomy, baseline hemodynamics and presentation type。

## Fact

- Moyamoya is progressive and has no curative treatment in this source。
- Surgical revascularization is suggested for children and adults with symptomatic moyamoya related to ischemia, TIA, ischemic stroke, cognitive decline or hemorrhage when there is no surgical contraindication。
- Surgical revascularization is suggested for asymptomatic patients with decreased regional cerebral blood flow or inadequate hemodynamic perfusion reserve。
- Asymptomatic patients with preserved cerebral blood flow continue medical management and follow-up surveillance imaging。
- Surveillance clinical exams assess neurologic findings and symptoms attributable to vascular stenosis, including provoked transient weakness。
- Surveillance imaging is performed because stenosis progression or perfusion impairment may precede symptoms。
- Yearly imaging may be used for asymptomatic patients; severe initial stenosis may justify six-month follow-up; stable adult patients for at least three years may be followed every other year or longer。
- MRA is generally preferred for surveillance; CTA is an alternative; DSA is usually reserved for nondiagnostic noninvasive testing or presurgical planning。
- Cerebral blood flow imaging options include perfusion CT, perfusion MRI, arterial spin labeling, PET and SPECT。
- Direct revascularization connects an external carotid branch to a cortical artery and can improve perfusion rapidly。
- Indirect revascularization places vascularized tissue on the ischemic hemisphere to encourage delayed revascularization and is commonly used in children。
- Combined revascularization uses direct plus indirect approaches。
- There are no convincing data that one revascularization method is universally more effective than another。
- Meta-analyses largely using observational data found lower subsequent stroke risk with surgery than conservative management。
- Perioperative stroke is a major complication; one single-center series reported 30-day major stroke rate of 6.8 percent。
- Hyperperfusion syndrome may occur after revascularization, with reported symptomatic incidence 15 to 47 percent after bypass surgery in this source。
- A 2020 meta-analysis reported postoperative hyperperfusion incidence of 20 percent in adults and 4 percent in children。
- Natural history tends to be progressive, but rate and extent vary substantially。
- Untreated long-term cohorts reported progressive neurologic deficits and poor outcomes in 50 to 66 percent。
- One pediatric cohort reported radiographic progression within five years in 36 percent。

## Inference

- A patient with recurrent provoked TIA-like episodes should be escalated to perfusion and surgical-referral thinking rather than only medication adjustment。
- A stable asymptomatic adult with preserved perfusion can be monitored, but only if surveillance is explicit and recurrent symptoms are actively sought。
- Rehab planning after stroke or surgery should treat hyperventilation, pain, dehydration and blood pressure instability as cerebrovascular safety variables。

## Assumption

- Imaging quality is sufficient to distinguish stenosis progression from stable anatomy。
- Perfusion reserve testing is available or referral pathways exist。
- Surgical timing is decided by a cerebrovascular neurosurgery team after recovery potential and perioperative risk are assessed。

## Uncertainty

- Comparative effectiveness of direct vs indirect vs combined procedures remains limited by selection bias and center experience。
- Optimal timing after ischemic stroke or hemorrhage is individualized。
- Pediatric timing data may not generalize to adults, MMS or hemorrhagic presentations。
- Exact surveillance intervals are not fixed by high-quality evidence。

## Limitations and Misreadings

- 誤讀：「moyamoya 一診斷就一定手術。」症狀、hemodynamic compromise、progression risk and surgical candidacy 才是關鍵。
- 誤讀：「無症狀就不用追蹤。」Preserved flow patients 仍需 serial surveillance。
- 誤讀：「direct bypass 永遠最好。」來源指出沒有 convincing data 支持單一術式 universally superior。
- 誤讀：「手術只處理 ischemic moyamoya。」Hemorrhagic moyamoya 的 recurrent hemorrhage risk 也可能因 revascularization 降低，但證據仍有限。

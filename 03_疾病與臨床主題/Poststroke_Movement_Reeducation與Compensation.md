# Poststroke Movement Reeducation 與 Compensation

## One-Sentence Definition

Poststroke movement reeducation 是以 active patient-initiated movement、task-specific practice、fading assistance、alignment control 與 feedback 改善 movement control，同時只在安全或獨立性需要時策略性使用 compensation。

## Definition and Boundary

- 本頁整理 poststroke motor reeducation、assisted movement、learned nonuse 與 compensation decision。
- 本頁不宣稱任何單一 stroke rehab technique 絕對優於其他方法。
- 本頁只使用單一來源：[[../09_來源摘要/Movement_Dysfunction_in_Stroke]]。

## Why It Matters

- 太早或太全面訓練 one-sided compensation 可能讓病人更快完成任務，但也可能造成 learned nonuse、asymmetry、secondary impairment 與 fall risk。
- 反過來，完全禁止 compensation 也不合理；安全、independence、care burden 與 participation 有時需要補償策略。

## Preconditions or Conditions

- 需先判斷 recovery potential、safety risk、task urgency、environment、caregiver support、pain / edema、cardiopulmonary tolerance 與 cognitive / communication capacity。
- 任務應選擇對病人有意義且可重複練習的 functional movement。

## Mechanism

```text
Patient attempts active movement
-> therapist assists alignment, degrees of freedom, weak muscle, and joint stability
-> repeated task practice with feedback
-> assistance fades as control improves
-> movement becomes more independent and task-relevant
```

```text
Task demand exceeds impaired-side capacity
-> compensation may improve immediate safety / independence
-> excessive reliance on uninvolved side
-> learned nonuse, asymmetry, secondary impairment, and future recovery barrier
```

## Observable Patterns

- Undesirable compensation：one-sided reliance on uninvolved arm / leg、asymmetric trunk strategy、unsafe movement、avoidance of impaired extremity。
- Movement reeducation target：initiation、timing、sequencing、force、postural control、trunk-extremity coordination、task completion。
- Assisted movement target：correct alignment、limit degrees of freedom、assist weak muscle、block / stabilize joint。

## Clinical / Research Implication

- The source supports a combination approach: remediate trunk-extremity patterns, minimize secondary impairments, teach compensation when needed, and practice functional movement in tasks.
- Compensation should be documented as temporary, safety-driven, independence-driven, or permanent adaptation, not simply taught by default.
- Pain and edema should be addressed before intensive active reeducation when they limit range, tissue mobility, or participation.
- Aerobic and strength training can support motor recovery and walking capacity when medically appropriate.

## Fact

- The source states no single physical rehabilitation intervention is superior, while physical rehabilitation overall is effective.
- Limiting compensatory trunk movement may increase arm-reaching performance in the source.
- Functional recovery can continue for months to years.
- Early return of movement is prognostically important for upper limb recovery in the source.
- Cardiovascular training after stroke is described as safe and beneficial across phases in a cited Cochrane review.

## Inference

- The best treatment plan often mixes restoration-oriented practice and necessary adaptation rather than treating them as mutually exclusive.
- A movement that finishes the task but prevents impaired-side activation should be questioned if recovery-oriented practice is still feasible.

## Assumption

- The person is medically stable enough for active practice and exercise progression.
- The therapist can monitor fatigue, cardiopulmonary response, pain, fall risk, and task safety.

## Uncertainty

- Optimal timing and intensity of restoration vs compensation depend on individual impairment severity, setting, support, and goals.

## Limitations / Common Misreadings

- 「會完成 task 就代表 movement quality 沒問題」不成立。
- 「補償一定不好」不成立；安全與獨立性有時優先。
- 「復健只要選一個流派」不成立；來源支持 problem-solving combination approach。

# Oropharyngeal Dysphagia 成人臨床辨識診斷與管理

## One-Sentence Definition

Adult oropharyngeal dysphagia 是 difficulty initiating swallowing and transferring bolus from mouth to pharynx 的 alarm symptom；臨床處理需先區分 esophageal dysphagia 與 globus，再評估 etiology、aspiration severity、nutrition route 與可治療的 neuromuscular / structural / UES target。

## Definition and Boundary

### What It Is

- Adult swallowing disorder 的一種 presentation。
- 核心是口腔到咽部的 bolus transfer 和 swallow initiation 失效。
- 可伴隨 nasopharyngeal regurgitation、aspiration、pharyngeal residue sensation、coughing、choking、drooling、regurgitation、weight loss 或 aspiration pneumonia。
- 是需要 urgent evaluation 的 alarm symptom。

### What It Is Not

- 不是單純「吃慢一點」或「改稠一點」即可處理的 diet-order 問題。
- 不是 esophageal dysphagia；後者通常是 swallow initiated several seconds later 才覺得卡住。
- 不是 globus；globus 的 lump/foreign body sensation 通常 independent of swallowing，且需排除 dysphagia、odynophagia、major esophageal motor disorder、GERD 或 structural lesion。
- 不是用單一 bedside impression 就能完整定義 aspiration physiology 的診斷。

## Why It Matters

Oropharyngeal dysphagia 可代表 stroke、myasthenia gravis、Parkinson disease、multiple sclerosis、myopathy、vocal cord paralysis、Zenker's diverticulum、head/neck malignancy、xerostomia/medication effect、post-intubation injury 或 structural lesion。若只處理飲食質地，會漏掉 malignancy、neuromuscular disease、UES obstruction 或需要 enteral nutrition / procedural treatment 的病人。

## Preconditions or Conditions

- Adult patient reports difficulty initiating swallow, neck-level obstruction sensation, choking/coughing with meals, nasal regurgitation, residue sensation, recurrent pneumonia, or unexplained weight loss.
- 先判斷 acute airway / aspiration / dehydration / nutrition instability；不穩定者先處理安全與 route。
- 任何明顯 red flag，例如 weight loss、blood in mouth、otalgia、smoking/alcohol history、weak cough/hoarseness、progressive neurologic sign、recurrent aspiration pneumonia，都不應只給 diet modification。

## Mechanism

```text
Neuromuscular dysfunction / structural lesion / UES dysfunction / xerostomia / post-treatment injury
-> impaired bolus formation, oral propulsion, pharyngeal contraction, laryngeal closure, or UES opening
-> delayed or unsafe bolus transfer
-> residue, penetration, aspiration, coughing/choking, regurgitation, pneumonia, weight loss
-> etiology-specific testing and management
```

## Observable Patterns

### Symptom Sorting

- Oropharyngeal pattern：difficulty initiating swallow、neck-level obstruction sensation、coughing/choking/drooling、nasal regurgitation、residual food sensation in pharynx。
- Esophageal pattern：difficulty several seconds after initiating swallow and food-stuck sensation。
- Globus pattern：lump/foreign body sensation between meals and independent of swallowing；diagnosis requires exclusion of structural, reflux and major motor causes.

### Etiology Clues

- Older age plus smoking, alcohol use disorder, blood in mouth or weight loss：consider malignant cause。
- Referred otalgia：consider hypopharyngeal lesion or larynx/pharynx/base-of-tongue cancer。
- Dry mouth or eyes：consider salivary insufficiency, xerogenic medication, head/neck radiation, Sjogren's disease。
- Hoarseness or weak cough：consider vocal cord paresis/paralysis。
- Dysarthria, nasal speech, nasal regurgitation：consider soft palate or pharyngeal constrictor weakness。
- Regurgitation, halitosis, neck fullness, pneumonia, delayed coughing minutes to hours after meals：consider Zenker's diverticulum。
- Dysphagia late in meal：consider myasthenia gravis。
- Post-intubation context：consider post-intubation oropharyngeal dysphagia。

### Examination

- Oral cavity, head/neck and supraclavicular exam：look for lymphadenopathy, mass, facial weakness, poor dentition, structural abnormality。
- Neurologic exam：focus on cranial nerves V, VII, IX, X, XI, XII and signs of myopathy, Parkinson disease, multiple sclerosis or other neuromuscular disease。
- Voice, cough, proximal strength, ptosis, repetitive swallowing effort and gait signs can change the workup.

### Instrumental Testing

- Suspected neuromuscular disease：source performs VFSS/modified barium swallow plus esophageal manometry to analyze swallowing mechanics and pressures。
- No evidence of systemic disease：source begins with FEES and proceeds to VFSS plus manometry if no etiology is found。
- VFSS：best for dynamic analysis of oral/pharyngeal transit, bolus movement, hyoid/larynx elevation, pharyngeal contraction, UES relaxation, laryngeal penetration and aspiration severity。
- FEES：portable bedside exam of structure/function, pooled secretions, pharyngeal/laryngeal anatomy, and selected functional swallowing; can identify structural abnormality and needs specialized training。
- Manometry：quantifies UES pressure/timing and helps identify UES dysfunction or myotomy candidates, though it rarely changes management broadly.

## Clinical / Research Implication

### Clinical Use

- Initial question is not "thin or thick liquid" but "oropharyngeal vs esophageal vs globus, and what etiology or aspiration severity is present?"
- Management goals are to improve food transfer and prevent aspiration.
- Mild dysphagia after stroke, head/neck trauma, surgery, or degenerative neurologic disease may use swallow rehabilitation to facilitate oral intake, but the source grades this as low-quality evidence (Grade 2C).
- Severe dysfunction or high aspiration risk may require enteral nutrition.
- Maneuvers and diet changes should match the defect: oral mobility, delayed pharyngeal contraction, unilateral pharyngeal dysfunction, impaired tongue base retraction, laryngeal closure, UES opening or pharyngeal pooling.
- Primary cricopharyngeal dysfunction may need myotomy if selection features are favorable; dysarthria predicts poorer response in the source.
- Balloon dilation can be considered when surgery is unsuitable, but long-term improvement may be lower and repeat dilation common.
- Botulinum toxin injection should be reserved for non-surgical/non-dilation candidates in expertise centers because swallowing disruption and aspiration risk are concerns.
- NMES remains optional/uncertain; evidence is heterogeneous.

### Research Use

- VFSS parameter standardization and outcome-linked interobserver reliability remain quality targets.
- FEES and VFSS difference in aspiration severity perception needs outcome validation.
- Swallow rehabilitation studies need larger phenotype-specific trials.
- NMES should be studied by disease, stimulation protocol and co-intervention rather than as one generic modality.

## Fact

- Oropharyngeal dysphagia is difficulty initiating a swallow and transferring food from mouth to pharynx.
- It can present with coughing, choking, drooling, regurgitation, aspiration pneumonia and weight loss.
- It is an alarm symptom requiring urgent evaluation.
- VFSS, FEES and manometry have distinct roles.
- Management aims to improve food transfer and prevent aspiration.
- Swallow rehabilitation is suggested for mild oropharyngeal dysphagia after selected neurologic or head/neck causes, but evidence is limited.
- Enteral nutrition is suggested when severe dysfunction creates high aspiration risk or oral nutrition is not tolerated.
- Cricopharyngeal myotomy has potential benefit in selected primary cricopharyngeal dysfunction but has complications and nonrandomized evidence limitations.

## Inference

- A safe plan requires cause, severity and route decisions, not only texture modification.
- FEES should be preferred when bedside portability, secretion burden or structural visualization is central; VFSS should be preferred when dynamic bolus flow and timing are central.
- Manometry should be targeted to UES pressure/timing questions rather than ordered reflexively for every dysphagia case.

## Assumption

- The patient is an adult and the symptom is suspected oropharyngeal rather than purely esophageal.
- Local access exists for SLP, ENT/GI, VFSS, FEES, manometry and nutrition support.
- Acute airway compromise, severe aspiration pneumonia or inability to maintain hydration/nutrition is handled first.

## Uncertainty

- Evidence for many swallow rehabilitation techniques remains limited.
- FEES/VFSS difference in aspiration-risk rating has uncertain clinical significance.
- Cricopharyngeal myotomy and dilation lack strong randomized comparative evidence.
- NMES role remains unclear due to heterogeneous trials.

## Limitations and Misreadings

- Do not diagnose globus if the patient truly has dysphagia or odynophagia.
- Do not infer esophageal dysphagia is excluded only because the patient localizes symptoms to the neck; distal esophageal disease can be felt near the suprasternal notch.
- Do not prescribe thickened liquids as a universal intervention; bolus viscosity should match physiology and goals.
- Do not treat a normal bedside impression as proof against silent aspiration.
- Do not treat botulinum toxin injection into cricopharyngeus as routine low-risk therapy.

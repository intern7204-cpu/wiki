# Cervicocephalic Artery Dissection 臨床辨識

## One-Sentence Definition

Cervicocephalic artery dissection 的臨床辨識，是在 acute/subacute head or neck pain、local compressive symptoms、TIA/stroke、SAH pattern、minor trigger and vascular predisposition 之間建立 suspicion，並決定是否需要 urgent head/neck vascular imaging。

## Definition and Boundary

本頁只處理 cerebral / cervical artery dissection 的臨床辨識與何時懷疑。

不處理完整 treatment、antithrombotic selection、recanalization follow-up 或 prognosis；這些需要 dedicated treatment source。影像確認流程見 [[Cervicocephalic_Artery_Dissection_影像確認與Pitfalls]]；一般 stroke mechanism confirmation 見 [[Stroke_Evaluation_病因確認與Directed_Diagnostic_Testing]]。

## Why It Matters

Dissection 是 young stroke 的重要原因，但可發生於任何年齡。臨床錯誤通常不是不知道 dissection 會中風，而是把 "spontaneous" 誤解成沒有 trigger、把 headache 當 migraine / cluster headache、把 isolated Horner syndrome 當眼科問題，或在 older adult 因沒有 neck pain / trauma 就停止考慮。

## Preconditions or Conditions

- Adult acute/subacute headache、neck pain、facial pain、TIA/stroke symptom 或 suspected SAH。
- 特別注意 recent minor trauma、sports / physical activity、intense coughing/sneezing、peripartum Valsalva、personal/family vascular or connective tissue disorder、migraine history。
- 可以安排 urgent MRI/MRA 或 CT/CTA of head and neck。

## Mechanism

```text
wall integrity compromise
-> blood enters arterial wall
-> intramural hematoma and false lumen
```

### Ischemic Pathway

```text
subintimal dissection
-> luminal stenosis or occlusion
-> thromboembolism more than hypoperfusion
-> TIA / ischemic stroke in carotid or vertebrobasilar territory
```

### Local Compressive Pathway

```text
subadventitial dissection or pseudoaneurysm
-> vessel dilatation and adjacent nerve / vasa nervorum compression
-> head/neck/facial pain, partial Horner syndrome, cranial neuropathy, cervical root symptoms, pulsatile tinnitus
```

### SAH Pathway

```text
intracranial dissection
-> thin adventitial support and vessel rupture risk
-> subarachnoid hemorrhage in a minority
```

## Observable Patterns

### High-Yield Suspicion Triggers

- Acute or subacute head / neck / facial pain with TIA or stroke symptoms.
- Neck or occipital pain with posterior circulation signs.
- Ipsilateral temple, eye, cheek or tooth pain with carotid territory symptoms.
- Acute partial Horner syndrome: ptosis + miosis without anhidrosis.
- Cranial neuropathy, especially CN XII, or multiple lower cranial neuropathies.
- Pulsatile tinnitus.
- Tooth pain without dental cause.
- Recent minor trauma, sudden neck movement, sports, vigorous exercise, cervical manipulation, coughing/sneezing or peripartum Valsalva.
- Personal or family history suggesting connective tissue / vascular disorder.

### Carotid Artery Dissection Pattern

- Ipsilateral head / facial pain can localize to temple, eye, cheek or teeth.
- Internal carotid artery dissection may produce partial Horner syndrome because sweat fibers leave the oculosympathetic pathway and travel with the external carotid artery.
- Ischemia may appear as anterior circulation stroke syndrome, transient monocular blindness, retinal artery occlusion or ischemic optic neuropathy.
- Cranial neuropathy may occur without ischemia.

### Vertebral Artery Dissection Pattern

- Neck pain may be more frequent with vertebral artery dissection than carotid dissection.
- Occipital headache plus posterior circulation symptoms is a key clinical pattern.
- It may lead to lateral medullary infarction, other posterior circulation stroke syndromes or cervical spinal cord ischemia.
- Dizziness/vertigo is common in vertebral artery dissection reports, but the source states ischemic causality is uncertain in one systematic review; dizziness alone is not enough.

### Intracranial Dissection / SAH Pattern

- Intracranial artery dissection can cause SAH.
- Thunderclap headache can occur in a minority of cervicocephalic dissections.
- SAH from dissection belongs in the differential diagnosis of nontraumatic SAH and requires vascular imaging to distinguish from saccular aneurysm or other vascular lesions.

## Clinical / Research Implication

- Acute stroke notes should record dissection clues explicitly: pain timing, pain side/location, trigger, Horner syndrome, cranial neuropathy, tinnitus, vascular territory, and imaging plan.
- Rehab or ward deterioration with new neck/occipital pain plus posterior circulation findings should be treated as possible recurrent vascular event until evaluated.
- Research cohorts should not combine "headache-only", "local symptoms-only", "ischemic", and "SAH" dissection phenotypes without stratification.

## Fact

- The source states dissection occurs when arterial wall integrity is compromised and blood collects between wall layers as an intramural hematoma.
- The source states arterial dissections are a common cause of stroke in the young but may occur at any age.
- The source states minor trauma or mechanical events are triggers in up to 40 percent of cervical artery dissection cases.
- The source states head and/or neck pain is the most frequent initial symptom, found in 57 to 90 percent of cases.
- The source states Horner syndrome occurs in approximately 25 percent of cases and is usually partial in internal carotid artery dissection.
- The source states carotid dissection may cause compressive cranial neuropathies in up to 12 percent of cases.
- The source states pulsatile tinnitus was present in 8 percent in the CADISP study.
- The source states cerebral ischemia was noted in 67 percent in one population-based report of 48 internal carotid / vertebral dissection patients.
- The source states intracranial artery dissection may result in SAH.
- The source states older patients with cervical artery dissection may be less likely to present with pain, preceding trauma or mechanical trigger.

## Inference

- Dissection suspicion is a pattern-recognition problem, not a single-symptom rule.
- "No major trauma" and "older age" are weak reasons to dismiss dissection when vascular symptoms remain unexplained.
- Vertebral artery dissection should be considered when posterior circulation symptoms coexist with new neck/occipital pain, but isolated dizziness needs broader differential diagnosis.

## Assumption

- The patient is in an acute or subacute diagnostic setting where vascular imaging can change management.
- The clinician can obtain enough history to reconstruct pain timing, trigger and neurologic symptom evolution.

## Uncertainty

- Many associated conditions are observational associations, not proven causal relationships.
- Cervical manipulation may trigger dissection, but causality and absolute incidence are uncertain.
- The source does not determine optimal antithrombotic treatment.

## Limitations and Misreadings

- 不可把 dissection 簡化成 young patient disease；any age 可發生。
- 不可因為沒有 trauma 就排除 dissection。
- 不可把 headache / neck pain alone 當成 dissection diagnosis；需 [[Cervicocephalic_Artery_Dissection_影像確認與Pitfalls]]。
- 不可把 ultrasound-negative 當成排除診斷。
- 不可把 dizziness/vertigo alone 寫成 vertebral artery dissection。
- 不可用本頁替代 dissection treatment / prognosis 專題。

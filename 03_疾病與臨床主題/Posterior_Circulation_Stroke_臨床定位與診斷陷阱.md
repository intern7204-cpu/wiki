# Posterior Circulation Stroke 臨床定位與診斷陷阱

## One-Sentence Definition

Posterior circulation stroke 是 vertebrobasilar territory 的 ischemic syndrome；辨識重點是把 vascular anatomy、brainstem/cerebellar/PCA topographic signs、acute timing and posterior fossa imaging limitations 整合，而不是把 dizziness 或 early negative MRI 當成單一判斷依據。

## Definition and Boundary

本頁處理 adult posterior circulation ischemia 的臨床定位與診斷陷阱。

不處理完整 reperfusion eligibility、antithrombotic selection、dissection treatment、lacunar stroke 全貌或 long-term secondary prevention。急性 first-hours stabilization 見 [[Acute_Stroke_Initial_Assessment與Stabilization]]；病因確認與檢查路徑見 [[Stroke_Evaluation_病因確認與Directed_Diagnostic_Testing]]；acute vestibular syndrome 的 HINTS 邊界見 [[HINTS_急性前庭症候群中樞與周邊鑑別]]。

## Why It Matters

Posterior circulation stroke 容易被誤判成 peripheral vestibulopathy、普通暈眩、舊中風波動、delirium 或非特異性 weakness。錯誤點通常不是不知道 vertebrobasilar anatomy，而是低估 posterior fossa lesion 的 imaging false negative、忽略 ocular motor / bulbar / gait signs，或把 NIHSS 低分誤讀成低風險。

## Preconditions or Conditions

- Rapid or sudden neurologic symptoms。
- 需要 posterior circulation localization：brainstem、cerebellum、thalamus、occipital lobe、medial/inferior temporal lobe。
- 可取得 focused cranial nerve、ocular motor、bulbar、cerebellar、sensory and gait examination。
- 仍須 brain imaging and vascular imaging confirmation；臨床定位不能取代影像。

## Mechanism

```text
vertebral / basilar / PCA arterial lesion
-> embolism, local thrombosis, dissection, low-flow state, anatomic variant or dolichoectasia-related branch compromise
-> posterior fossa / thalamic / occipital / temporal ischemia
-> ocular motor, vestibulocerebellar, bulbar, motor, sensory, visual, alertness/behavior/memory pattern
-> early CT / DWI MRI may under-detect small posterior circulation infarct
-> diagnosis depends on repeated anatomy-driven clinical and vascular evaluation
```

### Territory-to-Syndrome Logic

| Territory / vessel | High-yield pattern |
|---|---|
| ECVA origin disease | artery-to-artery embolism more often than low flow; dizziness is usually not isolated |
| ECVA / vertebral artery dissection | neck/occipital pain plus lateral medullary, cerebellar, posterior circulation TIA/stroke or cervical spinal cord ischemia pattern |
| ICVA disease | lateral/medial/hemimedullary infarction, PICA cerebellar infarction, basilar propagation or distal embolization |
| Basilar artery | pontine ischemia with motor, bulbar and oculomotor signs; bilateral but asymmetric signs are common |
| Top of basilar | altered alertness, behavior, memory, vertical gaze/convergence/pupillary abnormalities, thalamic and PCA territory signs |
| PCA territory | hemianopia, hemisensory loss, thalamic symptoms, alexia / agnosia / spatial deficits depending on side |

## Observable Patterns

### When to Suspect Posterior Circulation Ischemia

- Rapid or sudden neurologic symptoms with altered alertness、behavior or memory。
- Visual loss、hemianopia、visual neglect or higher-order visual syndrome。
- Eye movement abnormality、vertical gaze problem、skew deviation、ocular torsion、pupillary abnormality or INO。
- Bulbar weakness: dysphagia、dysarthria、dysphonia、hoarseness or aspiration risk。
- Somatosensory loss, especially crossed face-body pain/temperature pattern or PCA territory hemisensory loss。
- Dizziness/vertigo with gait ataxia、limb ataxia、nystagmus pattern or other neurologic signs。
- Occipital/neck pain with posterior circulation symptoms raises concern for vertebral artery dissection in the right context。
- [[Cervicocephalic_Artery_Dissection_臨床辨識]] adds that vertebral artery dissection most often involves the V3 segment near the foramen magnum, less often V2, and that dizziness/vertigo is common in reports but not by itself specific for ischemia.

### High-Risk Syndrome Clusters

- Lateral medullary infarction: vestibulocerebellar signs, ipsilateral facial pain/temperature loss, contralateral body pain/temperature loss, dysphagia/hoarseness, Horner syndrome, possible respiratory/autonomic dysfunction。
- Large PICA cerebellar infarction: initial vertigo/gait ataxia can evolve after the first day into worsening headache/vomiting, decreased consciousness and brainstem compression signs。
- AICA infarction: vertigo, unsteady gait, dysarthria, ipsilateral LMN facial weakness/numbness, tinnitus or early ipsilateral hearing loss。
- Basilar occlusive disease: motor weakness with bilateral/asymmetric pyramidal signs, bulbar weakness with aspiration risk, horizontal gaze palsy/INO/one-and-a-half syndrome, small pupils with preserved light response, locked-in syndrome in severe cases。
- Top of basilar: altered alertness/behavior/memory, vertical gaze palsy, convergence abnormality, pupillary dysfunction, peduncular hallucinosis, amnesia。
- PCA infarction: hemianopia is common; hemisensory loss plus hemianopia without paralysis strongly supports PCA territory involvement in this source.

## Clinical / Research Implication

- Acute care: low NIHSS or nonspecific dizziness cannot de-escalate posterior circulation suspicion when ocular motor, gait, bulbar or altered-alertness signs exist。
- Rehabilitation: new dysphagia, aspiration, unexplained gait collapse, diplopia, acute visual field complaint, altered alertness or severe occipital headache needs recurrent stroke / posterior fossa evaluation before attributing symptoms to deconditioning。
- Documentation: write the suspected vascular territory and clinical evidence, e.g. "posterior circulation ischemia suspected due to acute vertigo + direction-changing nystagmus + truncal ataxia."
- Research: posterior circulation studies should stratify by territory, mechanism, timing from onset, DWI false negative risk and bedside examiner expertise.

## Fact

- The source states posterior circulation structures account for about 20 percent of ischemic brain events.
- The source states large posterior circulation arteries include innominate and subclavian arteries, vertebral arteries, intracranial vertebral arteries, basilar artery and PCAs.
- The source states subclavian/innominate disease can cause arm ischemia and TIAs but seldom strokes.
- The source states most proximal vertebral artery occlusive lesions are atherosclerotic.
- The source states artery-to-artery embolism is more frequent than hemodynamic insufficiency in proximal ECVA stenosis.
- The source states lateral medullary infarction is the most common and important syndrome related to intracranial vertebral artery occlusion.
- The source states basilar artery occlusive disease most often presents as pontine ischemia.
- The source states most PCA territory infarcts are due to embolism from the heart, aorta or vertebral arteries.
- The source states CT is less sensitive than DWI MRI for acute ischemic stroke, especially posterior fossa stroke.
- The source states even DWI MRI has suboptimal sensitivity for small acute posterior circulation infarcts.
- The dissection source states vertebral artery dissection most often occurs near the foramen magnum at the skull base (V3) or less often in the C6 to C2 transverse processes (V2).
- The dissection source states vertebral artery dissection may lead to lateral medullary infarction, other posterior circulation stroke syndromes, or cervical spinal cord ischemia.
- The dissection source states dizziness/vertigo was the most common symptom in a systematic review of vertebral artery dissection reports, although ischemic causality was uncertain.

## Inference

- Posterior circulation diagnosis is an anatomy-driven probability model; early imaging and nonspecific symptoms must be interpreted against the bedside pattern.
- A negative early DWI should not stop evaluation when posterior circulation signs remain convincing.
- Dizziness/vertigo becomes stroke-relevant when it is continuous, acute, associated with central ocular motor signs, severe gait/truncal ataxia, new hearing loss, bulbar symptoms or other focal signs.
- Neck/occipital pain increases the need to look for vertebral artery dissection when posterior circulation localization is otherwise plausible.

## Assumption

- Adult acute/subacute ischemic stroke setting.
- Examiner can obtain ocular motor, cranial nerve, cerebellar and gait findings.
- Vascular imaging can evaluate vertebral, basilar and PCA territories when suspicion remains.

## Uncertainty

- Vertebral artery hypoplasia may be associated with posterior circulation ischemic stroke, but the source describes this as not established.
- Anatomic variants can change expected territory attribution.
- HINTS performance depends on examiner training and is syndrome-specific, not a universal dizziness test.
- Pediatric stroke, pregnancy-related stroke and rare vasculopathies require separate sources.

## Limitations and Misreadings

- 不可把 isolated dizziness 直接寫成 vertebrobasilar TIA；來源指出 dizziness during TIAs is seldom the only neurologic symptom。
- 不可用 early CT / MRI negative 排除 posterior circulation stroke。
- 不可把 HINTS 用在 momentary positional vertigo 或已無 dizziness 的 TIA。
- 不可把 NIHSS 低分等同 posterior circulation low risk。
- 不可把 subclavian steal physiology 視為常見 stroke mechanism。
- 不可把 proximal ECVA stenosis 的機制簡化成 low flow；來源較支持 artery-to-artery embolism 更常見。
- 不可把 dizziness/vertigo alone 視為 vertebral artery dissection；需 pain/trigger/localizing signs and [[Cervicocephalic_Artery_Dissection_影像確認與Pitfalls]]。

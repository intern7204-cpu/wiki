# Stroke Epidemiology 全球與美國分布

## One-Sentence Definition

Stroke epidemiology 是描述 stroke incidence、prevalence、mortality、disability burden、subtype distribution and population disparities 如何隨地區、收入層級、sex、race / ethnicity and time period 改變的 population-level framework。

## Definition and Boundary

本頁只處理 population distribution，不處理個別病人的 etiologic diagnosis、acute treatment 或 secondary prevention drug choice。個別病因分類見 [[Stroke_Etiologic_Classification]]；臨床評估流程見 [[Stroke_Evaluation_病因確認與Directed_Diagnostic_Testing]]。

Epidemiology 只能改變 pretest probability。它不能替代 imaging、vascular evaluation、cardiac monitoring or echo。

## Why It Matters

Stroke 不是單一負擔。High-income countries、low- and middle-income countries、美國東南部、不同 sex 與 race / ethnicity groups 的 subtype distribution、mortality and disability burden 不同。若把一個地區的比例直接套到所有病人，會誤判 hemorrhagic burden、復發風險與 prevention priority。

## Preconditions or Conditions

- 解讀的是 population-level data，而不是個別病例的病因證明。
- 必須注意資料來源、年份、地區、case definition and surveillance method。
- 需要把 subtype proportion、incidence、prevalence、mortality and disability 分開看。

## Mechanism

```text
population risk factors + health system context + surveillance method
-> observed subtype distribution
-> incidence / mortality / disability pattern
-> public health and prevention priority
-> individual care uses these data only as pretest probability
```

## Observable Patterns

### Global Versus United States

- Source states stroke is among the leading global causes of mortality and disability.
- Adult lifetime stroke risk from age 25 years and older is approximately 25 percent in the cited global estimate.
- The source identifies East Asia, Central Europe and Eastern Europe as high-risk regions.
- United States stroke subtype distribution in the source is approximately ischemic stroke 87 percent、ICH 10 percent、SAH 3 percent.
- Global subtype distribution is internally inconsistent in this source: the epidemiology section and summary provide different proportions. Treat this as a source-drift / source-internal uncertainty, not as a stable single number.

### Secular and Regional Pattern

- The source states stroke incidence is decreasing in high-income countries including the United States, but increasing in low-income countries.
- Overall stroke-related mortality rate is decreasing across high- and low-income countries, while absolute global burden remains high because stroke survivors, deaths, and disability burden continue to accumulate.
- In the United States, annual new or recurrent stroke count is approximately 795,000, including approximately 610,000 first-ever and 185,000 recurrent strokes.
- The southeastern United States has higher incidence, prevalence and mortality than the rest of the country in this source.

### Sex and Race / Ethnicity

- Lifetime stroke risk is higher for females than males in this source.
- Black and Hispanic Americans have higher stroke risk than White Americans in cited observations.
- Northern Manhattan Study data in the source report higher relative rates of intracranial atherosclerosis, extracranial atherosclerosis, lacunes and cardioembolism among Black and Hispanic Americans compared with White Americans.
- Greater Cincinnati / Northern Kentucky data in the source report small vessel and undetermined strokes nearly twice as common, and large vessel stroke 40 percent more common, among Black compared with White patients; cardioembolic stroke incidence was not significantly different in that observation.

## Clinical / Research Implication

- Clinical: use epidemiology to frame prior probability, not to assign etiology.
- Rehabilitation: population burden explains why recurrent stroke prevention and disability management are core rehabilitation tasks rather than optional add-ons.
- Research: studies must specify region, calendar period, stroke definition, subtype classification system and sampling method before comparing rates.
- Public health: disparities should trigger examination of vascular risk factors, access, environment and care quality; they should not be reduced to individual biological determinism.

## Fact

- The source states that the United States subtype proportions are approximately ischemia 87 percent, ICH 10 percent and SAH 3 percent.
- The source states the United States has approximately 795,000 new or recurrent strokes annually.
- The source states approximately 610,000 of those are first-ever strokes and 185,000 are recurrent strokes.
- The source states adult lifetime stroke risk from age 25 years and older is approximately 25 percent.
- The source states stroke incidence is decreasing in high-income countries including the United States and increasing in low-income countries.
- The source states stroke-related mortality rates are decreasing, while absolute global burden remains high and increasing.
- The source states lifetime stroke risk is higher for females than males.
- The source reports higher stroke risk among Black and Hispanic Americans compared with White Americans in cited United States studies.

## Inference

- A Taiwan-specific or East Asia-specific epidemiology page would require separate local or regional sources.
- United States data can inform general framing but should not define local patient probabilities without local data.
- Subtype disparity may reflect risk-factor distribution, social determinants, health access and classification practices; it should not be overread as direct causal biology.

## Assumption

- The source is summarizing adult population data.
- Surveillance estimates are comparable only after accounting for method and definition.

## Uncertainty

- Global subtype proportions differ within the same source between the main epidemiology text and the summary.
- Exact rates will change over time and by study method.
- The source does not provide Taiwan-specific rates.

## Limitations and Misreadings

- 不可把 epidemiologic proportion 當作個別病人的 diagnosis。
- 不可把 United States ischemic predominance 套用到所有低收入或中等收入國家。
- 不可把 race / ethnicity 差異寫成 deterministic biological rule。
- 不可用 older incidence trend 取代目前 local surveillance data。

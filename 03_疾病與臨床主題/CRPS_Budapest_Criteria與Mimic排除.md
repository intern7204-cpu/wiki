# CRPS Budapest Criteria 與 Mimic 排除

## One-Sentence Definition

CRPS Budapest criteria 是 adult CRPS 的 clinical diagnostic gate：continuing disproportionate regional limb pain，加上 sensory、vasomotor、sudomotor/edema、motor/trophic 四域的 symptom/sign 門檻，且沒有其他 diagnosis 更能解釋。

## Definition and Boundary

### What It Is

- Adult CRPS 的 clinical criteria framework。
- 適用於 suspected limb CRPS 的 history and physical examination。
- 核心不是影像或 block response，而是 regional disproportionate pain + four-domain symptom/sign pattern + mimic exclusion。

### What It Is Not

- 不是 treatment algorithm。
- 不是 pediatric CRPS criteria page。
- 不是 bone scan、MRI、CT、thermography 或 sympathetic block 的檢查適應症頁。
- 不是把所有 post-traumatic pain 或 neuropathic pain 都歸為 CRPS。

## Why It Matters

CRPS 容易被兩種錯誤處理：一是把 regional pain、edema、skin color change 直接貼上 CRPS 而漏掉 infection、DVT、compartment syndrome 或 vascular disease；二是因影像陰性、EMG/NCS 不支持或 sympathetic block 沒有效就否定臨床上符合 criteria 的 CRPS。Budapest criteria 的價值是把診斷維持在可檢查、可反駁、可排除 mimic 的臨床框架。

## Preconditions or Conditions

- Adult patient with regional limb pain, commonly after fracture、soft tissue injury、sprain、crush injury、surgery or nerve injury。
- Symptoms usually emerge within four to six weeks after inciting event, but up to 10 percent have no identifiable trigger in this source。
- Pain and associated findings are not fully explained by the original injury。
- Findings are distal-predominant or extend beyond the trauma region or a single nerve/root territory。

## Mechanism

```text
Inciting limb trauma / surgery / nerve injury
-> peripheral inflammation and neurogenic inflammation
-> catecholamine hypersensitivity and autonomic dysregulation
-> central sensitization and cortical sensorimotor reorganization
-> regional pain + sensory, vasomotor, sudomotor/edema, motor/trophic findings
```

This is a proposed multifactorial mechanism. The source explicitly states that CRPS pathogenesis remains unknown.

## Observable Patterns

### Core Clinical Pattern

- Continuing spontaneous or evoked regional pain.
- Pain is disproportionate in time or degree to the usual course of trauma or lesion.
- Pain is not limited to a specific nerve territory or dermatome.
- Usually distal-predominant abnormal sensory, motor, sudomotor, vasomotor or trophic findings.
- Pain is often burning, stinging or tearing, continuous or undulating, and can be worsened by movement, contact, temperature change or stress.

### Budapest Criteria

Required entry:

- Continuing pain disproportionate to any inciting event.

Symptoms reported by patient:

- At least one symptom in three of four categories for clinical diagnosis.
- Research criteria are stricter: at least one symptom in all four categories.

Signs displayed at evaluation:

- At least one sign in two of four categories for clinical diagnosis.
- Research criteria are stricter: at least one sign in three of four categories.

Four categories:

- Sensory：hyperesthesia, allodynia, hyperalgesia or related sensory abnormality.
- Vasomotor：temperature asymmetry, skin color change, or skin color asymmetry.
- Sudomotor/edema：edema, sweating change, or sweating asymmetry.
- Motor/trophic：decreased range of motion, weakness, tremor, dystonia, hair/nail/skin trophic change.

Final requirement:

- No other diagnosis better explains the signs and symptoms.

### Testing Boundary

- No gold-standard test confirms CRPS.
- Bone scintigraphy can support diagnosis in selected early/atypical cases, but negative scan does not rule it out.
- Plain radiographs may show patchy osteoporosis, but sensitivity is low.
- Autonomic testing and serial temperature measurement can support selected cases but are not routine universal confirmation.
- MRI may help exclude mimics, but does not confirm CRPS.
- CT is not suggested as a diagnostic test.
- Sympathetic block response is not diagnostic; it may suggest sympathetically maintained pain.

### Mimic Checklist

- Infection of skin, muscle, joint or bone.
- Compartment syndrome.
- Peripheral vascular disease.
- Deep vein thrombosis.
- Peripheral neuropathy, including diabetic polyneuropathy.
- Vascular thoracic outlet syndrome.
- Rheumatoid arthritis.
- Raynaud phenomenon.
- Erythromelalgia.
- Functional neurological symptom disorder.
- Factitious disorder.

## Clinical / Research Implication

### Clinical Use

- Document symptoms and signs separately; reported symptoms alone are not enough.
- Compare affected and unaffected side for temperature, color, edema, sweating, movement and trophic changes.
- Do not use "regional pain after trauma" as a shortcut diagnosis.
- Use labs, Doppler, radiographs, MRI or specialty referral when mimic probability is clinically meaningful.
- If the presentation is atypical, testing is mainly for support and exclusion, not replacement of clinical criteria.

### Research Use

- Budapest criteria improve specificity compared with older criteria but remain imperfect; a cohort cited by the source found sensitivity 82 percent and specificity 68 percent.
- Warm/cold and central/peripheral/mixed phenotypes are hypothesis-generating categories, not settled routine clinical buckets.

## Fact

- CRPS type I lacks evidence of peripheral nerve injury and represents approximately 90 percent of clinical presentations.
- CRPS type II has peripheral nerve injury, but type I/type II clinical utility is uncertain.
- Pathogenesis is unknown.
- Proposed mechanisms include peripheral inflammation/autoimmunity, neurogenic inflammation, catecholamine hypersensitivity, central sensitization, cortical reorganization, glial activation and genetic associations.
- CRPS is more common in females than males, with reported female-to-male ratio 2:1 to 4:1.
- Most common inciting events are fractures, soft tissue injuries, sprains and surgery.
- The older three-stage model is largely abandoned by most experts due to limited evidence.

## Inference

- The highest-yield CRPS diagnostic habit is not ordering a confirmatory test, but forcing a structured symptom/sign table and a differential diagnosis table.
- A normal MRI or negative bone scan should lower confidence only in context; it should not automatically erase a clinically coherent Budapest-positive presentation.
- Positive sympathetic block should shift language toward sympathetically maintained pain, not "CRPS confirmed."

## Assumption

- The patient is an adult and the clinical question is diagnosis, not treatment choice.
- Examiner can assess pinprick hyperalgesia, light-touch allodynia, temperature/color asymmetry, edema/sweating, range of motion, motor dysfunction and trophic change.

## Uncertainty

- CRPS mechanism remains unresolved.
- Type I/type II distinction may not map cleanly to treatment response.
- Adjunctive testing has limited and context-dependent diagnostic value.
- Budapest specificity is imperfect, so mimic exclusion remains mandatory.

## Limitations and Misreadings

- Do not diagnose CRPS without considering infection, DVT, compartment syndrome, vascular disease and neuropathy.
- Do not require sympathetic block response for diagnosis.
- Do not call CRPS psychogenic because psychosocial/personality predisposition evidence is controversial and not confirmed by later studies in this source.
- Do not use the old stage 1/2/3 model as a required progression.
- Do not use this page to choose steroid, bisphosphonate, ketamine, sympathetic block or SCS treatment; that requires the treatment/prognosis source.
